# How solar panels work

## The photovoltaic effect
A solar panel is made of **photovoltaic (PV) cells**, usually **silicon**. When sunlight hits a cell, its energy is absorbed and knocks electrons loose; the cell's layered structure directs this flow in one direction, creating a **direct current (DC)**, collected by thin metal contacts. Silicon is used because it's abundant, relatively cheap and conducts well once refined. Commercial panels typically convert **around 18–22%** of the sunlight hitting them into usable electricity.

## Panel types
- **Monocrystalline** — grown from a single pure silicon crystal; uniform black look, highest typical efficiency (~17–22%), slightly higher price.
- **Polycrystalline** — multiple crystal fragments cooled together; blue marbled look, lower efficiency (~15–17%), lower cost.
- **Thin-film** — non-crystalline layers; cheaper and flexible but lower efficiency (~10–13%) and needs more roof area, so rarely used on homes.

## Inverters
Panels produce **DC**, but homes and the grid run on **alternating current (AC)**, so a **solar inverter converts DC to usable AC**.
- **String inverter:** one central inverter for a whole series ("string") of panels — cheaper and simpler, but if one panel is shaded or the inverter fails, the whole string's output can be affected.
- **Microinverters:** a small inverter on each panel, converting DC to AC at the panel — each panel performs independently, so shading/faults on one don't drag down the others, and the system is easier to expand, at higher upfront cost.
- A **hybrid inverter** manages both panels and a battery in one device.
