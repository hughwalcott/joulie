# Hot water heat pumps for your home

Water heating is about a third of a typical household's energy use, so an efficient hot water system makes a big difference. A hot water heat pump extracts heat from the surrounding air and transfers it into the water, rather than heating the water directly with an element.

## Running cost and savings

- Uses **around 60–75% less electricity** than a conventional electric resistance hot water cylinder (about **one third of the energy**).
- **Around 60% cheaper to run** than an electric resistance cylinder or **piped natural gas** water heating, and **around 75% cheaper** than bottled **LPG**.
- Saves the average household **about $284 a year** (EECA estimate; larger if you have solar or higher electricity prices).

## Upfront cost and life

- Typical **purchase plus installation: around $7,000**.
- Working life **about 15 years**.
- Despite the higher upfront cost than a standard cylinder, EECA rates it the **cheapest option once running costs are included**.

## Emissions

Because New Zealand's electricity is highly renewable, a hot water heat pump can save **around 7,000 kg of emissions over 15 years** versus gas or LPG water heating — more again if paired with rooftop solar.

## ⚠ Currency note
EECA cost and savings estimates are updated periodically and depend on assumptions (household size, electricity price). Figures above match EECA's page as at retrieval; confirm before quoting as current.
