# Heat pumps for home heating

## How a heat pump warms a room
A heat pump **doesn't generate heat — it moves it**. It pumps a refrigerant between an outdoor and an indoor unit, using the temperature difference to make the refrigerant **evaporate** (absorbing heat from outdoor air, even when cold) or **condense** (releasing that heat indoors). Because it moves existing heat rather than converting electricity directly into heat, it delivers several times more heat energy than the electricity it uses.

## COP and efficiency
**Coefficient of performance (COP)** is the ratio of useful heat delivered to electrical energy consumed. A plain electric heater is about COP 1 (one unit of heat per unit of electricity); a heat pump can have a COP **greater than one** — e.g. **COP 3 means three units of heat per one unit of electricity**, the other two drawn from the outside air. EECA states heat pumps can provide **about 3–5 times** the heating for the same input energy as a gas heater achieves from its gas. Higher COP = better efficiency and lower running cost.

## Types
- **Single-split:** one outdoor unit to one indoor unit — cheapest, heats one room.
- **Multi-split:** one outdoor unit to several indoor units in different rooms.
- **Ducted:** one outdoor unit to a central indoor unit distributing air through ductwork for whole-house control; higher upfront cost.

## Cold weather and defrost
Modern heat pumps keep extracting heat in cold conditions, though efficiency falls as temperature drops. In cold, damp weather frost can build on the outdoor coil, so the unit runs a short automatic **defrost cycle** (typically **5–15 minutes**), briefly reversing to send warm refrigerant through the outdoor coil to melt the frost, then resuming heating. This is normal, not a fault. Cold-climate ("hyper-heating") models maintain output to much lower temperatures.

## Heating and cooling
Because it moves heat, **reversing the refrigerant cycle** lets the same unit heat (bring heat in) or cool (move heat out) — which is why most residential heat pumps also work as air conditioners in summer.
