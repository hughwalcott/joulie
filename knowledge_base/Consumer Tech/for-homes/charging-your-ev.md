# Charging your EV

## Home charging options
EECA describes three main options:
- **Portable 3-pin cable** (usually supplied with the car) — the **slowest**, not intended as a household's primary method.
- **Standard wall-mounted AC charger** — professionally installed, faster and safer for regular use.
- **Smart wall-mounted AC charger** — EECA's recommended option: **fastest, safest and most convenient**, adding app scheduling and control (see the smart EV charger record).

## AC vs DC charging
- **AC charging** (home and most destination chargers): the EV's **onboard charger** converts AC to DC for the battery — this onboard converter limits how fast AC charging can go.
- **DC fast charging** (highway/public sites): converts AC to DC **before** it reaches the car, feeding DC straight to the battery and bypassing the slower onboard charger — much faster.

## How much range DC adds
EECA examples: a **25 kW DC charger adds ~50 km in under 30 minutes**; a **50 kW DC charger ~50 km in under 15 minutes**; higher-power DC chargers **~100 km in as little as 13–16 minutes** — all far faster than AC (a full home charge typically takes several hours).

## Connectors used in New Zealand
- **AC:** **Type 2 (Mennekes)** is standard on new EVs; older Japanese imports (e.g. early Leafs) often use **Type 1 (J1772)**.
- **DC:** **CCS2** is the dominant standard on new vehicles; **CHAdeMO** (older, common on early imports) is still supported at many chargers but declining.

## Two-way charging (V2G / V2H)
Bidirectional charging lets power flow both ways — into the battery and back out to a home (V2H), building (V2B) or grid (V2G), letting an EV act as a mobile battery. As at EECA's most recent assessment, two-way chargers are **emerging but not yet widely available** in New Zealand; EECA has run real-world trials (including in Queenstown) ahead of any wider rollout.
