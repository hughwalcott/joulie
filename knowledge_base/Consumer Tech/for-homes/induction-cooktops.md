# Induction cooktops

## Efficiency
Electric cooking uses **about half as much energy** as cooking with gas. A gas burner is **roughly 30% efficient** (much heat escapes around the pot), whereas an **induction cooktop is roughly 80–90% efficient** because it heats the cookware directly using an electromagnetic field.

## Cost and savings
- **Around $2,980 installed**, lasting **about 15 years**.
- Direct cooking-energy saving over gas is **modest — about $17 a year**.
- The larger saving comes from **avoiding the daily gas fixed/connection charge** if you disconnect from piped gas entirely — roughly **$1–$1.60 a day (over $580 a year)**, regardless of how much gas is used.

## Safety
The cooktop surface stays much **cooler** than a gas flame or standard electric element (heat is generated in the pot itself), reducing burn risk, and there is **no open flame or combustion by-products** released into the kitchen.

## Emissions
An induction cooktop can save **around 2,000 kg of emissions over 15 years** versus a gas or LPG hob, on New Zealand's renewable grid.
