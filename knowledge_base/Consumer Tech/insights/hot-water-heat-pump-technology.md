# How a hot water heat pump works

## The refrigeration cycle
A hot water heat pump uses a **vapour-compression refrigeration cycle** — the same principle as a fridge, run in reverse:
1. A **refrigerant absorbs heat from the surrounding air** and evaporates from a liquid into a gas (in the evaporator).
2. That gas is **compressed**, raising its temperature above the target water temperature.
3. The hot, pressurised refrigerant passes through a **condenser coil**, giving up its heat to the water and turning back into a liquid.
4. The cooled liquid refrigerant returns to the start to repeat the cycle.

Because it **moves** existing heat rather than generating it, it delivers far more heat energy than the electricity it consumes. Its **coefficient of performance (COP)** typically ranges from about **2 to 4**, versus about 1 for a standard electric element.

## Integrated vs split systems
- **Integrated (all-in-one):** heat pump and cylinder combined in a single outdoor unit; simplest where there is outdoor room.
- **Split:** heat pump unit outside, cylinder installed separately (often indoors), connected by refrigerant pipework; more flexible where space is tight, and a split unit can often be **retrofitted to an existing hot water cylinder**, lowering cost and disruption.
- Both configurations use the same underlying technology and achieve **similar efficiency** — the choice comes down to layout and space, not efficiency.
