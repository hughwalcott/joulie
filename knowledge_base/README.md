# Document Resources for Joulie
This package contains the resoruces that will make up Joulie's knowledge base. 
This knowledge base is a collection of plain-text documents (Markdown files, ending in .md) describing New Zealand home electrification, energy use, the rules of the electricity system, and where households can get help. It was assembled from trusted New Zealand sources. Each source lives in its own folder so we always know where a fact came from and what we're allowed to do with it. There are about 150 content files plus a short "MANIFEST" file in each folder (a contents-and-notes page). 

## How each file is built
Every file has two parts:
### Header
A short header at the top (between two lines of three dashes). This is "metadata" — labelled information about the file. The most important labels are:
 • title — the plain name of the document. 
 • source_url — the exact web page it came from. 
 • publisher — which organisation produced it. 
 • section — where it sits on the source website. 
 • content_stance — whether the content is authoritative fact (from a government body) or a stated position/advocacy (from Rewiring Aotearoa). This is the single most important field for Joulie to respect. 
 • licence — what we are legally allowed to do with the content. 
 • source_date and retrieved — when the information is from, and when we collected it (all collected on 10 July 2026). 
 • corpus_note — any warnings or context for that specific file.
### Body 
The body — the actual information, rewritten in clear, faithful language with website menus, ads and clutter removed.

## The five sources (folders)
EECA — Energy Efficiency and Conservation Authority (from eeca.govt.nz) Folder contains: "for-homes" (about 34 files) and "regulations" (about 32 files), roughly 66 files plus around 10 earlier files. What it's for: practical, how-to advice for households — heating and cooling, hot water, cooking, insulation, solar, the Warmer Kiwi Homes grants, appliance energy-rating labels, and the efficiency regulations behind them. Licence note: EECA content is used with reuse approval still being sought, so treat it as "permission pending". A few Australian E3 documents inside are openly licensed (CC BY 4.0).

Rewiring Aotearoa (from rewiring.nz) Suggested folder: C:\Joulie-data-rewiring Folder contains: "go-electric" (9 files), "learn" (17 FAQ files), "learn/explainers" (14 long-form explainers), and "reports" (6 research reports) — 46 files. What it's for: education, economics and advocacy on going electric — practical guides, a plain-language electrification FAQ, in-depth explainers, and well-regarded research reports (for example Electric Homes and The Machine Count). Important: Rewiring is an advocacy charity, not a regulator. These files are marked as Rewiring's stated positions, and Joulie should present their claims as "Rewiring's argument" rather than neutral fact. Reuse approval is pending.

Electricity Authority (from ea.govt.nz) Suggested folder: C:\Joulie-data-ea Folder contains: "your-power" (15 consumer files), "regulations" (7 plain-language rule summaries), and "data-insights" (4 data dashboards) — 26 files. What it's for: the rules of the electricity market and consumers' rights — connecting solar to the grid, meters, understanding bills, time-of-use plans, consumer-care protections (including for medically dependent households), and keeping the power on. Licence note: this content is openly licensed (CC BY 4.0), and the Act, Code and Regulations aren't subject to copyright at all — so it's clean to reuse with attribution. It is general information, not legal advice.

Commerce Commission (from comcom.govt.nz) Suggested folder: C:\Joulie-data-comcom Folder contains: "lines-charges" (4 files). What it's for: explaining why the "lines" (network) part of a power bill costs what it does — the biggest driver of recent bill increases. Covers the split of roles between the two regulators, why lines charges are rising, and the current price limits. Licence note: openly licensed (CC BY 4.0).

MBIE — Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (from mbie.govt.nz) Suggested folder: C:\Joulie-data-mbie Folder contains: "energy-data" (4 files) and "policy" (2 files) — 6 files. What it's for: the official government energy statistics (the authoritative numbers behind everything else), the official future-demand scenarios out to 2050, the factual gas-supply picture, and the official definition of energy hardship. Licence note: openly licensed (mostly CC BY 4.0). Statistics are point-in-time and get revised, so always cite the year.

## The shared Resources file
At the top level of the combined dataset there is one extra file: RESOURCES-FOR-FURTHER-INFORMATION. This is a single, tidy list of the official interactive tools and calculators across all the sources — for example EECA's home-energy-savings calculator, Rewiring's electric calculator, and the Electricity Authority's "Billy" plan-comparison site. Every link in it was checked and confirmed working. This file is meant to sit at the root of the whole dataset, not inside one source folder, because it draws on all of them. Joulie can use it to point people to a tool when they want to run their own numbers.

## How to use these files with Joulie (guidance for the team)
• Keep the source folders separate. This preserves where each fact came from and keeps the different licences clear. 
• Read the MANIFEST in each folder first. It lists the files, flags anything to watch, and records the licence for that source. 
• Respect authoritative versus advocacy. Government sources (EECA, Electricity Authority, Commerce Commission, MBIE) are authoritative. Rewiring Aotearoa is advocacy — its claims should be attributed to Rewiring, not stated as fact. 
• Always cite the year for statistics. Energy figures change yearly (for example, the share of renewable electricity swings with rainfall), so a number without its year can mislead. 
• Treat regulator content as information, not legal advice. Point people to the official Code or their retailer for anything binding. 
• Mind the licences before publishing anything externally. Government content here is openly reusable with attribution; the EECA and Rewiring content is still "approval pending", so flag that if outputs are shared publicly. 
• Use the Resources file for tools. When someone wants a personalised estimate, direct them to the relevant calculator rather than guessing numbers, and note that results are indicative.
