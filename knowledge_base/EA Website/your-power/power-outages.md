# Power outages (planned and unplanned)

Power cuts are rare, but companies can't guarantee a constant supply, so it's best to be prepared.

## Why outages happen

- **Planned outages** are scheduled for necessary maintenance on distribution networks or Transpower's national grid. Affected properties may lose power if supply can't be re-routed. You'd be told in advance by your **retailer, local lines company or Transpower**.
- **Unplanned outages** happen when distribution or transmission infrastructure is damaged by bad weather, natural disasters or equipment failure.

## What to do in a power cut

- Switch off electrical appliances that shouldn't be left unattended when power returns.
- Leave one light on so you know when power is restored.
- On a cold day/evening, check on neighbours and wrap up warm.

## Compensation

Power companies **do not usually compensate** for lost business, inconvenience or loss caused by supply problems. Utilities Disputes publishes a factsheet on when you might be able to claim.

## If you're medically dependent

If you or someone in your household relies on electricity for medical reasons, tell your power company immediately and apply for **medically dependent consumer** status — and tell any new company if you switch, as they won't know unless you do. Even then, a constant supply can't be guaranteed, so agree an **emergency response plan** with your health practitioner (see the medically dependent consumers page).
