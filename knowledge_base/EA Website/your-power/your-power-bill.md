# Your power bill (charges, terms and buy-back explained)

Electricity is charged per **unit** — one unit is **1 kilowatt-hour (kWh)**, i.e. 1,000 watts used for one hour (running a 1,000 W heater for an hour uses one kWh). Your power company sets the price per kWh (in cents), and you also pay a **fixed/daily charge** that covers grid and network maintenance regardless of how much power you use.

**Upcoming change:** from **30 October 2026**, bills must show charges in clearer, more consistent, plain language, making plans easier to compare. (This is part of the Authority's billing reforms, which also cap "back bills" at six months and require retailers to check annually that residential customers are on the best plan the retailer offers.)

## Common bill terms

- **Anytime rate** — the typical rate for any unit used, day or night, that isn't "controlled" by your power company.
- **Off-peak rate** — power is cheaper to generate outside peak periods (early morning, late evening), so a company may offer a lower per-unit price then to encourage off-peak use and ease pressure on the system (see the time-of-use plan page).
- **Controlled** — if you have a "controlled" hot-water cylinder, you may get a lower rate in exchange for letting the company switch the cylinder off briefly when local demand is high.
- **Fixed / daily charge** — a set daily fee for being connected, charged regardless of usage.

## If you generate your own power (buy-back / export)

If you generate power and are connected to the grid, your bill may have a **buy-back** section: exported power is paid into your account or deducted from your bill, measured in c/kWh. For example, exporting 20 kWh in a month at 13 c/kWh gives a $2.60 credit. Depending on your usage and generation, you could be paid more for what you export than you pay for what you use — though you'll **still pay the fixed charges**. (Spot-pricing contracts, where you pay the live wholesale price, exist but are uncommon; most plans use fixed charges over a set period that smooth out wholesale fluctuations.)

## What makes up the bill

New Zealand's electricity system relies on several services, all paid for through your bill. The Authority publishes a breakdown (updated annually at end of June) of **seven cost types** as a share of the average monthly household bill:

- **Transmission** — much electricity is generated at large South Island hydro stations and must be moved around the country via the national grid.
- **Distribution** — local lines that carry power to your house, which must be built and maintained.
- **Generation** — producing the electricity.
- **Retail operating costs** — your power company buying and managing supply on your behalf.
- **Metering** — reading and maintaining your meter.
- **GST** and **government levies** — for operating and regulating the market.

Lines charges (transmission + distribution) are regulated by the **Commerce Commission** and typically make up roughly one-half to two-thirds of recent bill increases. You can request an **itemised bill** from your power company for any period, and compare your costs against regional averages using Billy (billy.govt.nz) or the Authority's regional power prices dashboard.
