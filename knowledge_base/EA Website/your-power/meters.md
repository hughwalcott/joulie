# Meters (types, smart meters and data)

An electricity meter records your power use in kilowatt-hours (kWh). This matters for home electrification because access to **time-of-use plans, solar buy-back, and easy switching** generally depends on having a smart meter that captures **half-hourly** data.

## The three main meter types in New Zealand

- **Smart meters** — record half-hourly use electronically and transmit it to your power company via cellular reception. Around **83% of NZ residential connections** now have one (~1.5 million).
- **Non-smart / non-communicating meters** — work like smart meters but are read manually; used where there's little or no cell reception.
- **Analogue / "legacy" meters** — record consumption mechanically and are read manually; very few remain in use.

## Benefits of a smart meter

- **Near real-time information** on your use, so you can see when you could be more efficient.
- **Accurate (not estimated) billing** — you pay only for what you use.
- **Access to more plans** — including time-of-use plans with a "free hour of power" or cheaper night rates, which need half-hourly data.

If you don't have a smart meter and want one, contact your power company (there may be a cost). Retailers are required to ensure there's an **accurate, certified meter** at your property, and may need to replace or recertify meters and access them to do so.

## Data and privacy

Your consumption data is **encrypted in transit** on both cellular and radio networks. The Electricity Industry Participation Code 2010 requires your power company (and the metering equipment provider) to keep your consumption data secure, and participants are bound by the Privacy Act. Meter data carries an **ICP identifier** (your connection's reference), not your name or address, and can only be disclosed to you or people you authorise.

## The Authority's role and disputes

The Authority regulates the **standards, installation, testing, accuracy, reading and data-security** requirements for meters, but does **not** mandate smart meters or specify which type is installed. If you're unhappy with your retailer's response about a smart meter, you can contact **Utilities Disputes**, a free, independent dispute-resolution service.
