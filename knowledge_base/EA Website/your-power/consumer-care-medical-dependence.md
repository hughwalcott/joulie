# If you rely on electricity for medical reasons (medically dependent consumers)

If you or someone at home relies on electricity to **prevent serious harm to health**, tell your power company — they must help you get registered as a **medically dependent consumer**.

## The core protection (and its limits)

- Your retailer **cannot disconnect** a **post-pay** account if it knows a medically dependent consumer permanently or temporarily resides there — even for non-payment. If they disconnect you by mistake, they must reconnect **immediately**.
- You are **still required to pay your bills**, and the company can still take action for non-payment (short of disconnection).
- **Prepay plans are different:** on prepay, supply will stop if/when the account runs out of credit. If you're medically dependent, discuss whether a prepay plan is suitable.

## Getting certified

Consult a **qualified health practitioner** (e.g. Health NZ Te Whatu Ora, a private hospital, or an appropriately registered GP). If you qualify, they give you a **Notice of Potential Medically Dependent Consumer Status** ("HP notice") to pass to your retailer as soon as possible — keep a copy for yourself.

## A constant supply can't be guaranteed — have a plan

Power cuts still happen for many reasons, and being medically dependent does **not** guarantee uninterrupted supply. You should agree an **emergency response plan** with your healthcare providers and support people, and have **backup power** and alternative arrangements ready for when the power goes out. In emergency outages, efforts are made to contact medically dependent consumers where time permits, but this can't be assured.

## Help and complaints

For help finding a suitable plan, contact your power company (or compare via Billy, billy.govt.nz). If you have concerns that a disconnection didn't meet the Consumer Care Obligations, you can raise a concern with the Electricity Authority.
