# Solar power (generating, storing and selling your own power)

Solar can help a household become more self-sufficient, reduce its carbon footprint and lower energy costs. Any household or business can generate power for its own use and sell the excess back into the grid.

## Generating your own power

Small-scale systems let you generate power, store it and sell it back to the grid. You can generate using solar panels, small wind turbines, micro-hydro, biomass/biogas engines, or diesel/bio-diesel generators — but most people choose **rooftop solar panels** because they're relatively cheap and easy to install and maintain. A typical setup is panels, an inverter, and sometimes a battery bank to store excess generation. Some lenders, including the major banks, now offer low-interest loans for home generation systems.

## Being paid for what you export

The Electricity Authority has decided that households and businesses should be **paid more for supplying electricity back to the grid when it's most needed**. This means better prices for solar users who can store power during the day and release it at peak times (like the evening when everyone gets home). This sits alongside the Authority's wider "more choices for how we use, buy and sell electricity" reforms.

## Benefits

- **Saves money** — you generate free power to use; selling excess back lowers your bill, and even if you don't cover all your own use you still reduce it. Electricity prices are likely to rise over time, adding to the saving.
- **Can increase property value** — buyers tend to pay more for a home with solar, and most solar costs are upfront so savings start immediately.
- **Helps the environment** — clean generation reduces your carbon footprint and supports New Zealand's net-zero-by-2050 target.

## Is your property suitable?

- Output varies with weather — even light cloud can significantly cut generation.
- Solar works across Aotearoa but performs best in high-sunshine regions (e.g. Nelson/Marlborough, the Far North). It's better value if you have a **battery**, or if you use a lot of power during the day.
- For best output, panels should face **north** (roof ideally north-east to north-west facing) with a **15–45° pitch**. A solar company can assess viability.

## Solar generation vs your usage

Solar generates when the sun shines, which doesn't match a typical daily use pattern (morning, evening, sometimes overnight). Peak household use is morning and evening. That's why a **battery plus a grid connection** works well — store daytime generation for later, buy extra from the grid when needed, and sell what you don't use.

## Rules you must comply with

- **Code Part 6 (Connection of distributed generation):** if you connect solar to the grid to sell power back, you must comply with Part 6 of the Electricity Industry Participation Code 2010, including inverter standards and rules for connecting to the distribution network.
- **Your lines company (distributor):** solar can overload local lines, so you must contact and work with your local lines company, which has network connection rules, approval criteria, and requirements for the correct inverter type — typically one that can be **remotely turned off** in an over-power situation.
- **Registration:** the Authority does *not* require consumers with their own generation to register as a generator participant **unless the system can export more than 10 MW** back into the grid.
- **Council consent:** solar systems typically don't need council consent, but check with your council or installer.

## How to get started

1. **Evaluate your energy use** — ask your power company for your consumption data.
2. **Research** whether solar suits your home/business — compare your usage with likely output (tools include the SEANZ Solar Optimiser and the EECA Solar power calculator).
3. **Consider a battery** — to store generation and sell what you don't need.
4. **Consider an import/export meter** — you need one to sell power back; ask your retailer or metering provider.
5. **Get quotes** and choose products and an installer.
6. **Contact your retailer, metering provider and lines company** about connecting, selling back, and installing the import/export meter.
7. **Get the system and meter installed**, then start enjoying the savings.
