# How electricity works (generation to your home)

New Zealand's electricity reaches you through a chain of four roles: **generation → transmission → distribution → retail**.

## The journey of electricity

1. **Generation** — electricity is produced at power stations (much of it renewable: hydro, geothermal, wind, and increasingly solar).
2. **Transmission** — from power stations, electricity flows through large **high-voltage transmission lines** to substations. It's sent at high voltage because a small proportion is lost as heat along the lines. This national grid ("the grid") is owned, operated, maintained and developed by **Transpower** (a state-owned enterprise), which runs it in real time and charges lines companies a fee for using it.
3. **Distribution** — **lines companies** (also called distributors or network companies) deliver electricity from local substations through local networks to homes and businesses.
4. **Retail** — your **power company (retailer)** buys electricity on your behalf, manages your account and bills you.

## A changing system

Electricity demand is growing as the economy electrifies. To meet it, more generation is being built (geothermal, wind and solar farms) and new technologies are being introduced, such as **smart grid technologies and smart energy systems**, plus more small-scale renewable generation (like rooftop solar) located closer to communities.

## Reliability

Power cuts are rare but happen from time to time. Even though power companies aim to supply electricity constantly, a constant supply **cannot be guaranteed** (see the "Power outages" and "Keeping the lights on" pages). For accessible explainers, the Authority publishes its "Eye on electricity" series.
