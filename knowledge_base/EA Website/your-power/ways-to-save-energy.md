# Ways to save energy (efficiency tips and appliances)

Small changes to your home and appliances save power and reduce bills. Using less also **helps the environment** — less use means less generation and infrastructure is needed, and New Zealand typically generates **5–15% of electricity per day from non-renewable sources** (coal and gas). The less power we use, especially at **peak**, the more we can rely on renewables (supporting net zero by 2050).

## Top tips

- **Consider a time-of-use plan** — if you can shift use to off-peak times, you can save (see the dedicated time-of-use page). Best for households that can move big loads like EV charging, hot water, spa/pool to cheaper hours.
- **Switch to LED bulbs** — incandescent bulbs waste most of their power as heat (only ~30% becomes light). An LED giving the same light as a 100 W incandescent uses only ~8–12 W. LEDs cost more upfront but pay off quickly, so switch now rather than waiting for old bulbs to blow.
- **Rethink resistive heating** — heating with resistive heaters (fan, bar, oil heaters) is very energy-intensive and expensive; more efficient options (like heat pumps) cost far less to run.
- **Reduce heat loss through windows** — thermally broken or non-conductive frames (e.g. uPVC over aluminium) keep more heat in.
- **Manage moisture** — drying clothes indoors adds up to five litres of moisture to the air, and moist air costs more to heat; running the dryer briefly can be more cost-effective.
- **Upgrade old appliances** — replace old energy-draining appliances with high energy-star models; this is often cheaper over time than keeping inefficient ones going.

## Free help

**Eco Design Advisors** work at some local councils and can provide **free home assessments**. For appliance running costs and energy tools, EECA (Gen Less) is the complementary government resource.
