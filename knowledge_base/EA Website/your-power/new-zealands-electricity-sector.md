# New Zealand's electricity sector (how the market is structured)

There are nearly **two million electricity consumers** in Aotearoa — from a typical household using about **7 MWh per year** to the Tiwai Point aluminium smelter at about **570 MW** (around a third of all South Island electricity and 13% nationally). The system is transforming to electrify New Zealand and reach **net zero by 2050**, shifting toward more renewable intermittent generation (wind, solar), **distributed energy resources** (rooftop solar and batteries), mass participation, and **two-way power flows**.

## The three key elements

Supplying electricity involves **generating** it, **transporting** it to distribution companies, and **selling** it to customers.

### Generation
Generation companies produce electricity at power stations and inject it into either transmission lines (**grid-connected generation**) or distribution lines (**embedded generation**). Many companies generate, but the majority comes from four — **Contact, Genesis, Meridian and Mercury**. Electricity is sold via the **wholesale/spot market** to retailers and large industrial users, with buyers purchasing every **30 minutes via auctions** at a spot price that changes each auction to reflect market conditions. Overall there are about **80 generation companies, 62 retailers, and four "gentailers"** (combined generator-retailers).

### Transmission
The national grid transports electricity at high voltage (up to **220,000 volts**) using a high-voltage alternating current system: over **11,000 km of lines, ~25,000 pylons, and 170 substations**. A **high-voltage direct current (HVDC)** inter-island link, with a line under Cook Strait, transfers power between the North and South Islands. (The grid is owned and operated by Transpower — see "How electricity works".)

### Distribution and retail
Lines companies deliver electricity through local networks to homes and businesses, and retailers sell it to you. You can choose the combination of price, product and service that suits you, and compare/switch using Billy (billy.govt.nz).

## The Authority's role in a changing system

As New Zealand's electricity regulator, the Authority aims to provide proactive, fit-for-purpose regulation so consumers get the benefits of innovation and competition at least cost while keeping a reliable supply. It has a multi-year plan to improve security of supply, investment and innovation — boosting competition and efficiency to put downward pressure on prices and improve affordability. A low-carbon future will require large quantities of new renewable generation, much greater use of distributed energy resources, and new ways (and more participants) to take part.
