# Data: Shift to 10kW export limits

This dashboard tracks distributors' (lines companies') progress in setting a **default export limit of at least 10kW** for residential connections. It directly affects how much a home solar/battery system is allowed to send back to the grid.

## What export limits are

Export limits are **caps on how much electricity distributed generation** (like rooftop solar and batteries) can export to the network **at any one time**. They're a necessary tool for distributors to keep networks safe and reliable (e.g. within voltage limits). Under the previous rules, distributors had wide discretion, and some used a **blanket 5kW** limit for residential connections — unnecessarily curtailing exports and consumer benefit.

## The change

A new rule came into effect on **11 May 2026** ensuring all distributors set **at least 10kW** limits for small-scale distributed generation **where they can** (it won't be possible everywhere on every network). Distributors may also offer a **dynamic/flexible export limit** as an alternative to a fixed 10kW, flexing with network conditions (future-proofing for vehicle-to-grid). The Code also sets **default voltage-response settings for inverters** (using Australian settings, aligned with AS/NZS 4777) and standardises export-limit measurement/calculation across the **29 lines companies**; where a limit below 10kW is needed, distributors must use an industry-developed assessment methodology (intended as a last resort).

## Why it matters for households

Higher, consistent export limits let households install and benefit from **larger solar and battery systems**, improve return on investment clarity, and push more locally generated electricity house-to-house — lowering system costs for everyone. Because lines companies are **regional monopolies**, homeowners can't shop around for a better export limit, which is why the Authority standardised the default. (Example: Aurora Energy lifted its single-phase residential limit from 5kW to 10kW on 1 August 2025 after a smart-meter data trial.) Check the live dashboard for each network's current position.
