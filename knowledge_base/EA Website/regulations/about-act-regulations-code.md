# About the Act, Regulations and Code (the legal framework)

New Zealand's electricity industry is governed by a three-layer legal framework administered by the Electricity Authority.

## The Act

The **Electricity Industry Act 2010** is the legislation under which the Authority operates. It provides the framework for regulating the industry and **authorises the Authority to make regulations and to make and amend the Electricity Industry Participation Code 2010**, and sets out the process the Authority must follow. Section 15 sets the Authority's **statutory objective** and section 16 lists its functions. The Authority's statutory objective is to **promote competition in, reliable supply by, and the efficient operation of the electricity industry for the long-term benefit of consumers** (with a later addition relating to promoting consumers' interests in the context of the transition to a low-emissions economy).

## The Regulations

Regulations made under the Act include, for example, the **Electricity (Exemptions from Registration) Regulations 2022** (which exempt two classes of participant from registering with the Authority), the **Electricity Industry (Participants and Roles) Regulations 2012**, and the **Electricity Industry (Enforcement) Regulations 2010**.

## The Code

The **Electricity Industry Participation Code 2010** sets out the detailed **rules and responsibilities of all participants** in the electricity industry — nearly every aspect of how the industry operates. It's organised into numbered Parts (1–17), several of which are summarised in this corpus (Part 6 distributed generation, Part 6B distributor pricing, Part 10 metering, Part 11A consumer care).

## How the layers interact

Any Code amendment must be **consistent with the Authority's statutory objective**, and can impose obligations only on **industry participants or on the Authority** (not on consumers directly). If any Code provision **conflicts with the Act or regulations, the Act or regulations prevail**. It's each participant's responsibility to comply, and participants are legally obliged to report all Code breaches.
