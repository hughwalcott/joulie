# Code Part 6 — Connection of distributed generation (plain-language summary)

**Distributed generation (DG)** is electricity generated at or near where it's used and connected directly to a **local network** (not the national grid) — from household rooftop solar and batteries to small wind, micro-hydro, and commercial solar/wind farms. **Part 6 of the Code** governs the **application and approval process** between a prospective distributed generator and the connected distributor (lines company). This is the key rule for anyone connecting home solar or a battery that can export.

## What Part 6 requires

- A defined **application and approval process** to connect DG to a distributor's network.
- **Standards** for the power inverter and for connecting safely to the distribution network (lines companies typically require an inverter that can be **remotely switched off** in an over-power situation).
- Different pathways by size: the Authority provides guidelines for **small-scale DG** and separate guidance for **DG greater than 10 kW**.
- The Authority **monitors and supports compliance** by distributors and generators, can take **enforcement action** for non-compliance, and may help with **dispute resolution** between generators and distributors.

## Export limits (recent change)

Under rules announced in July 2025 (in effect **1 April 2026**), lines companies must set a **default export limit of 10 kW** for straightforward, small-scale DG such as household solar and battery systems — a nationally consistent, transparent approach. Lines companies may also offer a **dynamic/flexible export limit** as an alternative, flexing above or below 10 kW as network conditions change (rather than a permanently lower cap). This future-proofs the rules for smarter technologies, including **vehicle-to-grid (V2G)** charging that returns power from EV batteries. At the time of the change, about **75,000 households had solar** and **14,700 of those had batteries**, but export had been capped lower than necessary.

## Registration threshold

You do **not** need to register with the Authority as a generator participant unless your system can export **more than 10 MW** to the grid — so ordinary household solar/battery systems don't need to register.

## Note: Part 6 is under review

Part 6 has changed little in ~20 years, while the number, size and complexity of connection applications have grown. The Authority's **Network connections project** is progressively updating it (e.g. easier application processes for residential solar, connection obligations, and up-front charges), with changes phased in through 2026–2027.
