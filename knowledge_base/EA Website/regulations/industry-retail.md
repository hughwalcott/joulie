# Retail market (retailers' role, obligations and metering)

**Retailers** (power companies) buy electricity on the wholesale market and sell it to homes and businesses, managing accounts and billing. The retail market is where most consumers interact with the electricity system, so several Code obligations protect consumers.

## Retailers' obligations (a consumer-relevant selection)

Retailers must comply with all their Code obligations. The Authority's guidelines cover, among other things:

- **Meaningful choice** — offering a range of pricing plans, products and services so consumers can make informed decisions.
- **Fair contracts** — contracting principles and minimum terms and conditions for domestic contracts that are fair and reasonable, covering connections, disconnections and contract termination, and the actual supply of electricity and related services (including metering).
- **No unfair costs** — unexpected or unfair costs should not be imposed on consumers.
- **Transparent price changes** — applying guidelines on how to communicate price changes to consumers and the media.
- **Customer compensation scheme** — paying qualifying customers when they're asked to conserve electricity during an official conservation/compensation campaign.
- **Consumer Care Obligations** — the Part 11A protections (see that summary).
- **Billing requirements** — from 30 October 2026, clearer plain-language bills (the Authority provides an implementation toolkit and exemplar bills), plus capped back-bills and annual best-plan checks.
- **Tariff transparency** — under clause 11.32G, if asked, a retailer must provide information about its generally available retail tariff plans (a consumer shouldn't have to enter a specific address to obtain this).

## Metering and the registry

Retailers must ensure an accurate, certified **meter** is in place (see Part 10 and the consumer Meters page), typically working with a metering equipment provider. The **electricity registry** records every connection by its **ICP** identifier, enables energy flows between retailers to be reconciled, and informs retailers when a customer **switches** supplier — which is what makes switching quick (especially with a smart meter, since no manual final read is needed).
