# Code Part 6B — Distributor pricing methodologies (plain-language summary)

Part 6B sets rules for **how lines companies (distributors) price their networks** — which drives the **distribution (lines) component of your power bill**. Distributors must **reference the Authority's distribution pricing principles**, **publish their pricing methodology**, and **discuss their pricing plans and progress** with the Authority each year. (Distributors are *also* subject to price-quality regulation by the **Commerce Commission**.)

## Distributed generation pricing principles (why this matters for solar/batteries)

A key part of this area is the **Distributed Generation Pricing Principles (DGPPs)** — the rules for how lines companies recover costs from people who supply electricity to the local network, and how they **reward** them for the benefits their supply creates. The aim is that distributed generation and batteries connect **where and when they deliver the most value**, keeping costs low for everyone over time.

- **Peak export rebate (effective 1 April 2026):** lines companies must **pay a rebate** when households and eligible small businesses inject electricity into the network **at peak times** — rewarding the cost savings that small-scale generators create by supplying power when it's most needed.
- Connection charges for distributed generation must be based on the **reasonable, incremental costs** the distributor incurs, and must consider any avoided/avoidable costs.

## Ongoing reform

The Authority is running multi-stage reform of these pricing rules to fix issues such as: some costs created by distributed generators being under-allocated (and paid by everyone else); unclear cost/benefit allocation; and situations where a generator whose connection triggers a network extension pays the **full upgrade cost** (a barrier to new generation). More cost-reflective pricing is intended to unlock network capacity for solar and batteries and encourage generation where it brings the greatest benefit. (This is the network-pricing counterpart to Rewiring Aotearoa's "symmetrical export tariff" advocacy — cross-reference in the Rewiring corpus.)
