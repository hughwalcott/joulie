# Healthy homes standards

The healthy homes standards set minimum requirements for rental properties to make them warmer, drier and healthier. They sit under the Residential Tenancies Act 1986 and are set out in the Healthy Homes Standards Regulations 2019. Landlords are responsible for meeting the standards and keeping the property compliant; non-compliance is a breach of the Act and can attract financial penalties (up to $7,200 per breach for smaller landlords, higher for larger portfolios), and every tenancy agreement must include a healthy homes compliance statement.

## The five standards

1. **Heating** — one or more **fixed heaters** capable of directly heating the **main living room** to at least **18 °C**. Heaters must be an acceptable type and meet the minimum heating capacity for the room's size (a qualifying heater must generally be at least 1.5 kW; required capacity is worked out using Tenancy Services' online heating assessment tool, which accounts for room size, ceiling height, window area and climate zone).
2. **Insulation** — ceiling and underfloor insulation has been **compulsory in all rental homes since 1 July 2019**. The healthy homes insulation standard builds on that: some older or thinner insulation must be topped up or replaced to meet the current minimum, subject to specified exemptions (e.g. where installation is unsafe or physically impractical).
3. **Ventilation** — each liveable space must have windows or doors that open to outside and can be fixed open; kitchens and bathrooms must have **extractor fans** that vent outside to remove moisture.
4. **Moisture ingress and drainage** — efficient drainage, guttering and downpipes for stormwater, surface water and ground water; where there is an enclosed, accessible sub-floor, a **ground moisture barrier** is required.
5. **Draught stopping** — no unreasonable gaps or holes in walls, ceilings, windows, floors and doors that cause noticeable draughts (gaps over ~3 mm generally need sealing); open fireplaces must be blocked unless the tenant agrees otherwise.

## Compliance timeline (important currency point)

Insulation has been compulsory since **1 July 2019**. The full healthy homes standards were phased in for private rentals from 1 July 2021, with a compliance window (originally 90 days, later 120 days) from the start or renewal of a tenancy. That phase-in has now ended: since the **final deadline of 1 July 2025, all private rental properties must comply with all five standards at all times**, regardless of when the tenancy started — there is no remaining grace period. Boarding houses, Kāinga Ora and registered community housing provider properties were required to comply earlier.

## ⚠ Currency note

The key update since older guidance is the **1 July 2025 final deadline**: compliance is now continuous and universal for private rentals, not tied to a tenancy-start grace period. Confirm current requirements and any exemptions against Tenancy Services before relying on specifics, as standards and enforcement settings can change.
