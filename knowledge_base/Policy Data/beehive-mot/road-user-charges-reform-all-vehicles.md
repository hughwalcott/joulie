# Replacing petrol excise with electronic road user charges

## The reform

The Government has announced plans to eventually move **all light vehicles — not just diesel and electric — onto electronic road user charges (eRUC)**, and to phase out fuel excise duty (petrol tax, currently around 70 cents per litre). The rationale is that under a fuel-tax model, fuel-efficient and electric vehicles pay proportionally less toward road funding for the distance they travel, so a distance-based charge spreads the cost more evenly and is more durable as the fleet electrifies.

## Timeline (as announced)

- Legislation and market engagement were planned through **2026**.
- The RUC system is intended to open to **competing third-party providers from 2027**, so drivers could buy and manage RUC through commercial apps and services rather than only through NZTA.
- A **full-fleet transition date has deliberately not been set** — the Government has said it wants to prioritise getting implementation right rather than commit to a fixed date.

## Relationship to the EV RUC change

This reform is broader than, and separate from, the April 2024 change that brought light EVs and plug-in hybrids into the existing RUC system. That change ended the EV exemption; this reform is about moving the *whole* light fleet off petrol excise and onto eRUC over a longer horizon.

## ⚠ Currency note

This reform is still in progress and its timeline and detail can change. Treat the 2026 legislation / 2027 third-party-provider milestones as the announced plan, not settled implementation, and check for updated timing before relying on specifics. This is an appropriate area to hedge rather than state a firm transition date.
