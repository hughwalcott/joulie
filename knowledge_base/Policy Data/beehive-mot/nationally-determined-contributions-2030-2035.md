# New Zealand's Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) under the Paris Agreement

A Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) is the emissions-reduction pledge each country makes internationally under the Paris Agreement. New Zealand has two.

## First NDC — 2030

New Zealand's first NDC, updated in 2021, commits to reducing **net emissions to 50% below gross 2005 levels by 2030** (equivalent to about a 41% reduction on an emissions-budget accounting basis), within a provisional emissions budget of 571 megatonnes of CO2-equivalent for the 2021–2030 period.

## Second NDC — 2035

On **30 January 2025** the Government announced New Zealand's second NDC: a commitment to reduce **emissions by 51–55% below 2005 levels by 2035**.

## How NDCs differ from the domestic 2050 target

The NDCs are New Zealand's *international* pledges. They are distinct from the *domestic* framework — the 2050 target and the five-yearly emissions budgets set under the Climate Change Response Act — although the two are related. The Government has also signalled it is investigating a "split-gas" approach to future Paris commitments (treating biogenic methane separately), consistent with the domestic split-gas target.

## ⚠ Currency / context note

The adequacy of NDC2 is contested: the Climate Change Commission's advice indicated more ambitious targets were feasible, and some commentators question whether NDC2 represents New Zealand's highest possible ambition or alignment with 1.5 °C. Present the 51–55%/2035 and 50%/2030 figures as the Government's stated targets, and treat debate over their adequacy as a matter on which views differ rather than settled fact.
