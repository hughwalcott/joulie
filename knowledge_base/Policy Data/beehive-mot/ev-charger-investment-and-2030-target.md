# Government investment in public EV chargers and the 2030 target

## The target

The Government's stated goal is **10,000 public EV charge points by 2030** — roughly one charger for every 40 EVs. Before the current programme began, New Zealand had **just over 1,800 public charge points**, which the Government describes as one of the lowest charger-to-EV ratios in the OECD.

## Investment and expansion

- **March 2026 announcement:** a $110m+ package — $52.7 million in government zero-interest loans alongside about $60 million co-invested by ChargeNet and Meridian Energy — to add **2,574 new charge points** (1,374 DC fast chargers and 1,200 AC chargers). With chargers already in progress plus this investment, the network was expected to **more than double to around 4,550 charge points**, about half in the six main urban centres and half in regional areas.
- **August 2026 (second round):** the Government opened a **second round of zero-interest loans (about $21 million)**, administered by National Infrastructure Funding and Financing (NIFFCo), covering up to 50% of eligible project costs at zero interest over a term of up to 12 years, to encourage private-sector-led buildout. Commentary at the time noted the two loan rounds together reach only about half of the 10,000 goal, so reaching the target will require considerable acceleration.

## Uptake context

The Government reported EV registrations in 2026 were up 96.4% on the same period the previous year, and points to EVs typically being cheaper to run than petrol on New Zealand's largely renewable electricity system.

## ⚠ Currency note

Charger counts move quickly and are reported on different bases. Independent reporting (Newsroom, March 2026) counted about 1,024 public charging points at the November 2023 election, 1,378 by December 2024, and 1,466 by December 2025 — lower than the "just over 1,800" the Government cites, likely because of differences in what is counted (in-service points vs points including those in progress, and points vs stations). Treat any single figure as a dated snapshot and check EECA's public EV charger dashboard / NZTA's live map for the current count.
