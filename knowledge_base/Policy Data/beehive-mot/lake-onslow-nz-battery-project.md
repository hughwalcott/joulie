# The NZ Battery Project and Lake Onslow

## What it was

The NZ Battery Project investigated large-scale storage options to cover **"dry years"** — when hydro lake levels are low and there is less renewable generation available. Its most prominent option was a **pumped-hydro scheme at Lake Onslow, in Central Otago**, which would store energy by pumping water uphill when power is plentiful and releasing it to generate when supply is tight.

## Why the government scrapped it

The Government **scrapped the Lake Onslow option in December 2023**, citing:

- an estimated cost of around **$16 billion**;
- a build timeline of **at least a decade**; and
- concern that the prospect of a large state project was **discouraging other private renewable investment** in the meantime.

The Government's stated alternative was to accelerate wind, solar and geothermal development and streamline consenting (via Electrify NZ) rather than pursue one large storage project, and it has pointed to the cancellation as helping restore private investment confidence.

## A private-led revival (2026)

Notably, although the *government* cancelled the state project, the **consortium behind Lake Onslow has continued privately** — as at mid-2026 it had secured funding to finalise a resource consent application, aiming to lodge it under the fast-track process before 2027. So "Lake Onslow" is best described as: cancelled as a government project in 2023, but being pursued privately.

## ⚠ Currency note

This is a politically charged and evolving topic. State clearly that the *government* scrapped the state-led Lake Onslow scheme in December 2023, and that any current activity is a separate private-led effort whose status should be re-checked. Dry-year risk is now being managed through a proposed reliability obligation (see the MBIE dry-year record) rather than a single storage project.
