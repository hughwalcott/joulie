# New Zealand's 2024 winter electricity supply crisis

## What happened

In **winter 2024**, a combination of high demand, **constrained hydro and wind generation**, and **limited gas supply** for gas-fired peaking generation pushed wholesale electricity prices **above $800/MWh** at times (compared with typical prices well under $200/MWh). Because gas is used as flexible backup ("peaking") generation, reduced gas availability lowered the system's ability to respond to tight conditions.

## Consequences

Some large industrial users **cut back or shut down operations** in response to spot prices; the New Zealand Aluminium Smelter's demand-response agreement with Meridian was called on, and industrial electricity demand fell year-on-year. The episode sparked wider debate about New Zealand's need for more flexible/peaking generation, clearer investment signals, and mechanisms such as long-term power purchase agreements to improve future resilience.

## Why it matters for electrification and policy

The 2024 crisis is the backdrop to several current policy workstreams: the proposed **dry-year reliability obligation**, the **Energy Package** (including LNG import facility procurement), and the emphasis in **Electrify NZ** on building more (and more diverse) renewable generation with adequate firming. It illustrates the central challenge of decarbonisation — meeting growing electrified demand reliably as the system becomes more weather-dependent and domestic gas declines.

## ⚠ Currency note

This describes a specific historical event (winter 2024). Later winters differ: for example, favourable hydro made the October–December 2025 quarter a record 96.4% renewable. Use this as context for *why* reliability policy is changing, not as a description of current market conditions.
