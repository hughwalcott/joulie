# New Zealand's 2050 emissions target

New Zealand uses a **"split-gas" target**, set in legislation under the Climate Change Response Act 2002 (as amended by the Zero Carbon Act 2019) and reviewed every five years. It treats biogenic methane separately from all other greenhouse gases, reflecting methane's different warming behaviour.

## The two components

1. **All greenhouse gases except biogenic methane** (carbon dioxide, nitrous oxide, and others) — must reach **net zero by 2050 and beyond**. Being a *net* target, actual emission reductions can be combined with removals such as forest carbon sequestration.
2. **Biogenic methane** (mostly from livestock and landfill) — managed under a separate **gross-reduction** target: at least **10% below 2017 levels by 2030**, and **14–24% below 2017 levels by 2050 and beyond**.

Most of New Zealand's methane is biogenic (produced by living things and processes); of biogenic methane, roughly 91% is from agriculture (livestock) and about 9% from landfill/waste.

## The 2050 methane range was revised (contested)

The 2050 biogenic methane range of **14–24%** reflects a change made by the Government — the Climate Change Commission's FAQ states the Government **last updated the target levels in December 2025**, revising the 2050 methane range **down from the original 24–47%** set when the target was first legislated. This followed a government-commissioned review against a "no additional warming" principle.

This change is politically contested:
- The Commission's own **2024 review recommended a *more* ambitious 2050 methane range (around 35–47%)**, not a weaker one, and did not recommend changing the 2030 requirement.
- Commentators and the Commission have noted the lower range is not aligned with a 1.5 °C pathway, and that there was not political consensus for the change.
- The 2030 requirement (10% below 2017) was left unchanged.

## ⚠ Currency / hedging note

The biogenic methane 2050 range is one of the most actively contested and recently changed figures in New Zealand climate policy. State the current legislated position as **14–24% below 2017 by 2050** (with 10% by 2030), note that this was revised in 2025 from 24–47% and is contested, and confirm the current legislated figures in the Climate Change Response Act (as amended) before treating them as settled. Do not present the methane target as uncontested.
