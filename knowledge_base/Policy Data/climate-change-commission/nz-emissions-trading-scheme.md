# The New Zealand Emissions Trading Scheme (NZ ETS)

The NZ ETS is New Zealand's main carbon-pricing tool. By putting a cost on emitting, it is intended to make lower-emissions choices — including electrifying heat and transport — more financially attractive.

## How it works

Businesses in covered sectors must report their greenhouse gas emissions and **surrender New Zealand Units (NZUs)** to cover them. Units are obtained by buying at government auction, purchasing from other participants, or (for some trade-exposed industries) receiving a free allocation. The supply of units and the auction settings are managed to align with New Zealand's emissions budgets and targets.

## Sectors covered (and when they entered)

- **Forestry** — since 2008.
- **Energy / liquid fossil fuels and industrial processes** — since 2010.
- **Waste (landfill)** — since 2013.

## Agriculture is not priced through the ETS

**Agriculture — roughly half of New Zealand's total emissions — is not currently priced through the ETS.** In 2025 the Government decided not to proceed with an on-farm agricultural emissions pricing system by 2030, favouring a technology-led and market-based approach instead. As a result, the ETS price effectively applies to around half of New Zealand's emissions.

## Role of the Climate Change Commission

The Commission provides independent advice to the Government on ETS settings (such as unit supply and price control settings) to help keep the scheme aligned with emissions budgets and the 2050 target, but the Government sets the rules.

## ⚠ Currency note

ETS settings (unit volumes, auction price floors and triggers) are reviewed and adjusted regularly, and agricultural pricing policy is politically sensitive and has changed. Treat the sector coverage and the "agriculture excluded / ~half of emissions priced" points as current-as-at-retrieval and confirm settings before quoting specific unit prices or volumes.
