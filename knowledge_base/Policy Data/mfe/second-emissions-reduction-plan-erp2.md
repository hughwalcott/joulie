# New Zealand's second emissions reduction plan (ERP2)

## What an emissions reduction plan is

An emissions reduction plan (ERP) is the Government's statutory plan, required under the Climate Change Response Act 2002 (section 5ZI), setting out the policies and actions to meet each successive emissions budget on the way to the 2050 target. Each budget is a five-year cap on total emissions.

## What ERP2 covers

ERP2 sets out the actions to meet the **second emissions budget (EB2), covering 2026–2030**. EB2 is a cap of about **305 megatonnes of CO2-equivalent** over the five years. ERP2 was **published in December 2024, amended in January 2026, and formally came into effect on 1 January 2026** (when the first ERP ended). It spans the Government's climate-strategy priority areas across key sectors: **energy, transport, agriculture, forestry and waste**.

## Electrification is central

The majority of emissions reductions expected in the second budget period come from reducing emissions from energy use, and **electrification is identified as the key way to meet New Zealand's early emissions budgets** — electrifying transport, home and water heating, and industrial process heat, supported by the "Electrify NZ" work programme to grow renewable generation. Forestry and carbon removals also play a large role, reflecting the Government's "net-based" approach.

## Notable policy positions in ERP2

- The Government decided in 2025 **not to proceed with on-farm agricultural emissions pricing by 2030**, favouring a technology-led, market-based approach.
- The final ERP2 projects New Zealand achieving net zero emissions by 2050, and reports EB3 (2031–35) tracking modestly unfavourable to budget (about 9.2 Mt CO2-e in the amended plan, improved from 17 Mt CO2-e in the July 2024 discussion document).

## Organisational note

The Ministry for the Environment (the plan's publisher) is now part of the **Ministry for Cities, Environment, Regions and Transport (MCERT)**; older links may resolve under either name.

## ⚠ Currency note

ERP2 has already been amended once (January 2026) and its projections and some policies (e.g. agricultural pricing, forestry settings) are subject to change. Use the amended-January-2026 version as the current reference and check for further amendments before relying on specific projection figures.
