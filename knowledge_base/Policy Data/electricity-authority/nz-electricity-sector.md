# How New Zealand's electricity sector is structured

New Zealand's electricity system runs from large generators, across the national grid, through local networks, to homes and businesses. Several distinct types of organisation are involved.

## Generation and the wholesale market

About **80 companies** participate in New Zealand's wholesale generation market, but the large majority of generation comes from four large **"gentailer"** companies (which both generate and retail electricity): **Contact Energy, Genesis Energy, Meridian Energy and Mercury Energy**. Generators offer electricity into a **wholesale spot market** where prices are set every half-hour through an auction-style process matching supply and demand at each grid location, under rules overseen by the Electricity Authority. Retailers and large users buy from this spot market or hedge their exposure with contracts.

## Transpower — the national grid and system operator

**Transpower** is the state-owned enterprise that owns and operates New Zealand's **national grid** — over **11,000 kilometres** of high-voltage transmission lines connecting generation to local distribution networks. Transpower also acts as the **System Operator**, balancing generation and demand in real time to keep the power system stable, and charges lines companies for use of the transmission network (a cost ultimately passed to consumers).

## Distribution (lines companies) and retailers

**29 local distribution companies ("lines companies")** own and operate around **150,000 kilometres** of local network that carries electricity from the national grid to individual premises. Around **62 retailers** then buy wholesale electricity and sell it to consumers, each setting their own prices. A household bill is built from wholesale energy costs, network (lines) charges, and retail margin.

## The Electricity Authority's role

The **Electricity Authority Te Mana Hiko** is the government regulator for the electricity market. Under the Electricity Industry Act 2010, it sets and enforces the rules governing the wholesale and retail markets, aiming to ensure electricity is supplied reliably and efficiently and that consumers benefit from competition and innovation at the lowest reasonable cost.

## ⚠ Currency note

Company counts (generators, lines companies, retailers) and the gentailer landscape shift over time with market entry, exit and mergers; the four-gentailer structure and the figures above were current as at retrieval. Confirm against the Electricity Authority before quoting exact counts.
