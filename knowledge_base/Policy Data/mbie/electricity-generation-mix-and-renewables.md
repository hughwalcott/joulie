# New Zealand's electricity generation mix and renewable share

New Zealand's electricity generation is predominantly renewable, dominated by hydro and geothermal, with wind and solar growing quickly and gas and coal used mainly as backup ("firming") when renewable output is low.

## Renewable share

- **Full-year 2024: 85.5% renewable** (down from 88.1% in 2023). The 2.6-point fall was driven by low hydro inflows, which required more coal and gas generation.
- **October–December 2025 quarter: a record 96.4% renewable**, driven by favourable hydro conditions and growing solar/geothermal output.
- The annual figure is always lower than the best quarter, because dry periods force more thermal (coal/gas) backup.

## Generation mix by fuel (2024 calendar year)

Per MBIE's *Energy in New Zealand 2025*: **hydro 53.5%, geothermal 19.9%, gas 9.3%, wind 8.9%, coal 5.1%, solar 1.4%**, with the remainder from other sources. Hydro and geothermal together provide the large majority of baseload renewable generation. Total national generation was about 43,879 GWh (up 0.9% on 2023).

## Why coal use rose in 2024

Coal-fired generation rose from **2.4% of the mix in 2023 to 5.1% in 2024**, mainly because low hydro lake inflows during winter reduced available hydro generation, requiring more thermal generation to keep the system reliable.

## Records and new capacity (2024)

Geothermal hit a record 8,741 GWh, wind a record 3,919 GWh, and solar a record 601 GWh. Renewable capacity grew about 556 MW; wind capacity nearly doubled from 691 MW (2020) to 1,269 MW (2024), and new geothermal plants (Tauhara, May 2024; Te Huka 3, October 2024) added about 225 MW.

## ⚠ Currency note

Renewable share varies quarter to quarter with hydro and wind conditions — treat any single percentage as a dated snapshot, not a fixed figure. The full-year mix percentages are from MBIE's annual *Energy in New Zealand* series (2024 data); check the latest edition and MBIE's New Zealand Energy Quarterly for more current figures. (For example, later quarters of 2025 ranged from the low-80s to the mid-90s percent renewable depending on hydro.)
