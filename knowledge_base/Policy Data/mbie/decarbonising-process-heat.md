# Decarbonising industrial process heat

Process heat — heat used in manufacturing, food processing and similar industrial activity — is one of the largest remaining fossil-fuel uses in the economy outside transport, and a major focus for business electrification.

## How reliant industry is on fossil fuels

MBIE figures show **56% of New Zealand's industrial process heat is currently supplied by fossil fuels**, mainly natural gas and coal. Switching this heat to electricity (electrode boilers, industrial heat pumps) or biomass is a central decarbonisation opportunity.

## Coal boiler phase-out

Government policy (via National Policy Statement and National Environmental Standard settings) **bans the installation of new low- and medium-temperature coal boilers**, and **requires existing ones to be phased out by 2037**. Large process-heat users must also develop greenhouse gas emissions reduction plans.

## Support to switch

Fuel-switching is co-funded through EECA's **Government Investment in Decarbonising Industry (GIDI) Fund**, which supports replacing coal or gas boilers with electrode boilers, heat pumps or biomass systems. (Detailed GIDI figures and projects are covered in the EECA corpus.)

## Why it matters

Process-heat decarbonisation, alongside declining domestic gas supply, is a key driver of projected growth in industrial electricity demand — and of the case for businesses reliant on gas or coal to start planning alternatives.
