# Why New Zealand's natural gas supply is declining

New Zealand's natural gas comes from a small number of ageing fields in the Taranaki region, and both the reserves and the rate they can be produced are falling faster than previously forecast. Because New Zealand has historically had no facilities to import or export natural gas, all gas produced is used domestically — which means any decline in supply is met by a decline in demand, pushing gas users toward alternatives such as electrification.

## Reserves (latest: as at 1 January 2026)

MBIE's annual Petroleum Reserves data shows New Zealand's **proven-plus-probable (2P) natural gas reserves fell 23% to 731 petajoules (PJ) as at 1 January 2026** — the lowest level since recording began 20 years ago. The 217 PJ drop was made up of 108 PJ produced during the year plus 109 PJ of downward revisions to existing estimates. The largest single change was at the **Pohokura** field (down 129 PJ, partly from a well underperforming), partly offset by an upward revision at **Mangahewa**. The **Turangi** field now holds 380 PJ — about **51% of all remaining 2P reserves** (up from 42% a year earlier), concentrating supply in fewer fields. Separately, 2C contingent resources (known but not currently commercially recoverable) were 1,950 PJ, down 3%.

## Production is falling faster than forecast

- Gas **supply in 2024 was about 115.7 PJ, down ~20.9%** on 2023, mainly from natural field decline (largest drops at Maui and Pohokura).
- Gas **delivered from fields fell 22% in 2024** versus 2023; the Pohokura, Maui, Mangahewa and Kupe fields together dropped about **109 TJ/day**.
- Operators now expect **2026 production of about 85 PJ**, 15% below the profile submitted a year earlier.
- MBIE had brought forward its forecast for when annual production falls **below 100 PJ from 2029 to around 2026** — a roughly three-year acceleration on the previous outlook.

## What it means for gas users

MBIE's advice is that tighter gas supply is likely to put **upward pressure on gas prices** and **increase the risk of supply constraints**, particularly during winter peak demand, and it recommends that gas users — especially businesses reliant on gas for process heat — **start planning for alternative energy sources**. Constrained gas also reduces the fuel available for gas-fired peaking generation, contributing to electricity price and reliability pressure in dry, low-wind periods.

## ⚠ Currency note

Reserves and production figures are updated **annually** (each Petroleum Reserves release, around May) and change materially year to year. Use the latest: **731 PJ 2P as at 1 January 2026** (down from 948 PJ at 1 January 2025 and 1,300 PJ the year before). Always cite the "as at" date, and check MBIE's latest Petroleum Reserves release before quoting a figure — an older number (e.g. 948 PJ) will read as stale.
