# Reticulated natural gas vs bottled LPG

New Zealand homes and businesses use gas in two quite different forms, with very different availability.

## Reticulated (piped) natural gas

Reticulated natural gas is produced **entirely in the Taranaki region** and delivered continuously through underground **transmission and distribution pipelines**. The network covers most of the **North Island** (from Northland and Auckland down to Wellington, and across to Gisborne/Napier). The **South Island has no reticulated natural gas network**, so it is only available to North Island premises that are connected. New Zealand has historically had no facilities to import or export natural gas (all gas is used domestically), and its only large-scale gas storage is at **Ahuroa** in Taranaki, which helps balance seasonal and short-term supply.

## Bottled LPG (liquefied petroleum gas)

LPG is a **by-product separated out during gas (and oil) processing**, then compressed, bottled and **delivered by truck**. Because it isn't tied to the pipeline network, LPG can be supplied **anywhere in the country, including the South Island** and off-grid areas. It is generally **more expensive per unit of energy** than piped natural gas, and is typically used for cooking, water heating and space heating in homes without a piped connection (e.g. bottle-fed hobs and instantaneous califonts).

## Practical implication

- If a household is on **piped natural gas**, it is in the North Island, on the network, and exposed to the declining domestic gas supply and its daily fixed connection charge.
- If a household uses **LPG bottles**, it can be anywhere, but pays a higher unit price.
- Either way, EECA's running-cost comparisons generally favour electric alternatives (hot water heat pump, induction, space-heating heat pump), and disconnecting from piped gas also removes the daily fixed charge. (Those running-cost and fixed-charge figures are EECA-sourced and covered in the EECA corpus.)

## ⚠ Currency note

Network coverage and the general reticulated-vs-LPG distinction are stable, but note the recent change on imports: as part of the Energy Package the Government has moved toward **LNG imports** (a regasification facility planned at Port Taranaki), so the long-standing "no import/export facilities" position may no longer hold — confirm the current status. Relative pricing between piped gas and LPG shifts with market conditions.
