# New gas connections and the Gas Transition Plan

## Has New Zealand banned new natural gas connections to homes? — No

**There is currently no regulatory ban on new natural gas connections to homes in New Zealand.** In its 2021 draft advice, the Climate Change Commission raised the option of restricting new natural gas connections (a 2025 start date was discussed in public debate), but this **was not adopted as government policy** — it was opposed by parts of the gas industry and was not carried forward by the government elected in 2023. So a household building or renovating can still, in principle, connect to piped gas where the network is available.

Instead of a ban, the main force expected to move households and businesses off gas over time is the **declining domestic gas supply itself** (see the gas-supply-decline record) — tighter supply, rising prices and supply-security risk — rather than a regulatory prohibition.

## The direction of gas policy is mixed and evolving

Gas policy currently pulls in more than one direction, which is why this is an area to describe carefully rather than as a settled trajectory:

- On the **"keep gas going" side:** the Government has overturned the previous ban on new petroleum exploration, offered to take up to a 15% equity stake in new gas projects to encourage activity, and is progressing **LNG imports** (a regasification facility planned at Port Taranaki) to firm supply.
- On the **"manage the decline" side:** domestic production is falling faster than forecast, and the **Gas Transition Plan** work programme is planning for a lower-gas future.

## The Gas Transition Plan

The Gas Transition Plan is a work programme led by MBIE (with input from the Gas Industry Company) examining how New Zealand manages the phase-down of fossil gas as domestic supply declines. It considers options for **repurposing existing gas infrastructure for renewable gases** such as biogas and hydrogen, and how to manage the transition fairly for existing users (households and industry) — including the risk that, as demand falls, the pipeline network becomes uneconomic to maintain for those who remain.

## ⚠ Currency / hedging note

This is a **politically sensitive and evolving** area and a common point of public confusion. State clearly that there is **no current ban** on new home gas connections (the 2021 proposal was never adopted), and present the policy direction as **mixed** — the Government is simultaneously trying to shore up gas supply (exploration, equity stakes, LNG imports) and planning for its decline (Gas Transition Plan). Re-verify the current status before treating any part as settled, and avoid stating either "gas is being banned" or "gas has a secure long-term future" as fact.
