# Future electricity demand and generation (MBIE scenarios)

Electrification of transport, home and water heating and industrial process heat is expected to drive substantial growth in electricity demand over the coming decades. MBIE's Electricity Demand and Generation Scenarios (EDGS, July 2024) model this.

## Demand growth to 2050

- Reference-case electricity demand reaches about **62.1 TWh by 2050 — a 56.6% increase on 2023**.
- Across scenarios, plausible growth ranges from **35.3% to 82.0%**.
- Electricity is projected to meet **around half of New Zealand's total energy demand by 2050**, up from about **26% in 2023** — reflecting the shift of transport, heating and industry off fossil fuels onto electricity.

## New generation to meet it

Most new generation capacity is projected to come from **onshore wind and solar farms** (the lowest-cost new-build technologies), with some additional geothermal and small-scale hydro. **Gas peaking plants are retained for short-term "firming"** when wind, solar and hydro output is low. Renewable generation's overall share is projected to reach roughly **96–98% by 2050** across most scenarios (with about 92% renewable projected by 2035).

## Why this matters for the grid

Meeting this demand requires a large, sustained build-out of renewable generation and transmission — the rationale behind consenting reform (Electrify NZ) and the focus on managing dry-year reliability as the system becomes more weather-dependent.

## ⚠ Currency note

These are scenarios from the July 2024 EDGS, not forecasts. The Government Policy Statement expects MBIE to update the EDGS at least every three years, so check for a newer edition before relying on specific TWh or percentage figures. Ranges (not point values) are the appropriate way to express long-run projections.
