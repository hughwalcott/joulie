# Electricity Demand and Generation Scenarios (EDGS)

**EDGS** is MBIE's independent set of scenarios exploring **potential future electricity demand and the generation capacity needed to meet it, out to 2050**. It's the government's authoritative modelling of "how much more electricity will we need, and how might it be generated?" — the official counterpart to the demand projections in the advocacy corpora.

## Why EDGS matters (it links the three regulators)

EDGS is used by the **Commerce Commission** to assess **Transpower's** proposals for future capital investment in the transmission grid. It has an explicit role in the **investment test** for approving Transpower's proposals under the Transpower Capital Expenditure Input Methodology (Capex IM), part of the Commission's individual price-quality path regulation under Subpart 7 of Part 4 of the Commerce Act 1986. So EDGS (MBIE) → grid investment decisions (Commerce Commission) → the transmission charges on your bill — connecting the statistics, the network regulator, and the grid operator.

## What the scenarios explore

The **2024 release** (published July 2024, with data files updated into 2025) models a range of hypothetical futures across demographic, economic, policy and technology dimensions, to investigate key uncertainties such as: the **type and location** of new generation; **technology costs** (existing and emerging); **resource availability and cost** (particularly natural gas); and the shape of **future demand** (including the electrification of process heat and road transport).

## Selected insights from the modelling

- Across all scenarios, roughly **2,700 MW of existing generation is retired by 2050**, including all remaining large **coal-fired and baseload gas** plant as it reaches end of life.
- The **renewable share of electricity generation rises** over time, as wind, solar and batteries are added.
- **Battery energy storage (BESS)** grows substantially — on the order of **733 MW by 2029**, rising toward ~**1,700 MW by 2049** in the higher-electrification scenarios.
- **Total energy-sector emissions fall significantly**, reaching around **17 Mt CO₂-e by 2050** in the Reference scenario — driven largely by falling transport emissions and fuel switching in industry and commerce.
- In some scenarios, the **Huntly Rankine units are refitted for biomass** rather than decommissioned.

MBIE aims to refresh EDGS roughly every three years, and notes some assumptions date quickly as the sector changes. Because these are **scenarios, not forecasts**, Joulie should present EDGS numbers as conditional on assumptions.
