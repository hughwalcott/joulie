# Energy hardship (definition, measures and reporting)

MBIE provides the government's data and insights on **energy hardship** — at a national level and across key demographic groups — and is the body that **defined the term** for New Zealand.

## The definition

In **June 2022**, MBIE released New Zealand's **first nationally accepted definition** of energy hardship and energy wellbeing (previously there was no agreed definition or way to measure it):

> Energy hardship is when individuals, households and whānau are **not able to obtain adequate energy services to support their wellbeing** in their home or kāinga.

## Measures and reporting

- In **June 2023**, MBIE published an initial list of **five measures** of energy hardship — setting out what data should be gathered to measure and monitor it — alongside its **first report**, covering the period **1 July 2012 to 30 June 2022**.
- MBIE now reports on these measures **annually**, tracking hardship nationally and among key groups.

## Where it fits with the other sources

This is the **policy and measurement layer** that sits above the consumer-protection rules Joulie already knows about: the **Electricity Authority's Consumer Care Obligations** (which restrict disconnections and require support for customers in difficulty) and the **Commerce Commission's** work on the affordability impact of lines-charge increases. Together they give Joulie: the definition and national data (MBIE), the binding retailer obligations (EA), and the price-regulation context (ComCom). For an individual struggling with bills, the practical advice remains: contact your retailer early, check for a cheaper plan (Billy), and see the EA "finding it hard to pay" guidance.
