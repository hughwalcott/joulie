# Renewables statistics (renewable energy resources)

This is MBIE's data on New Zealand's renewable resources: **hydro, wind, geothermal, solar, woody biomass, biogas and liquid biofuels**. The renewable share of **total primary energy supply reached a record 45.5% in 2024**, and total renewable energy supply reached about **379 PJ**.

## The resource picture

- **Hydro** — generates electricity from plants across both islands; the largest by capacity are in the South Island. It's the biggest single electricity source but varies year to year with rainfall and inflows.
- **Geothermal** — concentrated in the **Taupō Volcanic Zone** (plus the Ngāwhā field in Northland), run as **baseload**. It's the **largest contributor to renewable primary energy supply**, but its ~15% conversion efficiency to electricity means it supplies **less than a fifth** of electricity.
- **Wind** — the largest wind farms are in the North Island; capacity has been growing quickly (e.g. Kaiwera Downs, Harapaki, Turitea).
- **Solar** — a newer source, historically **distributed** (rooftop/on-site), now joined by the first **utility-scale** farm (Kohirā, 2023). Fast-growing but still a small share.
- **Woody biomass, biogas and liquid biofuels** — used for electricity and, importantly, **direct heat**.

## Renewables aren't only electricity

A key point for the electrification story: renewable energy is also used for **direct heat applications** — milk-powder drying, paper making, commercial space heating, and even Rotorua's heated pools — not just wind/solar/hydro electricity. **Renewable generation capacity grew 556 MW in 2024** (up 17%, or 1,262 MW, since 2020), and increased renewable supply combined with falling non-renewable production pushed the renewable share to its record high.
