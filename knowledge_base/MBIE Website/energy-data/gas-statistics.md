# Gas statistics (natural gas supply and demand)

New Zealand's **natural gas fields are concentrated around and off the coast of the Taranaki region**. Natural gas is mainly methane, extracted much like crude oil, and is used in homes, in industry (for process heat and as a **non-energy chemical feedstock** in methanol production), and for electricity generation.

## The supply picture — a declining resource

- **Natural gas consumption fell to its lowest level since 2011** (2024 data), and total gas **production has been declining** with ongoing **field depletion** and lower supply.
- The decline of major fields — notably **Pohokura**, historically one of the country's largest — has repeatedly reduced the gas available for electricity generation.
- When gas supply is short **at the same time as low hydro** (a "dry year"), the system leans on **coal** and other thermal generation to keep the lights on. In 2024, reduced gas supply meant relatively unchanged gas-fired generation but a sharp **rise in coal** to meet demand.

## Why this matters for home electrification

This is the **authoritative, factual counterpart** to the advocacy framing in Rewiring Aotearoa's "A bad case of gas" explainer. The MBIE data confirms the underlying trend — falling domestic gas production and consumption — without the advocacy conclusions. For a household, the practical implication that both sources agree on is that **gas is a declining, increasingly constrained resource**, which is part of the context for decisions about whether to install or replace gas appliances. Joulie should present the supply/demand trend as MBIE statistics, and keep any predictions about future prices or the pace of decline attributed to whoever is making them.
