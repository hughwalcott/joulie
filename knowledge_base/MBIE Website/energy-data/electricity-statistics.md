# Electricity statistics (generation mix and demand)

New Zealand's electricity is mostly generated from **renewable sources** (hydro and geothermal), supplemented by thermal "peaker" plants when demand is high or during dry periods when hydro storage is low. Non-renewables (coal, gas, some oil/diesel) typically supply around **a fifth** of annual generation.

## Generation and the renewable mix

- **2023:** the renewable share of generation reached **88.1% — the highest since 1981** — with total generation steady at **43,488 GWh**. Hydro hit its highest since 2004 (heavy catchment rainfall); wind rose 13% to **3,206 GWh** (new Kaiwera Downs Stage 1 and Harapaki farms); solar rose 30.3% to **367 GWh**; geothermal eased 3.4% to **7,758 GWh**.
- **First utility-scale solar:** the **Kohirā solar farm** near Kaitaia was commissioned in November 2023 — New Zealand's first utility-scale solar farm — with most solar growth still coming from **distributed installations** (like household rooftop panels).
- **2024:** generation rose 0.9% to **43,879 GWh**, but the renewable share fell to **85.5%** as **hydro dropped to 23,490 GWh** (lowest since 2013) and **coal generation rose 118% to 2,243 GWh**; renewable capacity grew 556 MW.
- **Geothermal efficiency:** geothermal is the largest single contributor to renewable *primary energy supply*, but because geothermal fluid is much cooler than combustion steam its conversion efficiency to electricity is only ~15%, so it supplies **less than a fifth of NZ's electricity** despite being over half of renewable supply.

## Demand by sector

Roughly **a third of electricity goes to households, a third to industry** (mostly basic metals, food processing, wood/pulp/paper), and about **a quarter to commercial** users. Industrial electricity use has been declining (e.g. wood processing fell 32.8% in 2023). The **Tiwai Point smelter** is the largest single user.

## Electrification is visible in the data

**Transport electricity consumption grew 42.4% in 2023 to 275 GWh**, driven by EV uptake: there were **76,506 battery electric vehicles** in the fleet at the end of 2023, up from **29,292 at the end of 2021** (fleet data: Ministry of Transport). This is the official statistical signal of the household electrification the other corpora advocate for.
