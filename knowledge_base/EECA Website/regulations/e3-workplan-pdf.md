# E3 Workplan (2026)

The E3 Workplan is the annual tool that communicates the discrete projects the trans-Tasman Equipment Energy Efficiency (E3) Program is progressing each year to deliver the E3 Strategic Plan and Implementation Plan. It is the third of the three foundational strategic documents developed by E3 jurisdictions in 2024–25, is updated each year after budget decisions, and lists the priority projects committed to by the governing committees (the Energy Efficiency Working Group, EEWG, and the Energy and Climate Change Senior Officials Group, ECSOG) and resourced through budget decision-making, with expected delivery timeframes for each stage.

The 2026 Workplan was led by the Australian Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water (DCCEEW) and approved by **ECSOG**. From 2027 onwards (subject to ECMC agreement), Workplans will be approved by the EEWG.

## Project categories and stages

**Product projects** (regulating a specific appliance or equipment) move through six stages: (1) Investigation — industry, market and international research and technical standards; (2) Consultation RIS (CRIS) — a Regulation Impact Statement published for public comment; (3) Decision RIS (DRIS) — finalised and provided to the Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council (ECMC) for decision; (4) Determination (Australia) / Regulation (New Zealand); (5) Implementation; and (6) Post-implementation review.

The Australian and New Zealand processes **diverge at stage 4**: in Australia the Determination is signed by the responsible Minister and published in the Federal Register of Legislation; in New Zealand, government approval is required to draft the Regulation from an ECMC-approved DRIS, which is then finalised and published. Timings from "Determination" onwards apply to Australia only — New Zealand has a separate national regulation-making process, and **no new regulatory update is envisaged before 2027**.

**Cross-cutting projects** (not tied to a specific product — e.g. overarching reform) move through four stages: Investigation, Consultation, Commitment, and Implementation.
