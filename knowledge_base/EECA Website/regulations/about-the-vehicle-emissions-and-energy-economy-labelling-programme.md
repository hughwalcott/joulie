# About the Vehicle Emissions and Energy Economy Labelling Programme

The Vehicle Emissions and Energy Economy Labelling (VEEEL) programme improves vehicle efficiency in New Zealand through mandatory labelling, letting consumers compare vehicles on carbon dioxide emissions and energy economy alongside purchase price.

## What the label shows

- **Vehicle type** — petrol, diesel, electric, hybrid or plug-in hybrid.
- **Energy economy star rating** — up to 6 stars for the most fuel-efficient vehicles.
- **CO₂ emissions star rating** — up to 6 stars for the lowest-emission vehicles.
- **CO₂ emissions** in grams per km (tailpipe).
- **Fuel economy** in litres per 100 km (petrol and diesel).
- **Electricity economy** in kWh per 100 km (electric and plug-in hybrid).
- **Estimated yearly running costs** — based on a stated energy price and driving 14,000 km/year, including road user charges for diesel, electric and plug-in hybrids.

There is one star-rating scale for all vehicles: six stars for the most efficient, fewest stars for the highest CO₂ emitters and biggest fuel users. Ratings are derived from test results.

## How vehicles are tested

Vehicles are assessed using the Worldwide Harmonised Light Vehicles Testing Procedure (WLTP), an international standard for energy economy, emissions and EV range. The figures don't account for driving style, traffic or other real-world variables, so they provide a standardised way to compare vehicles but aren't a guarantee of what any individual driver will achieve. Data is provided to NZ Transport Agency (Waka Kotahi) and loaded into the Motor Vehicle Register and the Fuelsaver database, from which EECA's Emissions and Energy Economy Label Generator draws.

## Energy prices used on labels

The electricity price used for EVs' cost-per-year is an off-peak rate of **$0.15/kWh**; actual cost varies by plan and off-peak home charging (many retailers offer EV-specific plans with cheaper night rates). Charging at home is the most cost-effective option — over **80%** of EV charging is done at home, almost always overnight at off-peak rates. Prices on labels are representative, for comparison only, and reviewed every 12 months, with traders notified of updates in advance.

## Data availability

CO₂ emissions and energy economy data exists for most cars made after 2000 and some from 1992–1999. If data isn't available for a vehicle, displaying a label isn't compulsory (a label noting the information was unavailable can be shown instead). The data reflects the vehicle tested new under laboratory conditions and doesn't account for modifications or wear.

## Who uses it

Consumers compare efficiency, emissions and running costs; importers and manufacturers use it to promote their vehicles; and motor vehicle traders use it to help customers choose lower-emission, lower-running-cost vehicles.
