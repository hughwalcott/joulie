# E3 Implementation Plan (2025)

The E3 Implementation Plan sets out *how* the trans-Tasman Equipment Energy Efficiency (E3) Program will deliver the E3 Strategic Plan's vision, outcomes and impacts through to 2035. It is one of three foundational documents developed by E3 Program jurisdictions in 2024–25 — the **Strategic Plan** (10-year direction), the **Implementation Plan** (focus areas and actions; reviewed every two years), and the **Workplan** (annual projects). The Implementation Plan was led by EECA on behalf of the E3 Program and approved by the Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council (ECMC). It is structured as Part A (seven focus areas) and Part B (three enablers).

## Part A — Focus areas

The seven focus areas direct the program at the project level (reflected in each annual Workplan); they are not mutually exclusive and are applied in a coordinated way, each with recommended actions.

1. **Continued focus on significant energy efficiency opportunities** — energy efficiency has been the program's core for 20+ years; both countries have pledged to double the annual rate of energy efficiency improvement by 2030. A continuous process screens the market and reviews where regulatory intervention (under Australia's GEMS Act and NZ's EEC Act) could seize opportunities, pursuing statutory amendments where settings are limiting.
2. **Expansion to strategically important electrical appliances** — as the grid decarbonises and electrification grows, electricity use and system pressure rise; the program will focus on regulating high-energy-use electrical products to deliver consumer savings and system stability.
3. **Expansion to commercial and industrial sectors** — as of 2025 there are 20 product regulations in NZ and 21 in Australia, mostly residential; an IEA (2021) figure notes non-transport, non-residential use accounts for 45% of total energy use, so there is large untapped potential in shops, offices, industrial premises, hotels, schools and hospitals. Substantial legislative change (in Australia) is needed to expand here.
4. **Technology-neutral, system-level approach to regulation** — following the EU and US, shifting from individual-product to whole-system regulation (e.g. cross-fuel labelling for all hot water technologies, and a lighting-systems project), while managing interactions with schemes like NatHERS, NABERS and the NZ Building Code.
5. **Demand-flexibility-capable products** — demand flexibility (DF) is two-way communication between smart devices and flexibility service providers, enabling load-shifting and renewable optimisation with consumer control; the program will scope how to regulate DF functionality (e.g. for air conditioners and EV supply equipment), aligning with Australia's National Consumer Energy Resources (CER) Roadmap and pursuing GEMS/EEC Act amendments.
6. **Future-proofing innovative technologies** — keeping regulation abreast of fast-moving technology so new products don't fall through loopholes, partly via technology-neutral approaches.
7. **Empowering consumers** — behavioural change toward efficient products is the ultimate goal; the program will modernise the Energy Rating Label, expand online labelling as soon as possible, and align internationally, while noting complementary non-regulatory tools (rebates, incentives, EnergyRating.gov.au).

## Part B — Enablers

1. **Collaboration and governance** — the program operationalises the Trans-Tasman Mutual Recognition Agreement (1997), is given effect by inter-governmental agreements, and is underpinned by the GEMS Act (Australia) and EEC Act (NZ). This enabler defines principles for **selecting** Workplan projects (alignment, collective value across jurisdictions, feasibility, material impact, costs/benefits, amenability to regulation, alternatives to regulation), **prioritising** them (urgency, authorising environment, delivery timeframe, implementation readiness, resource balancing, potential benefits), and **tracking** progress and budget through defined roles for the Project Lead, the E3 Chair/Secretariat, and the E3 Steering Committee.
2. **Integrity** — enhancing trans-Tasman cooperation between the two regulators (GEMS Regulator and EECA), identifying compliance challenges that should inform policy, and undertaking compliance monitoring and follow-up.
3. **Communication** — an additional enabler with three objectives: amplify the program's impact, target behavioural change, and learn and share international best practice (including via the IEA and APEC). It emphasises escalating success stories to secure resourcing and filling data gaps (notably Australian sales data).

## Governance hierarchy

Decision-making ascends through the E3 Steering Committee → Energy Efficiency Working Group (EEWG) → Energy and Climate Change Senior Officials Group (ECSOG) → Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council (ECMC). The Implementation Plan is reviewed every two years, with the EEWG deciding whether revisions are needed and the ECMC agreeing them.
