# How to comply with Vehicle Energy Economy Labelling regulations

Sellers of vehicles in New Zealand must display Vehicle Emissions and Energy Economy Label information under the Energy Efficiency (Vehicle Fuel Economy Labelling) Regulations 2007 and the Energy Efficiency (Vehicle Energy Economy Labelling) Amendment Regulations 2022. Obligations apply to motor vehicle traders, new vehicle distributors, and private sellers advertising online.

## Find the label information

Use EECA's Vehicle Emissions and Energy Economy Label Generator — entering the VIN, registration plate, chassis number, model code, or Japanese export certificate model code — to find the information or print a label. On some trading websites the information appears automatically when you enter the number plate.

## If the information is available

- **On the vehicle** (traders and distributors): display the label so it's clear, prominent and obviously relates to the vehicle for sale — easy to read from a reasonable distance, and either firmly attached and visible from outside, or on an information stand next to the vehicle. The label can be removed once the vehicle is sold (a "sold" sign avoids confusion).
- **Online**: display the vehicle make and model, emissions, and energy economy as written text alongside the listing (from the first time of listing).

## If the information isn't available

You've met your legal obligation. Printing and displaying the "Not Available" label from the Label Generator shows buyers and EECA's compliance team that a genuine effort was made to obtain a label.

## Exemptions

You don't need to provide the information if you're selling a vehicle to another motor vehicle trader, or advertising in a magazine or newspaper.

## Who counts as a motor vehicle trader

A motor vehicle trader is anyone identified as such under sections 7 and 8 of the Motor Vehicle Sales Act 2003 who carries on a motor-vehicle trading business. You're also treated as a trader if you sell more than six vehicles in a 12-month period, or import more than three in 12 months, unless you can prove it's not for financial gain. Registered traders must also display a Consumer Information Notice (CIN) on used vehicles sold to the public.

## Penalty

Failure to comply can result in a penalty of up to **$5,000 per offence** (per vehicle).
