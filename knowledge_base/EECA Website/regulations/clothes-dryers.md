# Energy Rating Label for clothes dryers

Clothes dryers must have an Energy Rating Label supplied with them and displayed on the product in stores. The label shows how much energy a dryer uses per year and gives a star rating for comparing energy efficiency against other dryers. Clothes dryers carry both Minimum Energy Performance Standards (MEPS) and Mandatory Energy Performance Labelling (MEPL) requirements.

## How the rating is calculated

The annual energy consumption and star rating are calculated using the manufacturer's recommended settings to dry a damp load at the dryer's rated capacity, assuming it's used once a week and on standby the rest of the time. The rated load capacity on the label is the **dry** weight of clothing (wet weight is about 1.85–1.9 times the dry weight, depending on how much moisture a washing machine's spin cycle removed). Star ratings are generally between **1 and 10 stars**, with roughly a **15% reduction in energy consumption for each additional star**.

## Old vs new label

Australia has introduced an updated Energy Rating Label that's easier to read and consistent with other products (and displays program time), but New Zealand does not require this change — you may see either the old or new label on products supplied here. The calculations behind the old and new labels are closely aligned, so star ratings and energy consumption remain comparable.

## Regulatory notes

MEPL covers mains-electric clothes dryers for household and similar (residential) use — including vented dryers, condenser dryers (including heat pump dryers), and the drying function of combination washer/dryer units. The MEPS requirement is a maximum energy consumption relative to the mass of moisture removed from a test load (the Tested Energy Performance, TEP), which must be **≤ 1.36 kWh/kg**. Amendments to the NZ Energy Efficiency (Energy Using Products) Regulations 2002 come into force on **1 May 2026**, aligning with Australia's GEMS (Rotary Clothes Dryers) Determination and making relatively minor updates to testing, labelling and a low-level MEPS. For registration, New Zealand accepts test reports to AS/NZS 2442.1:1996 (with amendments) and AS/NZS 2442.2:2000 (with amendments). Combination washer/dryers must comply with the requirements for both clothes dryers and clothes washers. Importers/manufacturers must provide annual sales data. Queries: star@eeca.govt.nz
