# Equipment Energy Efficiency Programme (E3)

Under the Equipment Energy Efficiency (E3) programme, New Zealand and Australia jointly regulate the energy efficiency of products sold in both countries. The regulations cover products for home, commercial and industrial use.

## Two regulatory measures: MEPS and MEPL

E3 develops two measures, both reviewed regularly to keep pace with technology and raise energy performance:

- **Minimum Energy Performance Standards (MEPS):** products must meet minimum energy efficiency standards in order to be sold in New Zealand.
- **Mandatory Energy Performance Labelling (MEPL):** the Energy Rating Label helps consumers compare the energy efficiency and running costs of different products when deciding what to buy.

## Trans-Tasman strategy

The E3 Strategic Plan was developed collaboratively by the New Zealand and Australian state and territory governments through the E3 Programme. It sets a long-term vision (to 2035) for improving the energy performance of appliances and equipment, delivering practical benefits for households, businesses and the wider energy system while reducing greenhouse gas emissions.

The Strategy focuses on three key outcomes:

- improved energy efficiency for household, commercial and industrial products;
- empowered consumers, through clearer and more accessible energy information;
- flexible energy use — ensuring smart appliances and equipment capable of shifting their energy use are available to consumers.

## Trans-Tasman collaboration and emissions

New Zealand participates in E3 through a trans-Tasman Policy Framework and Funding Arrangement, which provides an agreed collaborative work practice among the energy efficiency regulators of Australia and New Zealand for the implementation, compliance and enforcement of MEPS, MEPL and other agreed projects. Collaborating with Australia shares the cost of regulation and makes compliance easier and cheaper for businesses trading in both countries. Efficient products also produce fewer carbon emissions.

## EECA's role

EECA develops and monitors compliance with the regulations, and helps importers, manufacturers and retailers understand and fulfil their legal obligations. EECA also tracks the energy savings delivered by MEPS and MEPL (see EECA's E3 programme sales and efficiency data insights).

## Legal basis

Legal requirements are set out in the Energy Efficiency (Energy Using Products) Regulations 2002, made under the Energy Efficiency and Conservation Act 2000 (http://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2000/0014/latest/DLM54948.html). EECA is currently reviewing the regulations to ensure they remain fit for purpose and will consult stakeholders on any amendments (see: Regulatory requirements under review).

## Information collection

Under the regulations, EECA collects information for sales data, compliance and enforcement purposes — including import data for regulated products and their importers/manufacturers, disclosed under EECA's agreement with the New Zealand Customs Service.
