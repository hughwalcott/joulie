# Best practice guide — residential solar PV and battery storage

This best-practice guidance, published as a Publicly Available Specification (PAS) developed by Standards New Zealand with EECA, industry and other experts, helps New Zealanders get residential solar PV and battery storage right. It helps homeowners and installers select a system aligned to a household's specific needs, understand the technology and how to participate in the electricity market, and — when used with a home energy management system (HEMS) — understand how solar can support demand flexibility.

## Benefits of getting it right

A well-chosen system can save money on power bills and buffer against rising electricity prices (by drawing less from the grid), reduce grid reliance and provide backup power during outages or extreme weather (if it has battery storage), reduce the need for electricity infrastructure upgrades, and help the environment through increased use of renewable energy.

To get the most out of a system, the PAS suggests considering products that: include a battery (to store and use more of your own solar power, especially in the evening); are grid-connected (so you can export excess and still draw from the grid when needed); and support smart energy use (e.g. working with timers for other products).

## How to approach a purchase

- **Understand your needs** — determine whether rooftop solar is right for you and what size and type of system you'd need.
- **Do your research** — look for products that meet the technical specifications (outlined in Appendix B of the PAS).
- **Plan for the future** — consider your future electricity usage and discuss potential upgrades with your installer from the start.

Begin with the residential solar PV system purchasing checklist, which guides you through the key things to consider before choosing and installing a system, and the questions to discuss with your installer.

## Installation note

The PAS states that for all grid-tied systems, both the installing electrician and the inspector are required to have an additional Electrical Workers Registration Board (EWRB) licence endorsement for mains parallel generation systems.
