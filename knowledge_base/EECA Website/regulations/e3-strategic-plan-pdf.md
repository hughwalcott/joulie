# E3 Strategic Plan (2025)

The E3 Strategic Plan sets a 10-year direction (to 2035) for the trans-Tasman Equipment Energy Efficiency (E3) Program, developed collaboratively by the Australian, state, territory and New Zealand governments and approved by the Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council in December 2025. It is one of three foundational documents in the E3 Strategy package: the **Strategic Plan** (vision, outcomes and enablers, reviewed at the five-year mark), the **Implementation Plan** (how priorities will be delivered; reviewed every two years), and the **Workplan** (the discrete projects progressed each year; updated annually).

## Background

The E3 Program is a multi-jurisdictional forum established in 1992 for collaboration on appliance and equipment energy efficiency initiatives and regulation, comprising the Australian Government, Australian states and territories (excluding the Northern Territory), and New Zealand. It is governed by inter-governmental agreements and underpinned by national legislation: the Greenhouse and Energy Minimum Standards Act 2012 (GEMS Act) in Australia and the Energy Efficiency and Conservation Act 2000 (EEC Act) in New Zealand, alongside the Trans-Tasman Mutual Recognition Agreement 1997. The respective regulators are the GEMS Regulator (Australia) and EECA (New Zealand).

The program's two key regulatory tools are minimum energy performance standards (MEPS) and mandatory energy performance labelling (MEPL, e.g. the Energy Rating Label). The plan reports that the E3 Program saved consumers an estimated A$12–18 billion in Australia (2011–2022) and NZ$2.6 billion in New Zealand (2002–2024), while preventing the trans-Tasman market from becoming a dumping ground for poor-performing products.

## Vision and mission

The stated vision is that by 2035 the E3 Program will manage energy performance outcomes that keep pace with rapid technological and market change globally, leverage complementary initiatives, and remain fit for purpose against emerging energy trends. Its mission is to ensure households and businesses in both countries have access to energy-efficient appliances and equipment, sufficient information to make informed purchasing decisions, and access to appliances capable of demand flexibility.

## Context: an evolving energy system

Both countries are shifting toward renewable electricity generation (Australia targeting 82% renewable generation by 2030; New Zealand aiming to double renewable energy generation by 2050). The plan positions appliance efficiency as making the net-zero transition easier and cheaper, notes the IEA's assessment of demand-side energy reduction as the most effective route to consumer cost savings, and highlights the growing importance of peak-demand reduction, load-shifting and demand flexibility as renewable generation grows. It describes the program as being at an inflection point: continuing its established cost-saving/efficiency/emissions objectives while broadening to support smart, interoperable appliances across the energy system.

## Three strategic outcomes

1. **Energy efficiency** — continue raising MEPS for significant energy-consuming products with the largest savings impact; with legislative amendments, expand focus to commercial and industrial sectors. Supports both countries' commitment (made at COP28) to the global target of doubling the rate of energy efficiency improvement by 2030.
2. **Consumer dynamics** — inform and empower consumers via clear, accessible energy information; maintain, expand and reform the Energy Rating Label, ensuring labels apply to appropriate products and appear on all sales platforms in line with international best practice; provide comprehensive information (efficiency, technology type, fuel source) including at point of sale.
3. **Demand flexibility** — ensure smart appliances capable of shifting energy use are widely available and adopted; align with Australia's National Consumer Energy Resources (CER) Roadmap and with New Zealand's recently integrated demand-flexibility regulatory capabilities under the Energy Efficiency and Conservation regime. Comprehensive regulation of demand-flexible capability requires legislative change to both the GEMS Act and the EEC Act.

## Enablers

- **Working together towards mutual interests:** the program acts as a collaborative forum for jurisdictions to exchange insights, align trans-Tasman regulation, and support complementary jurisdictional programs (e.g. whitelists and incentive schemes).
- **Integrity of the E3 Program:** the Australian and NZ regulators align approaches, share best practice, manage risk, and maintain rigorous compliance and enforcement activity.

## Dependencies

1. **Statutory settings:** the plan cannot be fully realised without timely amendments to the GEMS and EEC Acts and increased regulatory resourcing; ideally within one to three years. The next formal GEMS Act review opportunity is 2027; EEC Act amendments were envisaged to be introduced within about a year of publication.
2. **Authorising environments:** political contexts across jurisdictions influence decarbonisation and regulatory ambition.
3. **Jurisdictional priorities:** external events can shift priorities and delivery pace.
4. **Jurisdictional programs:** local whitelists and incentive schemes interact with the E3 Program and require close alignment.

## Governance and review

The Strategic Plan is reviewed at the five-year mark by the E3 Steering Committee, with findings agreed by the Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council (ECMC) via the Energy Efficiency Working Group (EEWG) and the Energy and Climate Change Senior Officials Group (ECSOG).
