# About energy rating labels

The Energy Rating Label helps people compare the energy efficiency and running costs of similar appliances when deciding which to buy.

## What the label is and how it's regulated

The Energy Rating Label applies to certain products under the **Energy Efficiency (Energy Using Products) Regulations 2002**, and those products must display the label when offered for sale. EECA administers label compliance in New Zealand; the same label also applies in Australia, having been developed under the trans-Tasman Equipment Energy Efficiency (E3) programme.

The label features:

- A **star rating** showing efficiency at a glance (more stars = more efficient than a similar-sized product).
- The product's **annual energy consumption** in kilowatt-hours (kWh) per year, based on assumed usage and standardised tests.
- The product's **Test Standard**. Products are tested to the standards cited in the Regulations, and the star rating and annual consumption are derived from those test results.

## How consumers and businesses use it

Consumers compare the efficiency and running costs of similar appliances before buying; importers and manufacturers use the information to promote their products' efficiency and running costs; and retailers use it to help customers choose more efficient products with lower running costs.

## Product-specific label pages

EECA publishes label guidance for specific product categories: fridges and freezers, heat pumps and air conditioners, televisions and monitors, clothes washers, and clothes dryers.
