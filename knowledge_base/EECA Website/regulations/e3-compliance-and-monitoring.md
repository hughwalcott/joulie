# Compliance and monitoring (Equipment Energy Efficiency / E3)

Manufacturers, importers and retailers all have a duty to comply with the product Regulations. EECA takes a consistent, risk-based approach, where "risk" means the level of harm that would result from a breach and the likelihood of it occurring.

## How EECA encourages and enforces compliance

- **Education and support** — presentations and newsletters to industry, and consumer programmes, to help everyone understand the Act; results and responses to compliance-monitoring activities, offences and contraventions are published.
- **Assisted compliance** — monitoring such as labelling surveys and site visits, plus product check-testing.
- **Proactive compliance** — responding to those who opportunistically or deliberately contravene the legislation.
- **Responsive interventions** — proportionate sanctions, warnings or directions.
- **Enforcement** — failure to comply can result in a fine of up to **$10,000 per offence** (per product).

## Product check-testing

Testing failures in the second round of testing can lead to compliance or enforcement action against the manufacturer or importer. This can include a formal warning to rectify, withdrawal of the product from the market, reparation to consumers, prosecution, and fines of up to **$10,000 per product**. Testing pass and fail results are published on EECA's website.

## Use of import data

Under an information-disclosure agreement with the New Zealand Customs Service, EECA has access to import data for regulated products and their importers/manufacturers. EECA uses this data to work with importers and manufacturers on meeting their obligations under the Energy Efficiency (Energy Using Products) Regulations 2002, and to monitor compliance.
