# Make the most of your solar system

Once solar is installed, a few habits and the right power plan help you reap the full rewards and keep the system running well for years.

## Choose the right power plan

The power plan affects both what you pay for electricity and the buyback rate for excess solar you sell. Which plan is best depends on your setup:

- **Without a battery** — a flat-rate plan is often better, because you'll buy power when solar isn't generating (especially at night) and a flat rate won't spike at peak times.
- **With a battery** — a time-of-use (ToU) plan is usually best, since you can charge the battery during the day, use stored electricity after dark, and avoid buying at peak.

Check you're on the right plan with Billy (the Electricity Authority's free comparison tool): https://billy.govt.nz/

## Understand buyback rates

The buyback rate is what your power company pays for each unit of solar you export. It's lower than what you pay for mains electricity — another reason to consume your own solar whenever possible. Rates vary a lot, so shop around. **From July 2026**, large power companies must offer a fair price for electricity exported during peak (busy) hours.

## Maximise self-use

Use as much of your everyday electricity as possible while the sun is out:

- Heat water in the daytime by adding a timer or solar diverter to the electric hot water cylinder.
- Use low-cost timers or smart plugs to run appliances when solar is available.
- Switch to daytime EV charging to use your own generation.
- Avoid running many appliances at once, which draws heavily on solar and makes it more likely you'll need to buy power.

## Monitor your system

Especially early on, monitor the system (often via an app included with, or added to, the system) to see how much it's generating, using and storing, adjust your habits, and catch any maintenance needs.

## Get your home solar-ready

- If still on gas, switching to an electric heat pump and electric hot water system boosts solar savings.
- More efficient appliances make solar power go further.
- A well-insulated home needs less heating and cooling, optimising solar use.
