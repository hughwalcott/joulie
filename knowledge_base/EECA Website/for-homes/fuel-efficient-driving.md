# Fuel-efficient driving

A few simple driving tweaks and basic maintenance can make fuel go up to **20% further** — worth around $44 on every tank — while also reducing vehicle wear and tear.

## Drive smoothly and moderate speed

- Air resistance rises sharply at speed. Driving over the speed limit can increase fuel use by **10%**, and fuel economy drops quickly above 80 km/h; easing from 100 km/h to 90 km/h saves up to **7%**.
- Take your foot off the accelerator to slow gradually rather than braking hard, and avoid unnecessarily quick acceleration.
- On the highway, use cruise control to hold a steady speed.

## Reduce drag and weight

- Remove roof racks and unused bike racks when not needed — the wind drag increases fuel use (removing small roof racks can save ~2%; larger cargo boxes over 15%).
- Check for and remove heavy items carried inside the car.

## Plan trips

- Combine several errands into one trip, and drive outside peak times when congestion is worst.

## Use your car's efficiency features

- Auto stop-start can save around **4–10%** on fuel in city driving (less effective on open highways). If stopping for more than 30 seconds in a safe place, switch the engine off.
- Cruise control and, where fitted, tyre-pressure monitoring also help.

## Keep tyres properly inflated

All tyres gradually lose air, and for every **5 psi** a tyre is under-inflated, fuel use rises by about **1%**. Correct pressure also improves handling and safety.

- Find the correct pressure on a label inside the driver's door, the fuel flap, or in the handbook.
- Check when tyres are cold — after the car has been parked at least 3 hours or driven less than 2 km at low speed — and don't forget the spare.
- It's easy at a service station: enter the correct pressure into the air pump and hold the gauge on the valve until it beeps.

Regular servicing (oil and air filters, brakes, tuning, and no air/fluid/exhaust leaks) keeps a vehicle running efficiently.
