# Solar quotes and installation

As with any large purchase, get **2 or 3 quotes** so you can compare both the advice and the costs. Choose an installer who will come to your home for an onsite assessment, since they need to check the property's suitability to design the right system. A solar provider directory is maintained by SEANZ (Sustainable Electricity Association of New Zealand).

## What a good quote should include

Use a checklist so there are no hidden post-install costs. A complete quote should cover:

- Components — all cabling, inverters, controllers, surge protection, and a purpose-designed panel racking system.
- Scaffolding or edge protection (if required).
- Any necessary roof work and consent applications.
- All required inspections.
- Any distribution-upgrade requirements.
- The cost of installing an import/export meter.
- Battery storage installation (if you're getting a battery).

## Questions to ask at the onsite visit

The installer should check roof condition, space and orientation, plus shading, switchboard capacity and mains cable capacity, and discuss your budget. They should size the system to your typical annual usage, desired kWh capacity, roof limits, and future plans (e.g. an EV). Also confirm:

- **Batteries** are optional — many homes fit panels first and add a battery later; they make sense where a lot of energy is used outside daylight hours.
- **Warranties** for both workmanship and hardware (longer is better).
- **Ongoing support** for optimising and troubleshooting the system.

## Financing

Most banks offer home energy loans that can finance solar, but you need a quote before you can apply.

## The installation process

Once you accept a quote and pay a deposit, the company sets an install date; the process is usually complete in **4 weeks or less** (longer if parts must be ordered or the job is complex, e.g. needing scaffolding).

- **Consent:** building consent is no longer required to install rooftop panels on most existing homes (with some exceptions). The installer lodges a distributed generation application; for systems under **10 kW** this is usually quick and low-cost, taking about **1–2 weeks** for the network operator to approve.
- **Install:** cabling connects the panels to the inverter, which connects to the switchboard with an added circuit breaker. The installer sends a certificate of completion to you and the electrical inspector.
- **Inspection:** a registered electrical inspector checks the installation is correct and safe; if it passes you receive an Electrical Safety Certificate. The wait can be next-day to several weeks in busy periods, and the cost should be in the quote.
- **Handover:** the installer turns the system fully on and shows you the basic inverter functions.
