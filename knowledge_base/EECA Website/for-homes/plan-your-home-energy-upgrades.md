# Plan your home energy upgrades

Using more efficient appliances and technology is one of the easiest ways to keep home energy costs down — many NZ households could save **over $1,000 a year** by switching key appliances to more efficient options. When replacing older appliances, renovating, or building new, it pays to look at the whole picture of a home's energy use and target the biggest savings. Planning ahead is useful even without imminent upgrades: about 20% of Kiwi homeowners will be in the market for one of these key appliances within the year, and researching an efficient option pays off long-term.

## The five biggest levers on home energy bills

Most of a home's energy goes on transport, heating and hot water, so long-term savings come from planning efficient, end-of-life replacements in these areas. EECA's "most efficient home" scenario combines:

1. **Space heating** — an efficient heat pump.
2. **Water heating** — a hot water heat pump.
3. **Cooking** — an induction cooktop.
4. **Transport** — electric vehicle(s).
5. **Generation** — solar panels.

Cooking uses relatively little energy, but it's worth considering the *type* of energy: if every other appliance is electric, switching the cooktop to electric too can remove the daily connection charge for a second energy type (piped gas).

## What the efficient scenario looks like

Compared with a typical NZ household, a home with the most efficient appliance options uses **less than half** the energy overall and produces around **85% less** greenhouse gas emissions — because there's no direct use of fossil fuels (petrol, diesel or gas) and 80–85% of NZ electricity comes from clean sources like hydro and wind. The illustrative scenario is a 3-person household with two cars; every home differs.

## Review your power plan after big changes

A major change to how a home uses, generates or stores energy (installing solar, buying an EV) can change which power plan is cheapest. It's worth reviewing the plan whenever the energy pattern shifts. See "Check you're on the right power plan": https://www.eeca.govt.nz/for-homes/save-on-your-energy-bills/check-youre-on-the-right-power-plan/
