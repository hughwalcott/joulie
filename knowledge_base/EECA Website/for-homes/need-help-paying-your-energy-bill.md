# Need help paying your energy bill?

Both short-term support and longer-term advice are available if you're struggling to pay your power bills. Many households feel the pressure, especially over the colder months — the sections below cover where to turn, what you may be eligible for, and your rights around disconnection.

## Support from power companies

Most power companies run hardship teams or funds to help households through difficult periods, so it's best to make contact as early as possible. Providers are obligated to help: once your electricity provider knows you're finding a bill hard to pay, they must offer alternative payment options.

More at the Electricity Authority's "Your Power" site: https://www.ea.govt.nz/your-power/

## Your rights when power could be disconnected

Knowing your rights protects your household if a provider is considering disconnection, particularly where timing or medical needs raise the stakes:

- Your supplier must attempt to contact you **at least five times** before disconnecting.
- Disconnection should not happen when reconnection would be difficult — i.e. not just before a weekend or public holiday, and not early in the morning or late at night.
- Power **must not** be disconnected if someone in the household is medically dependent on electricity (needs supply at home to prevent serious harm).
- Tell your provider promptly if anyone in the household is medically dependent.

More at the Electricity Authority (Consumer Care Obligations): https://www.ea.govt.nz/your-power/consumer-care-obligations/

## Power Credits Scheme

Some power companies offer a **$110 credit** to households on low-use plans as part of a government initiative. A second credit may be available if you've spoken with a budgeting advisor. Contact your energy provider to check eligibility.

## Utilities Disputes Ltd

For a formal complaint, Utilities Disputes Ltd (UDL) provides a free, independent dispute-resolution service covering electricity, gas, telecommunications and water. Most disputes are resolved by working with the parties; where agreement isn't reached, UDL can issue a decision. https://www.udl.co.nz/
