# Get winter ready

A guide to getting a home warm, healthy and ready for winter, with solutions to suit any budget — from a 30-minute free fix to a bigger upgrade. Preparing before the cold sets in helps keep heat in, prevents moisture and mould, and gets ahead of higher power bills. The actions are grouped into quick wins, small purchases and big improvements. All dollar figures are EECA's indicative annual savings for an average NZ home (methodology at the end).

## Quick wins (free, this weekend)

Low- or no-cost actions that keep heat in and prevent dampness:

- **Clean the heat pump filter monthly** — a clogged filter forces the system to work harder and cost more; a monthly clean saves on wasted energy (around $50/year). Pop off the front, remove the filter, gently vacuum or rinse, dry, and refit.
- **Set the heat pump thermostat sensibly** — around 18–21°C for living areas; not running it hotter than needed can save about $40/year.
- **Air the home each morning** — open windows for 10–15 minutes to clear moisture; use kitchen and bathroom extractor fans.
- **Close curtains at dusk** and seal obvious draughts to hold heat in.

## Small purchases (low-cost fixes)

- **Wrap the hot water cylinder and insulate the first metre of hot water pipe** — an easy DIY job that keeps heat where it's needed and can save around $100/year.
- **Fit good curtains** — full-length, thermally lined curtains that fit well noticeably reduce heat loss through windows.
- **Draught-stopping materials** — weatherstripping, door snakes and gap seals.

## Big improvements (bigger investment)

The most effective way to prepare a home for winter and lower energy costs for years to come. Grants or home energy loans can help with the upfront cost.

- **Insulation** — one of the most effective ways to keep a home warm, healthy and comfortable; can save up to **$330/year**.
- **Double glazing** — up to 30% of a home's heating energy is lost through windows, making them the biggest source of heat loss in an otherwise well-insulated home; double glazing can cut this to 20% or less.
- **Efficient heat pump** — one of the best long-term moves for lower bills; could save the average household **$122/year**.
- **Externally ducted extractor fan** — removes moisture before it causes problems; ensure kitchen and bathroom fans are big enough, cleaned regularly, and vented outside (not into the ceiling space).

## Funding help

- **Warmer Kiwi Homes** — grants of 50–90% of the cost of ceiling and underfloor insulation, and up to 90% of the cost of an energy-efficient heater, for eligible homeowners. https://www.eeca.govt.nz/co-funding-and-support/products/warmer-kiwi-homes-programme/information/
- **Home energy loans** — many NZ banks offer low-interest finance and home-loan top-ups for energy-performance upgrades. https://www.eeca.govt.nz/for-homes/improve-your-home/understand-home-energy-loans/

*Corpus note: EECA pages disagree on the Warmer Kiwi Homes heating grant. This page and the Funding page state "up to 80% off heating," whereas the programme's own information page states a 90% heating grant (up to $3,450) for those in most need, and other pages (home page, "easy ways to save") say "up to 90%." Per project convention, the programme's own page is treated as authoritative: up to 90% for the highest-need households, not available to middle-income areas.*

## Savings methodology (as stated by EECA)

Cost-saving estimates use average household energy demand from EECA's Energy End Use Database (EEUD) with fuel prices based on 2023 MBIE data. Each action is mapped to a share of average household electricity or natural gas demand and assigned an indicative percentage reduction, from which annual savings in kWh and NZD are derived. Key assumptions: heating uses either electricity or gas, not both (wood burners excluded); average setpoints of 23°C (heating) and 65°C (hot water); insulation savings assume increasing from the current average level to the standard for newly built NZ homes. Figures are indicative for an average home and vary with occupancy, consistency, prices and climate.
