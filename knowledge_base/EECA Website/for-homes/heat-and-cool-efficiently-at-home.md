# Heat and cool efficiently at home

Getting heating and cooling right in the rooms you use most makes a big difference to comfort, health and power bills. A heat pump is usually best for larger rooms, while plug-in electric heaters suit smaller rooms and offices.

## Heating tips

- **Choose the right heater** — for a portable heater, pick a higher-wattage model with a thermostat so the room reaches a comfortable temperature quickly without wasting energy.
- **Use a timer** — warm the room just before you need it and switch off when you don't; a separate timer plug or smart plug works if the heater has no built-in timer.
- **Don't overheat** — keep the thermostat no higher than 20–21°C to stay warm and dry while saving power.
- **Placement** — put portable heaters on the cold side of the room or near a window to help distribute heat.
- **Stay safe** — keep heaters away from curtains and anything flammable, and plug only one heater per socket.

## Insulate first

Insulation makes a big difference; a well-insulated home means the heat pump or heater works less hard, lowering bills. Start with ceiling insulation, then accessible underfloor spaces, and block draughts and window heat loss.

## Keeping cool in summer

- Open doors and windows in different rooms to move air through the home (security latches let you leave windows open while out).
- Plant deciduous trees to shade the house in summer; they let sun through once leaves drop in winter.
- Install external window shades — blinds, awnings or louvres; eaves or roof overhangs above north-facing windows block summer sun.
- Use fans — tabletop, floor and ceiling fans use far less energy than air conditioning. If you have a heat pump, try the fan-only setting with windows open.
