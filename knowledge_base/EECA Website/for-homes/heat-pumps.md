# Heat pumps

A heat pump is usually the best option for heating larger rooms and living areas — it's the lowest-cost and lowest-emission space heating available in New Zealand.

## Why heat pumps

- **Highly efficient** — the average NZ heat pump puts out about **three times more heat** than the electricity it consumes.
- **Low running costs** — the lowest-cost home heating option, around **a third of the cost of gas**; lower still if solar covers some daytime heating.
- **Instant heating and cooling** — room temperature is controllable at the press of a button and can be automated with timers.
- **Low emissions** — 100% electric, so on NZ's highly renewable grid it's the lowest-emission heating option, with around **10 times lower emissions** than gas or LPG.

## How they work

A heat pump moves a refrigerant between an indoor and outdoor unit, using the temperature difference to gasify or liquify the refrigerant and produce heating or cooling.

## Types

- **Single-split** — one outdoor unit connected to one indoor unit heating a single room; easy to install and cost-effective.
- **Multi-split** — one outdoor unit connected to multiple indoor units for different rooms; good for larger homes.
- **Ducted** — whole-house central heating that blows warm air through ducts into multiple rooms; more expensive upfront.

A time-of-use power plan with cheap or free off-peak periods can suit households with electric heating.

## Installation

For best results, have the heat pump installed by a licensed professional who can advise on the best location.

## Indicative saving

A 3-person Auckland household in a moderately insulated home, heating 1–2 days a week during the cooler months, could save about **$122/year** on heating by switching from piped gas to a heat pump (not including the gas daily connection charge, which adds further savings if gas is disconnected entirely).

Calculated with EECA's Home Energy Savings Calculator for the scenario above (3-person Auckland household, moderate insulation, daytime heating 1–2 days/week in cooler months, switching from piped gas heating to a heat pump).
