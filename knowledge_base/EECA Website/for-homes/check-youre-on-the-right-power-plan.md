# Check you're on the right power plan

Making sure you're on the best plan for your household is one of the simplest ways to pay the lowest price for the energy you actually use.

## Compare power plans

Billy, run by the Electricity Authority, is a free power-comparison tool that helps you find the best plan for your home and switch providers in a few clicks. It's worth a look if your bill seems high, if your usage has changed, or if you just want to see what options are available.

Compare and switch at Billy: https://billy.govt.nz/

## Consider an off-peak power plan

Power companies offer plans that reward you for using energy during off-peak times — nights and weekends — when national demand is lower. These plans go by various names (off-peak, time-of-use, flexi, ecosaver) and can save money while helping keep the grid sustainable. They suit households that use more energy off-peak, such as charging an EV overnight or heating bedrooms while children sleep.

Such plans typically offer cheaper or discounted power in specific windows and could save up to **$150 a year** — but check the details of each offer before switching.

Electricity Authority guide to time-of-use plans: https://www.ea.govt.nz/your-power/ways-to-save-energy/how-to-get-cheaper-power-bills-with-a-time-of-use-plan/

## Shift your energy use

An off-peak plan only pays off if you actually move usage into the discounted windows. Companies may set higher peak rates to offset the cheaper off-peak periods, so a household that doesn't shift its use can end up paying *more* on a time-of-use plan than on a standard one.

## Adapt when your usage changes

Household energy use changes over time, and the right plan should change with it. Major upgrades can materially alter a home's overall energy profile and shift which plan is best value:

- **Hot water heat pumps** — high efficiency reduces the energy used to heat water. https://www.eeca.govt.nz/for-homes/energy-saving-technology/water-heating/hot-water-heat-pumps/
- **Electric vehicles** — an off-peak rate is a key lever for lowering EV running costs. https://www.eeca.govt.nz/for-homes/vehicles/benefits-of-electric-vehicles/
- **Home solar** — the best plan depends on whether the system includes a battery.
