# Easy ways to save on your energy bills

With a few smart tweaks to how a home is powered, it can stay comfortable, healthy and warm all year round while keeping bills down. All dollar figures below are EECA's indicative annual savings for an average NZ household (methodology at the end of this document).

## Save on hot water

Water heating makes up around a third of household energy bills, so smart water heating and use delivers serious savings.

- **Use a cold water wash** — could save around $30/year (based on 4 washes/week); modern machines and detergents wash effectively in cold water.
- **Use an efficient shower head** — if the shower fills a 10-litre bucket in under a minute, it's wasting water; a head that cuts flow by 1 litre/minute could save a household of four around $80/year.
- **Fix dripping taps** — dripping hot taps can cost hundreds of dollars a year depending on severity; a replacement washer costs a few dollars.
- **Fully load the dishwasher** — run only when full, use the 'eco' setting if available, and rinse dishes with cold water if pre-rinsing.

## Heating your home efficiently

Heat pumps are generally the lowest-cost, lowest-emissions option for home heating, particularly for living areas and rooms needing frequent heating or cooling. Electric resistance heaters suit smaller rooms.

- **Heat only the rooms in use** — close doors to the others and seal draughts; saves up to $350/year.
- **Clean the heat pump filter monthly in colder months** — dust build-up forces the system to work harder; could save $50/year.
- **Set the thermostat to 18–21°C** for a healthy living environment (a little higher for older people or young babies).
- **Only run heaters when needed** — running a heat pump 24/7 uses more energy than heating rooms on demand.

## Seal up draughts

Good draught proofing keeps the home's temperature stable and prevents heat escaping through unwanted gaps.

- **Seal door and window gaps** with draught stoppers, door snakes or weatherstripping — one of the cheapest ways to stay warm in a classically draughty Kiwi house.
- **Close curtains and blinds at sunset** — saves $80–90/year by retaining heat.
- **Check hard-to-reach places** — gaps around wiring and plumbing hide behind kitchen/bathroom cabinets, hot water cupboards and wardrobes.

## Use appliances wisely

Devices left on — even in standby — can collectively add up to $200/year to a power bill. Run appliances efficiently and shift use to off-peak times.

- **Switch off at the wall** — second fridges, heated towel rails, small space heaters, standby devices.
- **Use smart technology** — smart plugs and devices help capture cheaper off-peak power and avoid waste from idle appliances.
- **Install a hot water thermostat** — automatically turns the cylinder off or down when hot water isn't needed.
- **Buy efficient appliances** — more stars on the Energy Rating Label means lower running costs; could save $140/year.

## Energy rating labels

Energy Rating Labels compare the efficiency and running costs of similar appliances: the more stars, the more efficient the appliance relative to a similar product of the same size.

## Grants

Warmer Kiwi Homes covers 50–90% of the cost to purchase and install insulation, and up to 90% off an efficient heater, for eligible households. Eligibility check: https://www.eeca.govt.nz/co-funding-and-support/products/warmer-kiwi-homes-programme/check-eligibility/

## Savings methodology (as stated by EECA)

Estimates use average household energy demand from EECA's Energy End Use Database (EEUD) with fuel prices from 2023 MBIE data. Each action maps to a share of average household electricity or gas demand, with these indicative reductions of the corresponding share:

| Action | Reduction |
| --- | --- |
| Heat/cool only rooms in use | 50% |
| Turn off appliances when not in use | 33% |
| Seal draughts around windows and doors | 15% |
| Close curtains at dusk | 13% |
| Clean heat pump filter | 10% |
| Set the heat pump to 21°C maximum | 2% |
| Select a cold wash for laundry | 4% (minimum) |
| Use appliances during off-peak hours | 0% (load shifted to off-peak) |

Key assumptions: heating uses either electricity or gas, not both (wood burners excluded); average thermostat setpoints 23°C heating / 65°C hot water; cold-wash savings assume 4 cycles/week with at least 1/3 switched to cold; "appliances that can be turned off" include miscellaneous electrical equipment plus 1/3 of refrigeration, electric resistance heating, computers and TVs; "off-peak-capable" appliances include washers, dryers, dishwashers plus 1/2 of cooking, computers/TVs and miscellaneous equipment; Energy Rating Label savings assume replacing air conditioners, dryers, washing machines, dishwashers, refrigeration, TVs and monitors with equivalents rated one star higher; figures above $50 rounded to the nearest $10. EECA describes the annual figures as indicative upper limits assuming intentional, consistent application in an average NZ household; actual savings vary significantly with consistency, occupancy, electricity prices, climate and other factors.
