# Choose an efficient fridge or freezer

Because a fridge or freezer runs continuously, choosing an efficient model makes a real difference to running costs — and helps smooth national electricity peaks, reducing reliance on fossil fuels. Around 80–85% of NZ electricity comes from renewables in an average year, with the remaining 15–20% still from fossil fuels, so buying efficient both saves money and cuts emissions. These appliances are regulated through labelling and performance requirements; in the year ended March 2025, appliance regulation saved 1.48 PJ of energy (about 35,000 homes' annual use, ~$36 million national benefit).

## Reading the label

The stars show efficiency at a glance, but only indicate a model is cheaper to run than similar models with fewer stars — read them alongside the **annual energy consumption (kWh)**, where lower is cheaper regardless of size. The consumption figure assumes the appliance runs 24 hours a day at the manufacturer's recommended temperature settings.

## Configurations

- **Combined fridge/freezers** are generally more efficient than separate appliances, though efficiency varies across all types.
- **Side-by-side** models split down the middle (freezer typically left, fridge right); they're narrower but deeper, which can make items at the back harder to reach. Through-the-door ice and water dispensers use more electricity and may need a plumber.
- **Top or bottom freezer** models split horizontally; a top fridge is convenient (accessed more often), while a bottom freezer may make the crisper drawer too cold.

## When to replace and how to dispose

Consider replacing when the appliance no longer reaches the right temperature — **2–4°C** for a fridge and **−15°C to −18°C** for a freezer. Old units contain recyclable metals and extremely potent greenhouse gases, so use a recycling station that extracts the useful components and recovers the gas.
