# Solar costs and savings

Solar system prices have fallen over time, making them more affordable than ever. To make an informed decision, look at the typical costs, the savings on power bills, and how you'll finance the install.

## Typical costs

The figures below are indicative, to compare against your own quote. Systems of **3–5 kW** tend to work well financially — they produce around **50–80%** of the electricity used by an average 3-person, all-electric household (with no EV). A small system starts around **$8,500**. Adding a battery increases the upfront cost, and the amount varies with battery type and capacity; a battery isn't essential, and solar without one generally gives the best returns.

## How solar saves money

Solar can save the average NZ home **$1,000 or more per year** off the power bill, regardless of region. The more of the generated power you use yourself, the less grid electricity you pay for.

## Payback

If a household can use most of the electricity its solar generates, it typically takes **7–10 years** for power-bill savings to repay the upfront cost. Achieving that assumes average-to-high daytime electricity use plus electric heating and hot water. Payback under 7–10 years is possible in parts of the country with above-average sunshine and power prices, and fully optimising daytime power use shortens it further. Adding a battery adds upfront cost and usually lengthens the payback.

## Financing

Most major NZ banks offer **0–1%** home energy loans for upgrades like solar, usually added to a mortgage as a top-up — often repayable well before the panels' warrantied lifespan. Understand the terms and get independent advice where needed. See "Understand home energy loans": https://www.eeca.govt.nz/for-homes/improve-your-home/understand-home-energy-loans/

## Savings basis (as stated by EECA)

Cost and savings figures are based on a 5 kW medium-sized solar install in a home with electric heating and hot water and average electricity consumption (2025 calculations). Regional savings vary with solar climate and electricity prices.
