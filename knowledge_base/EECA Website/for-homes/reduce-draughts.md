# Reduce draughts

Sealing draughts is one of the simplest, lowest-cost ways to make a home warmer, drier, healthier and more energy efficient. EECA estimates that sealing draughts around windows and doors saves around NZ$100 per year for the average NZ home. The principle: keep the home as airtight as possible while maintaining controlled ventilation, so heat doesn't escape through unwanted gaps and less extra heating and cooling is needed.

## Quick wins

- **Tighten loose hinges, catches and latches.** If doors or windows don't fit their frames snugly, repairs by a qualified builder may be needed.
- **Seal skirting boards and cornices** with flexible silicone-based or latex sealants, or remove them and block gaps with expanding foam.
- **Check less obvious places** — kitchen cabinets, wardrobes and ceiling hatches — and seal with weatherstripping or silicone.
- **Add weatherstripping** around doors and windows (hardware stores can advise on products).
- **Fit draught excluders:** brush-strip or "door snake" types for internal doors; spring-loaded automatic seals for external doors.
- **Replace damaged aluminium joinery seals** — DIY with correctly sized rubber seals, or hire a professional.

## Airtightness and where draughts hide

Some draughts are obvious (poorly sealed doors, windows, floorboards); others hide around downlights, pipes and power outlets, or behind fixed cabinets, so careful inspection pays off. Airtightness is a balancing act with ventilation: keeping warm air in and letting fresh outdoor air in are both essential for a healthy, energy-efficient home (see EECA's "Reduce dampness" guidance on ventilation).

## Gaps around wiring and plumbing

Penetrations for wiring and plumbing are often hidden — check behind kitchen and bathroom cabinets, inside hot water cupboards and wardrobes.

- Use silicone sealant for smaller gaps and expanding foam for larger ones.
- Check extractor fans and rangehoods; if they have shutters, confirm they close properly.
- Check recessed downlights for draughts. They can be replaced with better-sealed units that can be safely covered with insulation — ask for IC, IC-F or IC-4 rated LED downlights.

## Design stage

Airtightness should be considered when buying or building a house; if building or renovating, draught control can be built into the design from the start (see EECA's "Design for energy efficiency" guidance).

## Savings methodology (as stated by EECA)

The ~$100/year figure uses average household energy demand from EECA's Energy End Use Database with 2023 MBIE fuel prices. Sealing draughts around windows and doors is modelled as a 15% reduction of the corresponding share of household heating energy demand. Assumptions include: heating by either electricity or gas (not both; wood burners excluded); thermostat setpoints of 23°C heating / 65°C hot water; savings ranges reflect electricity vs gas heating; figures above $50 rounded to the nearest $10.
