# Compare home heating options and costs

Heating is one of the largest energy users in the average Kiwi home — about a third of the energy bill — so choosing the right system matters. This compares heat pumps, electric heaters, and efficient wood or pellet burners.

## What to consider

- **Energy efficiency** — a more efficient system is cheaper to run over time, usually worth the higher upfront cost. Heat pumps are the most energy-efficient for space heating.
- **The right size** — too small and it struggles; too big and you pay more. For a heat pump, a good supplier should assess the room size, number of windows and local climate before advising.

## The options

- **Heat pumps** — generally the lowest cost and lowest emissions for home heating, especially in living areas and spaces needing frequent heating or cooling. Can heat one room, or many with a ducted or multi-split system.
- **Plug-in electric heaters** — a good option for bedrooms, studies and other small rooms.
- **Wood and pellet burners** — wood is a low-emissions, renewable fuel. Wood fires and pellet burners are typically cheaper to run than gas but more expensive than heat pumps. A building consent is required to install or replace a wood burner, and many councils have specific rules due to air-quality concerns.
- **Gas heaters** — run on mains natural gas (North Island only) or bottled LPG; both are fossil fuels and directly generate emissions.

## Compare specific models

Some heat pump models are much more efficient than others, affecting running costs. EECA's Efficient Appliance Calculator lets you filter and compare heat pumps sold in New Zealand by energy efficiency rating and climate zone: https://www.eeca.govt.nz/tools/efficient-appliance-calculator/
