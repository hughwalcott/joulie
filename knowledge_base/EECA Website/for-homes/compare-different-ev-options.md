# Compare different EV options

Not all electrified vehicles are the same. The three main types differ in how they're powered and charged.

## Battery electric vehicle (BEV)

A BEV runs purely on an electric motor powered by a rechargeable battery — no petrol. The most energy-efficient way to charge at home is a dedicated wall unit (a standard socket also works), and public charging stations can be used on the road. Regenerative braking recovers and stores energy when slowing or braking.

**Best if** you prioritise low running costs, travel within the battery's range most days, and can charge off-street overnight at home or at work.

## Plug-in hybrid electric vehicle (PHEV)

A PHEV has both an electric motor with a rechargeable battery and an internal combustion engine (petrol or diesel). Most drive in EV-only mode until the battery runs low, then switch automatically to the engine. The battery is charged by plugging into an external power source, and topped up by regenerative braking while driving.

## Hybrid

A hybrid combines a combustion engine with a battery or on-board electric motor and is more fuel-efficient than a comparable petrol car — but it is **not** an EV. Unlike BEVs and PHEVs, it can't be plugged in; the battery is charged by the engine and regenerative braking.

## Running and ownership costs

EVs are cheaper to run — the equivalent of about **$1.60 per litre** to "fill up" when charged at home off-peak, including road user charges. They can also work out cheaper overall to own than a fossil-fuelled equivalent, thanks to low-cost charging and lower maintenance needs.
