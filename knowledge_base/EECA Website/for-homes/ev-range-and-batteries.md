# EV range and batteries

Electric vehicles have the range needed for everyday driving. Battery range — how far you can drive on a single charge — depends on the EV type, its battery capacity, the roads (flat, hilly or winding) and driving style. For most new EVs, a **300 km** drive is within easy reach, and many owners only need to charge every few days. Find an estimated range for a specific vehicle on its Vehicle Emissions and Energy Economy Label or on the Rightcar website (https://www.rightcar.govt.nz/).

## Looking after your battery

Good care helps maintain capacity for many years:

- In hot weather, park and charge in the shade or a garage; in freezing conditions, follow the manufacturer's battery-care instructions. Some EVs have thermal management systems that regulate battery temperature.
- Frequent fast charging may reduce battery capacity over time, depending on the model and climate — check the manual or manufacturer.
- Always have an EV serviced by a qualified technician, following the manufacturer's recommendations.

## Battery life and warranties

Most new EVs come with a battery warranty for a set period (typically around **8–10 years**) or distance (such as **160,000 km**). Capacity gradually decreases with use — like a mobile phone — and can also decline while a vehicle sits unused. A battery with reduced capacity still works well and suits buyers who don't need to travel far between charges.

## Buying a used EV — check the battery

Have the battery properly checked before purchase. Battery condition is described in several ways: percentage of capacity remaining, **State of Health (SoH)**, or (for a Nissan Leaf) the number of charge bars out of 12. A data reader can perform a health check, with results sent to a smartphone.

SoH describes the battery's overall condition (not its current charge) and is a useful gauge of remaining life — often **more informative than the odometer**. For example, an EV with very low mileage may have reduced SoH if it's been stored a long time or excessively fast-charged, so a car with slightly higher mileage but better SoH can be the better buy.

## End of life

When a battery no longer provides useful range (typically after many years), dead cells can sometimes be replaced to extend its life. If a full replacement is needed, fitting a compatible higher-capacity battery may actually improve range.
