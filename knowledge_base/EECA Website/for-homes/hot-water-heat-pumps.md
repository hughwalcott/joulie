# Hot water heat pumps

Home hot water heat pumps are a highly energy-efficient water heating option worth considering when building new or replacing an old system. They're cheaper to run than traditional electric cylinders or gas, especially cost-effective for households using a lot of hot water, and produce far fewer emissions than fossil-fuel heating.

## At a glance

- **Cost:** around **$7,000** including purchase and installation (higher for the most efficient or larger systems).
- **Indicative saving:** around **$284/year** for the average household (see methodology).
- **Efficiency:** uses around **60–75% less electricity** than a conventional electric hot water system — roughly a third of the energy of an electric resistance cylinder or gas.
- **Emissions:** can save around **7,000 kg of CO₂ over 15 years** compared with gas or LPG water heaters.

## How it works

A hot water heat pump extracts heat from the surrounding air and transfers it into the water, using only a modest amount of electricity to drive the compressor and fan (rather than heating water directly with an element). Its coefficient of performance (COP) typically ranges from about **2 to 4**, versus just under 1 for a conventional electric cylinder. COP is higher in warmer climates — the same unit performs better in Auckland than in Christchurch because warmer air is easier to draw heat from.

## Two main types

- **Integrated** — heat pump and cylinder combined into a single unit installed outside; often simpler to install depending on layout and plumbing.
- **Split** — separate outdoor heat pump plus a cylinder that can go inside or outside; more flexible and sometimes retrofittable to an existing cylinder.

The heat pump component must be installed outside; the cylinder can be inside or outside.

## Trade-offs

- **Higher upfront cost** but the lowest running cost and lowest emissions over time.
- **Can be louder** — the fan and compressor make more noise than other systems, so components are usually located outside or where noise is minimised.
- **Less efficient in cold weather** — efficiency drops when ambient air is colder (winter), and some models drop more than others, so check specifications before buying.

Because the technology is relatively new, it's worth shopping around for a plumber and electrician trained and experienced in installing them.

## Savings methodology (as stated by EECA)

The ~$284/year saving was calculated with EECA's Home Energy Savings Calculator for a 3-person Auckland household with average hot water use, switching from a traditional electric hot water cylinder to a hot water heat pump. Efficiency and emissions figures are EECA's; actual savings vary with climate, hot water use and the system chosen.

*Corpus note: EECA figures for hot water heat pumps vary by page and basis — this product page cites ~$284/year saving and ~$7,000 installed, while EECA's "Insights" article cites ~$7,500 installed and ~$333/year running cost, and the "compare options" page contrasts ~$480/year (electric cylinder) vs ~$180/year (heat pump). Differences reflect different scenarios and comparison baselines rather than contradictions; this page is treated as the consumer-facing product page.*
