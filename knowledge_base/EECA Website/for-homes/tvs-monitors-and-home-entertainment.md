# Choose an efficient TV or monitor

A more efficient TV or monitor uses less electricity for the same job, lowering power bills and helping smooth national electricity peaks (which reduces reliance on fossil fuels). In the year ended March 2025, appliance regulation saved 1.48 PJ of energy — about 35,000 homes' annual use and around $36 million in national benefit.

## Reading the label

The stars show efficiency at a glance, but only indicate a model is cheaper to run than similar-sized models with fewer stars — read them alongside the **annual energy consumption (kWh)**, where lower is cheaper regardless of screen size. The consumption figure assumes TVs and monitors are used **10 hours a day** and on standby for 14, in the manufacturer's recommended viewing mode.

## Why efficiency matters here

Some TVs use more than **three times** as much electricity as an efficient model of the same size — meaning more emissions and up to **$600 more** in running costs over 8 years. (As an illustration, EECA compares a 27" monitor at 7 stars vs 3 stars, assuming 10 hours/day use and $0.25/kWh; Source: EECA, 2024.)

## Choosing a model

- **Consider your space** — the ideal size and resolution depend on how far you sit from the screen, and screens look smaller in-store than at home.
- **Compare energy use** — check the annual kWh figure, not just the stars.
- **Look for more stars.**

## Do you even need a new one?

If a current TV's smart features are lacking, adding a streaming device (e.g. Chromecast) can be enough. If you do need a new TV, last season's models are usually cheaper and just as good.
