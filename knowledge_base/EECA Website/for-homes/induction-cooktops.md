# Induction cooktops

Electric induction cooktops are the most efficient cooktop option for home kitchens — and, per EECA, also the safest, healthiest and fastest cooking technology available. They're worth considering if you're upgrading the kitchen, your current cooktop is at end of life, or a gas cooker is the last gas appliance in the home.

## At a glance

- **Cost:** around **$2,980** including purchase and installation.
- **Indicative saving:** an induction cooktop could save the average household about **$17/year** (see methodology).
- **Efficiency:** electric cooking uses roughly **half** the energy of gas; electric cooktops are typically about twice as efficient as gas/LPG, which run at around 30% efficiency.
- **Install:** must be fitted by a qualified electrician (may require an added circuit or switchboard upgrade).

## How induction works

An induction cooktop produces an electromagnetic field that heats the pot or pan directly — effectively turning the cookware into the element. Because it heats cookware rather than an element or open flame, it wastes far less energy and heats almost immediately, bringing water to the boil around **20–40% faster** than gas.

## Induction vs other cooktops

The two common electric options are induction and ceramic electric-resistance cooktops; both beat gas on efficiency, and induction has advantages over ceramic (it uses less electricity than ceramic, and less than half the energy of gas or LPG). Benefits of induction:

- **Safer** — the surface stays cool except under the cookware, reducing burn risk.
- **Easier to clean** — a flat surface where spills don't bake on.
- **Faster** — rapid heating and boiling.
- **More control** — highly responsive temperature changes.
- **More efficient** — energy goes directly into the cookware.

## The real saving: leaving gas

Switching from gas to electric shaves only a small amount off the monthly power bill. The larger opportunity is disconnecting from piped gas entirely, if the cooktop is the **last** gas appliance in the home — removing the daily gas connection charge of roughly **$1–$1.60/day (over $580/year)**. Lifetime savings depend on the upfront cost of the cooktop; induction units are typically dearer to buy and install than traditional electric or gas (gas cooktops average around $1,000 excluding installation; induction prices vary widely).

## Savings methodology (as stated by EECA)

The ~$17/year figure was calculated with EECA's Home Energy Savings Calculator for a 3-person Auckland household switching from a piped gas cooktop to induction. Efficiency and emissions figures draw on the *Electric Homes Technical Report* (Rewiring Aotearoa, March 2024) and internal EECA analysis; the 20–40% faster-boil figure references Consumer Reports testing. Figures are indicative and vary by household, region and gas/electricity prices.
