# Understand home energy loans

Financing can be used to create a healthier, more resilient home that saves on energy bills. Most major NZ banks offer zero or very low interest loans to help upgrade a home — or personal transport — to more energy-efficient options.

## How a loan might help

Shifting to more efficient electric appliances and technology is one of the easiest ways to lower home energy costs, and a home energy loan can bring those savings forward. As with any big financial decision, it's worth getting professional advice to weigh the benefits, risks and costs. (EECA is not a financial advisor; the below is general information, not a recommendation.)

## What home energy loans can be used for

Criteria vary by bank, but across most big NZ banks a home energy loan may cover:

- **Efficient electric appliances** — e.g. a hot water heat pump or induction cooktop.
- **Insulation** — underfloor, ceiling or wall insulation, and double or triple glazing.
- **Moisture control** — a ventilation system, extractor fan, ground water barrier, or waterproofing for walls and foundations.
- **Transport options** — an e-bike, EV, hybrid or plug-in hybrid car, or a smart EV charger.
- **Renewable energy** — solar panels, batteries and inverters, or a solar hot water system.

## How home energy loans work

A home energy loan is usually added to a mortgage as a top-up. Most major banks offer a rate of **0% to 1%**. The amount you can borrow and the repayment term vary by bank.

**Be aware:** if the loan isn't repaid within the agreed time, the rate will likely rise to match the standard home loan rate, increasing total mortgage payments — so it's best to clear the balance within the low- or no-interest period. Read all loan terms before applying and check with the bank about anything unclear.
