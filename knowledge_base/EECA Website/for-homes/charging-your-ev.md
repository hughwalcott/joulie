# Charging your EV

Charging at home is the cheapest and most convenient way to keep an EV topped up. When buying an EV, it's worth getting the best charger you can afford.

## Home charging options

- **Smart wall-mounted charger** — 'smart' features let you automate when you charge and set preferences, usually via an app. This is the fastest, safest and most convenient home option, and often unlocks the cheapest rates.
- **Standard wall-mounted charger** — simple, safe and efficient; needs installation in an off-street parking spot and is typically more efficient than a portable cable. If it isn't smart-capable, you can still get cheap charging by plugging in at off-peak times.
- **Portable 3-pin cable** — usually supplied with the EV. Fine as a backup, but a wall-mounted charger is faster, more convenient and safer for regular use, and a 3-pin cable is unlikely to suit higher-capacity, longer-range EVs.

## Charging safely

Use the right equipment in the right way to protect your wiring and keep everyone safe. You can charge inside or outside, rain or shine, since equipment is designed for NZ conditions. But:

- **Never** use extension cables, or run a cable across a footpath.
- **Don't** connect the charging cable to the power supply via multi-boxes, double plugs or travel plugs.
- An adaptor to connect the cable to the car is only OK if confirmed for use by both the vehicle and cable manufacturers.
- **Never** use modified equipment (e.g. overseas gear fitted with an NZ plug) or damaged/faulty equipment — have faults checked by the manufacturer.

EECA commissioned Standards New Zealand to develop a free guide to buying and using an efficient, smart home charger.

## Power plans

Time-of-use plans with free or discounted off-peak periods suit households charging overnight. Compare plans with Billy (the Electricity Authority's free service): https://billy.govt.nz/

## Charging on the road

Take the chance to charge where and when you're already stopping, and use route-planning apps for peace of mind. Public chargers are found at places like supermarkets, malls and gyms, and at least every **75 km** along most state highways (fast chargers on highways, slower chargers where you'll be longer). The PlugShare app lists NZ chargers — note each one's kW rating to gauge how long a charge will take.

**Public charging etiquette:** charge only to 80% or take only what you need; use public chargers only when you can't charge at home, to keep them free for others; parks marked for EV charging are for charging only; and only unplug another vehicle if you're certain it has finished or you've been given permission.
