# The Commerce Commission's role in electricity lines

New Zealand's electricity system has **two primary economic regulators** who work closely together: the **Electricity Authority** oversees the wholesale and retail markets (and the pricing principles for lines charges), while the **Commerce Commission** regulates the **lines (network) monopolies** — Transpower (transmission) and the local lines companies (distribution). This page explains the Commission's half.

## Why lines companies are regulated

Electricity transmission and distribution businesses are **natural monopolies** — you can't shop around for a different set of wires to your home. Under **Part 4 of the Commerce Act 1986**, the Commission regulates markets with little or no competition, aiming to **mimic the outcomes of a competitive market** for the long-term benefit of consumers: businesses have incentives to innovate, invest and meet quality demands, share efficiency gains (including through lower prices), but are **limited in their ability to earn excessive profits**.

## The two tools

- **Price-quality paths** — set limits on the **total revenue** (or maximum average prices) a lines company can recover from consumers, plus **minimum quality-of-service standards** (so businesses can't cut quality to boost profit). These apply to **Transpower and the 16 local lines companies that are not consumer-owned**.
- **Information disclosure** — requires lines companies to publish key financial and network data (prices, quality measures, forecast investment). This applies to **Transpower and all 29 local lines companies**. The Commission summarises and analyses this to show how businesses are performing and compare them over time.

## Consumer-owned exemption

**12 of the 29** distributors are **consumer-owned** and are **exempt from price-quality regulation**, because Parliament decided their consumers already have enough input into how the business is run (they remain subject to information disclosure).

## What the Commission does *not* do

The Commission **does not set the price you see on your bill**, and does not choose each network's specific investments. It caps revenue and sets quality standards; retailers set the final retail price, factoring in lines charges that are determined in line with the **Electricity Authority's pricing principles**.

## Where to go with problems

For a billing issue, disconnection, damaged property, access or switching difficulty, contact **Utilities Disputes** (free, independent) — though they can't review the *amount* a retailer or lines company charges. The **Fair Trading Act** (also enforced by the Commission) covers what an electricity business tells you about its service, including misleading advertising or leaving out important information. To compare retail plans, the Commission points consumers to price-comparison services (Powerswitch / the EA's Billy).
