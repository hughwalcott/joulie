# DPP4 and RCP4 (2025 reset): investment and bill impacts

The current regulatory reset sets how much lines companies can recover from **1 April 2025**: the **fourth default price-quality path (DPP4)** for local lines companies and the **fourth regulatory control period (RCP4)** individual path for Transpower. Final decisions were published on **20 November 2024**. This reset is the main driver of the lines-charge increases households are seeing.

## Why the increases

The Commission concluded that **substantial network investment is clearly needed**: networks must grow and adapt for the **increasing electrification of transport and industrial process heat** and to connect **new local generation**, while also preparing for more **extreme weather** and replacing **ageing assets**. It acknowledges this will raise bills, but notes that **delaying investment would lead to even higher prices** later.

## Headline figures

- **Transpower (transmission):** the approved revenue represents an increase of about **43%** compared with the previous five years. To manage price shocks, increases are **smoothed** — capped at **15% per year in the first two years** and **5% for the remaining three years**.
- **Household impact (first year):** average increases of roughly **$10 per month** in many regions, and up to about **$25 per month** in some (often rural) regions — a range that reflects local network costs.
- **Share of the bill:** the **distribution** component is about **30%** of a typical retail bill; transmission adds more.

## Important caveats

- The Commission **limits revenue and sets quality standards — it does not set the price on your bill.** Retailers set final prices, factoring in lines charges determined in line with the **Electricity Authority's methods and principles**.
- Distributors **may choose to recover less** than the maximum allowed, provided they can still invest enough to maintain service quality — so actual charges vary by network.
- These are indicative averages from the 2024 final decisions; a given household's change depends on its retailer, plan, region and usage. The Commission publishes a short animated explainer of its role and this decision (in English and te reo Māori).
