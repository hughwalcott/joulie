# Understanding why changes to lines charges may impact your electricity bill

Lines charges — the **transmission and distribution** part of your power bill — are set to rise, and the Commerce Commission (which regulates them) explains why. Lines charges make up a large share of recent increases; the distribution component alone is roughly **30% of a typical retail bill**, and transmission adds more on top.

## Three key reasons lines charges are increasing

- **High inflation** — recent high inflation has significantly increased lines companies' costs (labour, materials, equipment).
- **Interest rate increases** — similar to a fixed-term mortgage, interest rates are **fixed for a five-year period** under the Commission's regulations. Rates are much higher now than when they were last set in **2019**, so the cost of the capital tied up in networks has risen.
- **Higher levels of investment** — Transpower and local lines companies plan to spend more than before to **maintain ageing networks and support growth** (including the electrification of transport and industry, and connecting new local generation), and to prepare for more extreme weather.

## How the Commission limits the increases

To keep network costs as low as reasonably possible, the Commission **reviews Transpower's and most local lines companies' expenditure forecasts** before deciding how much spending to allow, and **limits the rate at which they can increase prices** each year. It balances the genuine need for investment against the impact on consumers — noting that delaying necessary investment tends to lead to even higher prices later.

## What this means for you

The Commission regulates **revenue**, not the price on your bill, so the actual change to any household's bill depends on its retailer, plan, region and usage. **For questions about your specific bill, contact your electricity retailer** — they can explain which components are changing and by how much. For ways to reduce usage, EECA (Gen Less) offers practical energy-saving advice; for hardship support, talk to your retailer early (and see the EA Consumer Care pages).
