# Electrification guide — Electric cars

**Recommended: a battery electric vehicle (BEV).** First, though, consider whether active or public transport (an e-bike is hitting a sweet spot for short trips) could replace some car trips — then, if you need a car, make it electric.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| "Fill-up" cost (same distance as a 50 L petrol tank) | ~$30 from the grid, ~$10 from solar (before RUCs), vs ~$130 petrol at $2.60/L |
| Running cost vs petrol | ~⅔ less to drive/year on rooftop solar; ~½ less on the grid (incl. RUCs) |
| Saving over 15 years | ~$10,000–19,000 |
| Emissions saved | Very high — the biggest single household lever |
| Efficiency | 3–4× more efficient than petrol/diesel |
| Where charging happens | Mostly at home |

## Why it matters

The average NZ home has **1.8 cars** in the driveway and spends over **$3,000/year** on fuel and around **$1,000/year** servicing them. Vehicles are by far the largest energy use for NZ homes — **over 60% of the total** — and the largest source of household emissions. So electrifying your vehicles is the single biggest impact your household can make, on both bills and emissions. With prices dropping rapidly, EVs are expected to reach price parity with fossil-fuel cars within a few years, and by 2030 to cost around 80% of their petrol equivalents.

## Why an EV

- **Uses less energy** — EVs are 3–4× more efficient than internal-combustion (ICE) vehicles, mainly because an ICE car loses around 80% of its fuel energy as heat, noise and vibration.
- **Cheaper to run and service** — higher upfront cost, but even with road user charges (RUCs), EVs cost around two-thirds less to drive per year on rooftop solar, and about half as much on the grid. Fast charging costs more, but most charging happens at home. Growing second-hand supply makes electric driving cheaper still.
- **Better for the climate** — even accounting for the embodied emissions of making the EV, they're a huge improvement for reducing carbon, especially when charged with renewable energy.

## Common questions

- **Off-road / 4WD:** EVs built for 4WD often out-perform fossil-fuel 4WDs. The big new models (Ford F-150 Lightning, Mercedes-Benz G580, Rivian R1) may not be widely available in NZ yet (as at 2025), but many AWD EVs on sale today handle ski-field and dirt-road trips with ease.
- **Using your EV to power your home (V2X):** vehicle-to-home and vehicle-to-grid turn an EV into a battery. Rewiring's research concludes it's not NZ regulations or the grid holding this back — bidirectional chargers can be installed — but car manufacturers not yet unlocking the capability here. It's getting closer, and is another reason to go electric when the time comes (a potential new revenue stream and faster payback).
- **Batteries and mining:** modern batteries increasingly use transparent supply chains or alternatives like lithium iron phosphate (LFP); Australia is a world leader in ethically produced critical minerals; most EV-battery components can be recycled, with rates improving as the industry grows. Even counting battery production, Rewiring argues the environmental impact of EVs is "light years ahead" of fuel-powered cars.
