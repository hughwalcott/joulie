# Electrification guide — Home batteries

**Recommended: a home battery** (paired with rooftop solar) — primarily for resilience and emissions today, with economics improving fast.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Indicative cost | ~$6,000–7,500 (5 kWh); ~$14,000–18,000 (15 kWh); a 13.5 kWh Tesla ≈ $15,000 (~$1,100/kWh) |
| Warranty / lifespan | ~10 years (some now 15) |
| Recyclable | ~95% of a lithium battery |
| Best economics | Time-of-use plans, or plans that reward exporting at peak |
| Rebates | No (but eligible for some low-interest green loans) |

## Why it matters

Battery storage — from household to utility-scale, and within EVs — is a game changer in the energy transition. Solar penetration in NZ is still low but growing fast, and to make the most of it we need to capture and store it for when the sun isn't shining and demand is highest. With battery costs falling rapidly and performance improving, a battery can be a key piece of untethering from fossil fuels — whether your motivation is saving money, cutting your carbon footprint, or preparing for emergencies.

## Why a battery

- **Reduces running costs** — stores your own solar so you import less from the grid, especially valuable on a time-of-use plan during peak tariff windows (e.g. 7–11am and 5–9pm).
- **Improves resilience** — provides power during blackouts and extreme weather, and can recharge from rooftop solar.
- **Supports the energy transition** — every home with a battery effectively removes itself from peak load and reduces the need for expensive large-scale infrastructure. Individually small, they add up: **120,000 homes** (~5% of households) with a medium battery could cut peak load by as much as the Manapouri hydro station can supply for an hour or two.

## Is it worth getting one now?

It depends on your motivation. It's a firm **yes** if you want to reduce emissions, improve resilience and create an all-electric home. If your main concern is return on investment, batteries aren't yet the clear economic call that solar is — but the economics improve daily as prices keep dropping. A battery can make financial sense on a time-of-use rate, or on a plan that rewards exporting at peak (e.g. Octopus, Flick). Rewiring advocates for **Symmetrical Export Tariffs** — paying customers fairly for peak exports — to reveal a battery's true value and speed up payback.

## Finance

Batteries generally aren't eligible for rebates, but many lenders offer low-interest green finance (often requiring a home loan with them) for solar, batteries and EVs — ASB, BNZ, Kiwibank, Westpac, ANZ and others. MoneyHub summarises the options. (Rewiring doesn't endorse specific products; consult a financial advisor.)

## Renters

Asking a landlord for a battery is a harder sell. Rewiring's advice: pitch **solar** first (its ~5–7 year payback makes it an easier case), and join advocacy campaigns calling for finance and landlord incentives so renters benefit.
