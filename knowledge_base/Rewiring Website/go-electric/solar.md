# Electrification guide — Solar

**Recommended: rooftop solar** (ideally paired, over time, with a home battery and efficient electric appliances).

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Cost of delivered energy | Cheapest available to NZ homes — less than half the average grid price |
| Indicative install cost | ~$2,000 per kW (less per kW for larger systems) |
| Payback | Typically ~5–7 years (often within the ~7 years the average Kiwi stays in a home) |
| Lifetime | Panels 25–30 years (inverter usually replaced once in that time) |
| Roofs unsuitable | Only ~5%, per installers |

## Why it matters

Rooftop solar is the cheapest form of delivered energy available to NZ homes — less than half the average cost of grid electricity — because it's generated where it's consumed and doesn't need transporting over poles and wires. Households can use it to power efficient electric appliances and cars, and because fossil-fuel and grid prices look set to keep rising, solar effectively **locks in a lifetime of low, stable energy costs**.

## Doesn't our renewable grid make solar unnecessary?

Rewiring's argument: NZ's grid is ~80% renewable, but the grid doesn't include the fossil fuels we burn in vehicles — so only around **30% of NZ's total energy use is renewable**. As homes and transport electrify, each household will need up to twice as much electricity, and the lowest-cost way to supply much of that is rooftop solar. It also keeps water in the hydro dams for winter, reduces fossil-fuel generation, and (with a battery) adds resilience when the grid is down.

## Why solar

- **Cheapest energy** — you save from the day you start powering your home from your roof. Even a hypothetical free central power plant would be dearer to customers than rooftop solar, once poles, wires and margins are included.
- **Good for the climate** — clean generation displaces fossil fuels both at home (charging an EV) and nationally.
- **Improves property value** — homes with solar tend to sell faster and at higher prices.
- **Helps the community** — more local generation reduces transmission costs for everyone (in Australia's larger solar industry, ~39 jobs are supported per MW installed).

## What to consider

- **Inverter type** — microinverters (one per panel) cost more but are more effective and better for shady roofs or complex installs; string inverters connect the panels together.
- **Quality** — invest in high-quality panels and components from reputable manufacturers for reliability and longevity.
- **System size** — if you can, size for the future (e.g. an EV): it's cheaper to install more at once.
- **Roof and timing** — modern panels can be economic even on south-facing or shady roofs; if your roof is near end of life, consider replacing it first; if you're moving very soon solar may not pay off, though the ~7-year average tenure often covers the payback.

## Steps

Get quotes, then your chosen provider handles installation and connection to your switchboard and the grid. Most systems include monitoring tools to track production and performance. Have the panels cleaned every couple of years (rain does most of the work).

## Renters

Rewiring advocates for renter rights, electrification finance and landlord incentives so tenants benefit from solar. In the meantime, discuss options with your landlord (solar can raise property value and rental appeal), and note the emerging option of **plug-in ("balcony") solar** — one or two panels with a microinverter that plug into a standard socket without modifying the property (see the "watt now" explainer "Sun in a socket").
