# Electrification guide — Space heating and cooling

**Recommended: a heat pump** (also called a reverse-cycle air conditioner or split system).

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Average upfront cost (before rebates) | $3,000–10,000 installed, average home |
| Average annual running cost (with solar) | $183 |
| Average annual running cost (without solar) | $235 |
| Emissions saved | High — ~10× fewer than gas heating |
| Lifetime | 15–20 years |
| Saving over 15 years | ~$16,000 vs LPG; ~$10,000 vs gas |
| Rebates | Yes, for eligible homes |
| Install difficulty | Moderate; licensed heat pump installer required |
| Electrical upgrade | Generally not required |

## Why it matters

Space heating and cooling is about **30% of an average NZ home's energy use** (excluding vehicles) — the biggest energy use inside the home, and higher in colder southern and central North Island regions. Cooling uses far less energy here thanks to the temperate climate. The same efficient appliance handles both: the heat pump. Alongside water heating, space heating is a top gas user in homes, so electrifying it delivers health, cost and emissions benefits.

## Why a heat pump

- **Uses less energy** — 3–4× more efficient than gas heaters, and 2–3× more efficient than electric resistance heaters (oil-column, fan, radiant panel).
- **Cheaper to run** — switching from gas, oil or resistance heating can save up to $1,000/year; heat pumps deliver more than 3× lower energy bills than gas. Timed with rooftop solar (e.g. pre-heating during the day), running costs can average as low as $183/year.
- **Healthier** — unlike gas heaters and stoves (which emit carbon monoxide, methane, nitrogen dioxide and formaldehyde, especially when unflued), heat pumps put no combustion products into the home.
- **Lower emissions** — ~10× lower than gas heating.
- **Flexible** — most have timers (to run during solar generation) and do both heating and cooling.

## Types to consider

- **Split system (ductless)** — lower-cost and very efficient; one indoor coil and one outdoor unit (about suitcase-sized) linked by a refrigerant line. Multiple indoor units can create zones. A **multi-head** system runs several indoor units off one outdoor unit but is less efficient, with a larger outdoor unit.
- **Ducted central heat pump** — distributes warm/cool air through ducts (usually in the ceiling), with indoor and outdoor units. If existing ducts are present (from old central AC or ducted gas), check suitability with an installer. More expensive and less efficient to run than split systems.

## Cooling

Cooling needs far less energy than heating in NZ and existing methods are already nearly all electric and fairly efficient, so they can stay as-is in an electrified world (and benefit from cheap solar). Use passive cooling first — shading, plants, blinds. With solar but no battery, set timers to pre-cool during daytime solar production to cut evening load.

## Steps

1. **Research** the options and identify the key areas to heat.
2. **Seal leaks** — find and seal air-leak gaps and add simple draught-proofing.
3. **Get quotes** from licensed installers to correctly size the heat pump.
4. **Install and maintain** — clean the filter once a year (blocked filters cut efficiency).

## Government assistance

**Warmer Kiwi Homes** — eligible homes can get up to 80% of the cost of an approved heat pump installed, with heater grants capped at $3,000 incl GST (rebates noted as current May 2024). Check eligibility: https://www.eeca.govt.nz/co-funding-and-support/products/warmer-kiwi-homes-programme/check-eligibility/

*(Corpus note: EECA's own current figure for the Warmer Kiwi Homes heating grant is up to 90% / $3,450 for highest-need households — see the EECA corpus. Rewiring's page cites 80% as at May 2024. Treat EECA's programme page as authoritative on the current percentage.)*

## Renters

Renters (about a third of NZ households) can buy a window unit or portable heat pump, or make the case to a landlord (government rebates, the monitoring requirement for gas equipment, and the health impacts of gas). To cut heating/cooling costs, seal draughts, add temporary window glazing and external shading (plants, shade cloths, blinds), and close curtains and doors.

## Selected FAQ

- **Getting the most from a heat pump:** clean the filter yearly; for heating, mount low so warm air flows across the floor; choose timer/wifi/bluetooth control to coincide with solar; seal air leaks.
- **Cold climates:** heat pumps keep a home warm even down to around −10°C, and heat roughly half the homes in Norway, Finland and Sweden.
- **Insulate first?** Getting off gas is the priority; insulation helps efficiency (especially in cold or draughty homes) but isn't required before electrifying. Start with the low-hanging fruit (air leaks, window coverings, closing doors) before big-ticket ceiling/wall/floor insulation. Rewiring's position: if you're trying to stay warm, a heat pump is often cheaper and more effective than insulating.
- **Ductwork needed?** No — mini-split/ductless systems suit smaller homes or individual rooms and are ideal for retrofits.
