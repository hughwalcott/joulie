# Electrification guide — Get started

Electrifying a home is now, for most households, both a smart environmental decision and the smart economic one. An electrified home swaps some or all of the fossil-fuel machines used for water heating, space heating, driving and cooking for much more efficient electric equivalents — and, where possible, adds rooftop solar to generate cheap electricity and a battery to store it for when it's needed.

## The upfront cost vs long-run savings

Electric appliances and vehicles usually cost more upfront but are much cheaper over their lifetime. Rewiring Aotearoa argues New Zealand is one of the first countries to reach an "electrification tipping point" — where households and businesses can save money now by electrifying, even with upfront costs and finance built in — because local fossil fuels are expensive and the price of electric machines has fallen quickly.

- **Average saving:** homes currently using gas appliances and petrol vehicles could save around **$1,500 per year** (or around **$4,500 per year** with a low-interest loan) by switching to electric equivalents powered by a mix of rooftop solar, a home battery and the grid. Homes that use more power, hot water and vehicle travel tend to save the most.
- **When to upgrade:** every machine has a natural lifetime (gas hobs ~13–15 years, cars ~20 years), so the next replacement decision should be electric. Building new? A gas connection makes no economic or environmental sense. Every new fossil-fuel machine locks in years of emissions and rising fuel costs.

## The main machines (with indicative figures)

| Machine | 15-year saving | Install cost | Emissions saved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Space heating** (heat pump) | $10,000–16,000 | $3,000–10,000 | High |
| **Water heating** (hot water heat pump) | $4,000–7,000 | $3,000–10,000 | High |
| **Cooking** (induction/resistive) | $0–1,000 | $3,000–5,000 | Moderate |
| **Driving** (EV) | $10,000–19,000 | $30,000–70,000 new | Very high |
| **Rooftop solar** | payback 5–7 years | ≈$2,000 per kW | Moderate |
| **Home battery** | ~$3,750 | $700–1,300/kWh | Moderate |

Key points per machine:

- **Space heating** — heat pumps deliver about three times lower energy bills than gas heating and ten times lower emissions.
- **Water heating** — a shower from a hot water heat pump with solar costs less than a quarter of gas water heating; resistive electric with solar can be similarly low-cost but has higher ongoing bills than a heat pump.
- **Cooking** — cooking uses little energy, but electric is cheaper and much lower-emission than gas (and healthier); the biggest saving comes if the cooktop is the last gas appliance, letting you drop the gas connection. Induction is around a third cheaper to run than gas and safer (the surface doesn't get hot).
- **Driving** — the single biggest lever for both bill and emissions savings. Filling a 50 L petrol tank is ~$130 (at $2.60/L); the electric equivalent distance is ~$30 from the grid and ~$10 from solar (before road user charges). Most charging happens at home.
- **Rooftop solar** — the cheapest delivered energy in NZ, less than half the average price of grid electricity; also adds resilience with a battery when the grid is down.
- **Home batteries** — approaching the point of making economic sense for most homes, especially with a low-interest green loan; valuable for bill savings, resilience and emergencies.

## Common barriers (and how to get over them)

- **Knowledge feels inaccessible** — seek independent info (the Electric Homes report), talk to others who've electrified, and always get multiple quotes. Only ~5% of roofs are unsuitable for solar; check sunlight with NIWA SolarView.
- **Upfront cost is too high** — think of it as "swapping fuel for finance." All major banks offer low-interest green loans to home-loan customers (ASB, BNZ, Kiwibank, Westpac, ANZ and others); MoneyHub summarises the options. (Rewiring doesn't endorse specific products; consult a financial advisor.)
- **Too much hassle for time-poor people** — heat pumps install in a few hours; hot water heat pumps are newer but growing; induction needs a certified electrician (and maybe a plumber to disconnect gas), though portable options exist; solar often installs in under a day, frequently alongside an EV charger.
- **Export rates make solar not stack up** — Rewiring advocates for cost-reflective two-way tariffs (fairer export rates). In the meantime, some retailers offer better buy-back rates; compare plans on Powerswitch. But note: switching your *energy source* from fossil fuels to electricity usually saves far more than switching electricity provider.
- **Renters feel they lack control** — about a third of NZ households rent. Options include negotiating a "comfort levy" (small rent increase in exchange for a larger power-bill reduction from a landlord-funded upgrade), an EV charged from the grid, and portable options like plug-in induction stoves and portable heat pumps.
