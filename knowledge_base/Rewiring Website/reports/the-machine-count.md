# Report — The Machine Count

The Machine Count is New Zealand's first comprehensive **inventory of fossil-fuel machines** — from cars, tractors and utes to industrial equipment — developed by Rewiring Aotearoa in partnership with **Ara Ake** and **EECA** (released May 2025). Its purpose is to show how many machines the country needs to electrify, and how viable electrification is for each.

## Headline findings

- New Zealand has around **10 million fossil-fuel machines**.
- About **84% (roughly 8.5 million)** are ready to electrify today with readily available, cost-effective alternatives.
- Electrifying just the **top ~6 million priority machines** would save New Zealanders approximately **$3.7 billion and 7.5 million tonnes of CO₂-equivalent every year**.

## How machines are prioritised

The report ranks machines by *electrifiability*, then by an emissions ranking (based on emissions intensity, typical annual hours of use, and count), then by count. A waterfall chart of economy-wide energy emissions shows the largest single reductions come from a few target machines — **cars and commercial vehicles** (trucks, utes, vans) — but stresses that the cumulative effect of smaller annual savings across all the other machine types is large and shouldn't be overlooked; each is a permanent, year-on-year reduction opportunity.

Some machine types sit at different readiness stages depending on sub-type or fuel (e.g. smaller mining trucks in late R&D while larger trucks need new development). For **process heat**, solutions exist below 100°C (heat pumps) and 100–300°C (electrode boilers), while higher-temperature industrial processes (steel, methanol, fertiliser, cement) still need innovation and supportive policy.

## "Swap fuel for finance"

The report popularises Rewiring's framing that electrifying is about swapping ongoing fuel costs for upfront finance. It uses a car example: a **$52,000 EV** on a 5.5% loan costs about **$135/week** to run on the grid (or ~$128/week with solar), versus a **$39,000 petrol car** at about **$148/week** once maintenance and fuel are included — a saving of roughly **$11,000–16,000 over 15 years**. It notes panel and battery prices have fallen around **90% in the past decade**, so old quotes are worth revisiting.

## Policy asks

Underwrite low-interest electrification loans with the Crown balance sheet; provide on-bill finance so loan repayments and power bills are paid together; streamline approvals for longer-term green loans secured against property; run nationwide awareness campaigns from trusted sources; and support community groups and pilots.
