# Report — Symmetrical Export Tariffs

Symmetrical Export Tariffs is Rewiring Aotearoa's proposal to **pay customers fairly for the electricity they export at peak times** — the same way they are charged more for importing at peak. Today, most export (buy-back) rates are well below the value that customer-generated electricity provides to the system, especially at peak, which suppresses the returns on rooftop solar and (particularly) home batteries.

## The argument

- Charging structures are **asymmetric**: customers pay time-varying, peak-weighted prices to *import*, but are usually paid a flat, low rate to *export* — even when their exports are most valuable to the grid.
- Making tariffs **symmetrical** (cost-reflective in both directions) would reveal the true value of a home battery, shorten payback periods, and make batteries far more **"bankable"** — accelerating uptake.
- Retailers should not profit from customer-funded generation by buying exports below the wholesale price; at minimum, exports should earn the wholesale price, and ideally reflect peak value.

## Why it matters system-wide

If symmetrical export tariffs were implemented, New Zealand homes, farms and businesses with solar and batteries could **contribute much more to the energy system**, helping to **lower energy prices for all New Zealanders** and provide a more secure and resilient electricity supply — turning distributed customer investment into a shared benefit rather than a stranded one. This proposal is closely tied to Rewiring's **Delivered Cost of Energy** argument that distributed resources are undervalued by the current market framework.
