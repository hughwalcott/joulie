# Report — Delivered Cost of Energy

This report is aimed at decision-makers and challenges how New Zealand values different kinds of electricity generation. Its central argument: **the most important energy cost is not what a corporation pays to generate electricity, but what a consumer pays to buy it** — just as, with fossil fuels, what matters is the "price at the pump," not the price of crude oil. The same logic should apply to electricity.

## The core critique

- The national conversation about building the "right" generation is anchored in a decades-old framework that assumes plants connect directly to the transmission grid and earn the wholesale price.
- Proponents of grid-scale plants argue "bigger is better" because of lower capital cost per MW of installed capacity compared with rooftop solar. Rewiring argues this **ignores that the value of electricity varies by location**: a higher-cost form of generation can be the best option if it sits where prices (and therefore value) are higher.
- It's therefore **not appropriate to compare the long-run marginal cost (LRMC) of grid-connected plants directly with the LRMC of rooftop solar** — the value equation differs. The most efficient path isn't simply the lowest-LRMC plant, regardless of whether it's transmission-connected, distribution-connected or behind-the-meter.

## The "delivered cost" idea

To judge the value of electricity at the customer's premises, you must count the **delivered cost of energy** — all the supply-chain costs from the grid to the house or business that rooftop solar and batteries would reduce. Customers with rooftop solar are also motivated to shift demand to maximise daytime solar use, which benefits them and the system.

## Policy implications Rewiring draws

- **Retailers should not profit from customer-funded generation export**: paying export rates below the average wholesale price does exactly that. At minimum, exported solar should be paid the **wholesale price**.
- **Cost-reflective tariffs should be mandated**, and if distributed resources create greater value per kW than grid-connected resources, regulators should reduce the red tape around investing in them.
- **Distribution networks should be able to absorb far more rooftop solar** than exists today without major additional network investment.
