# Report — Electric Farms

The Electric Farms paper extends Rewiring's household analysis to the farm, examining the cost savings and emissions reductions from swapping diesel machines for electric equivalents powered by solar and batteries — with the all-electric **Forest Lodge Orchard** (Central Otago) as its flagship case study. It has generated significant interest from farmers nationally.

## Why farms matter to the energy system

Farms are unusually well placed to provide **mid-scale renewable generation**: they have more buying power than households, land and space for larger systems, owner-operators who can make decisions, and access to finance for long-term investments. Rewiring estimates that if each of New Zealand's approximately **50,000 farms** installed mid-scale solar, they could generate an additional **~60% of the country's current total electricity consumption**. Solar also fits farm irrigation (which lifts summer demand) and can help keep hydro lakes full for winter.

## The economic opportunity

The agriculture sector spends around **$700 million on diesel each year**. Electric machines cost more upfront but are much cheaper to run; powered by solar and batteries, farms can cut energy bills, improve resilience, and reduce on-farm emissions (and reliance on carbon offsetting).

## Case study — Forest Lodge Orchard

Forest Lodge runs around **21 electric machines** — including two electric frost-fighting fans, electric golf carts and New Zealand's first electric tractor — and generates about **85% of the energy it needs** from rooftop and ground-mounted solar plus battery storage. As a result it saves around **$40,000 per year** (across farm and home) by avoiding diesel, avoiding lines charges, and exporting electricity at high-value times. Its electric frost fans run at roughly **20% of the operating cost** of diesel fans, and a 40 hp electric tractor does the work of an 80 hp diesel at a fraction of the running cost. Because the orchard has batteries to store cheap solar, it is buffered against wholesale price spikes and is paid more to export at peak — making the batteries a more "bankable" investment.
