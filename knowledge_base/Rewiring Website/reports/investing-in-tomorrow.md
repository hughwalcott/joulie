# Report — Investing in Tomorrow: the electrification opportunity

Where **Electric Homes** made the case at the household level, **Investing in Tomorrow** scales it to the whole country and frames electrification as an **investment, not a cost**. Its core argument: because electric machines use energy far more efficiently, buying and financing them is cheaper over the long run than buying and fuelling fossil-fuel machines — so the big household savings add up to big national savings, while improving New Zealand's balance of payments and cutting emissions.

## Headline national numbers

- New Zealand homes and businesses currently spend around **$55 million per day — about $20 billion per year — on fossil fuels**, most of them imported.
- Electrifying could deliver combined savings of around **$29 million per day, or $10.7 billion per year, by 2040**, by avoiding expensive imported fossil fuels in exchange for locally generated renewable electricity — while avoiding millions of tonnes of carbon emissions per year.

## Why the efficiency gap drives the savings

- EVs are around **4× more efficient** than petrol vehicles; using a petrol-price equivalent, you can power an EV for about **$0.31/L with rooftop solar** and **$0.64/L from the grid**.
- Heat pumps output about **3–4 units (300–400%) of heat per unit of electricity**, versus gas space heaters at ~80% and gas hot water at ~95%.
- The biggest savings come from **vehicles**, followed by **hot water heating**.

The report reiterates the Electric Homes finding that the average home can save ~$1,500/year (including upfront costs and 5.5% interest) by switching to electric and running on a mix of grid, rooftop solar and batteries.

## Notability

The paper was co-authored by **Paul Conway**, then chief economist at the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, and peer-reviewed by **Geoff Simmons** — part of why it's widely regarded in the sector as a credible, well-produced piece of macroeconomic analysis of the electrification opportunity.
