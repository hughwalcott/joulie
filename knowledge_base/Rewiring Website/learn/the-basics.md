# Electrification FAQ — The basics

## What is electrification?

Electrification swaps some or all of the fossil-fuel machines in homes and businesses for much more efficient electric equivalents, powered by renewable electricity from the grid, rooftop solar and batteries. These technologies are available today. Although NZ's lights, dishwashers and ovens are already electric, many households still burn fossil fuels for water heating, space heating, driving and cooking. The electricity system is around 80% renewable, but electricity is only part of the picture — fossil fuels still make up around **70% of New Zealand's total energy use**.

## What is an electric home?

An electric home swaps those fossil-fuel machines for efficient electric equivalents and, where possible, adds solar panels to generate cheap electricity and a battery to store it for when it's needed.

## How much can the average home save right now?

Every home is different, but on average a home currently using gas appliances and petrol vehicles could save around **$1,500 per year** (or around **$4,500 per year** with a low-interest loan) by switching to electric equivalents powered by a mix of rooftop solar, a home battery and the grid. Homes that use more power, hot water and vehicle travel save the most.

## Why is electrification more efficient?

Heat pumps, induction stoves and EVs are on average **3–4× more efficient** at using energy than the fossil-fuel machines they replace. So while an electric home uses more *electricity*, it uses much less *total energy* — switching vehicles, water heating, space heating and cooking to electric can cut total household energy use by around **70%**. That efficiency is where much of the saving comes from. Rewiring's framing: "electrification is the energy efficiency we've always been looking for."

## When should I upgrade?

Every machine has a natural lifetime (gas hobs ~13–15 years, cars ~20 years), so the next replacement should be electric. It rarely makes sense to scrap a working appliance, though for some machines (particularly cars) early replacement can make financial and emissions sense. Building new? A gas connection makes no economic or environmental sense. Every new fossil-fuel machine locks in years of emissions and rising fuel costs, and going electric earlier locks in more savings.

## What about the upfront cost?

Electric appliances and vehicles usually cost more upfront but are much cheaper — and more price-stable — over their lifetime. Rewiring argues NZ has reached an "electrification tipping point" where electrifying saves money now, even with upfront costs and finance included, because local fossil fuels are expensive and electric-machine prices have fallen quickly.
