# Electrification FAQ — Space heating

## Does a heat pump work in cold climates?

Yes. Heat pumps work more efficiently in warmer climates but still work effectively in the cold — keeping a home warm even down to around −10°C, and heating roughly half the homes in Norway, Finland and Sweden. Rewiring's advice: avoid the cheaper versions.

## Does a heat pump require ductwork?

No. Mini-split (ductless) heat pumps let you regulate temperature in individual rooms and are ideal for retrofitting homes with non-ducted heating.

## Do I need to insulate my home first?

Getting off gas is the priority. Insulation improves efficiency and may be worthwhile in colder or draughty homes, but it isn't required before electrifying. Start with the low-hanging fruit — sealing air leaks, better window coverings, closing doors — before big-ticket insulation.

## Isn't a heat pump just wasting electricity in a cold, damp, draughty home?

Rewiring's position: it doesn't have to be either/or. Electric machines are far more efficient than fossil-fuel ones — a heat pump delivers about 3× lower energy bills than gas and 10× lower emissions — so if you're trying to stay warm, a heat pump is often cheaper and more effective than paying for insulation or double glazing first (though some insulation is still wanted for health).

## How efficient is a heat pump vs other heating?

Heat pumps are 3–4× more efficient than gas heaters and 2–3× more efficient than electric-resistance heaters (oil-column, fan, radiant panel). Timed with rooftop solar, running costs can average as low as ~$183/year.
