# Electrification FAQ — Emissions, offsets and individual actions

## Why is electrification the answer?

Rewiring frames the emissions problem as, at root, an **energy problem** (around 73% of global emissions come from energy) and, in practice, a **machine problem**: under every energy-emissions category sit fossil-fuel machines (power plants, boilers, petrol vehicles, gas water heaters). Swapping those for efficient electric alternatives powered by renewables — quickly — cuts a large chunk of emissions immediately and buys time for harder, longer-term changes. Because climate change is a cumulative problem, acting faster means less warming.

## How much difference do my decisions make?

The main benefit of electrifying your household is lower cost, but it's also likely the biggest single emissions decision you can make — and you can do it now. Rewiring calls these "dinner-table decisions," and argues households make up around **30% of NZ's domestic emissions** (more with businesses). Illustrative lifetime comparisons (return Auckland–Queenstown flights) for choosing fossil over electric: a new petrol car instead of an EV ≈ 107 flights; gas hot water instead of a hot water heat pump ≈ 20 flights; LPG home heating instead of a heat pump ≈ 35 flights; a gas cooktop ≈ 6 flights.

## Why is Rewiring's analysis different from previous emissions calculations?

Rewiring argues it's a categorisation issue dating to the 1970s: a car is counted under "transport," but the choice of car is a household decision. Recategorising by who makes the decision shows households make up a much bigger, more addressable share — "low-hanging fruit" for reductions — which is why Rewiring wants household electrification prioritised in emissions plans. It estimates around 25% of gross emissions come from small machines households and businesses use.

## We're a small country — why bother?

Smaller countries collectively account for over a third of global emissions — almost as much as China and India combined. Small nations like Norway (0.1% of global emissions, 80%+ of new cars electric) set examples. Rewiring argues NZ — with abundant renewables and a history of electrical firsts (world's first all-electric home, first practical electric home water heater, first electric cherry orchard) — has a real chance to become the world's most electric economy, saving billions and cutting emissions.

## Can't we just plant trees?

No. Rewiring argues we won't hit climate targets by planting trees; we need to cut fossil-fuel emissions. Example: at Forest Lodge Orchard, 9,300 cherry trees removed ~3 tonnes CO₂/year while burning diesel emitted ~50 tonnes/year — about 18× more. If the government needs offsets, Rewiring suggests it instead pay New Zealanders to cut fuel emissions through electrification.

## Why focus only on carbon?

Rewiring says it doesn't. It warns against "carbon tunnel vision" and argues electrification is worth doing even setting climate aside, because renewable energy is cheaper, cleaner, more efficient, more abundant and more resilient — improving human wellbeing on many metrics. It prefers "panoptic vision" over tunnel vision.
