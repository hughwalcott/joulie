# Electrification FAQ — Cooking

## Can I use an induction cooktop if I have a pacemaker?

Check with the appliance details and a healthcare professional. Induction cooktops generate electromagnetic fields that can affect a pacemaker if you get too close — the British Heart Foundation recommends keeping at least 60 cm (2 ft) between the stovetop and the pacemaker. Most people can use a hob with these precautions, and classic electric cooktops are a good alternative for those concerned.

## Do I need three-phase power for an induction cooktop?

For most houses, single-phase power is sufficient. Check with an electrician before purchasing to see whether any electrical upgrades are required.

## What if I leave the induction cooktop on?

An induction surface only heats when a pan is present, so once you remove the pots and pans no heat is generated — making it safer than gas or traditional electric cooktops.

## How does temperature control compare with gas?

Induction directly heats the cooking surface rather than the surrounding air (as gas does), giving very precise temperature control. A number of professional chefs have switched to induction for this reason.

## Renters and cooking

Portable induction stoves are available for renters who don't want to cook with gas — worth considering given the health impacts of indoor gas appliances.
