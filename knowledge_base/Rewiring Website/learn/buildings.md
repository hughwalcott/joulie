# Electrification FAQ — Buildings and insulation

## What about insulation — do I need it first?

Some homes are unhealthy and should be insulated, but Rewiring's position is that if you're trying to stay warm, it's generally cheaper to install a heat pump than to insulate. Illustratively: insulating a whole home might cut gas use by ~25%, whereas replacing that gas with a heat pump cuts energy use ~70% overnight without any insulation — because electric machines are far more efficient (heat pumps deliver about 3× lower energy bills than gas heating and 10× lower emissions).

Insulation still improves heating/cooling efficiency and may be worthwhile in colder areas or draughty homes, but it isn't essential before electrifying and shouldn't hold up getting off gas. Start with low-hanging fruit — sealing air leaks, better interior window coverings, covering windows and closing doors at night — before big-ticket ceiling, wall and floor insulation.

## Will better building standards help?

Buildings use around 40% of the world's energy and cause around 17% of emissions, so they matter. Standards for very efficient homes (e.g. "passivhaus") are a good idea, and a sufficiently good building may not need heat-pump heating. But Rewiring stresses we must also solve for the homes we already have — only a small share of housing stock is built new each year (~1% in the US), so retrofitting existing homes is essential, and electrification is "the efficiency we've always been looking for," cutting total energy use up to 70%. New gas connections make no economic, environmental or health sense.
