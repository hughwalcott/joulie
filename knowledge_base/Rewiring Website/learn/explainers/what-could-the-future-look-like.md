# Explainer — What could the future look like? (A future with two visions)

This explainer is a **scenario piece**: when it comes to our energy system, Rewiring argues we could do the bare minimum and pay the price, or think ahead and reap the rewards — and it sketches what each of those choices could look like in **2030**.

## The choice

New Zealand is at a critical point in its energy transition: customers face rising electricity, gas and petrol costs, there are supply challenges for both the grid and natural gas, and reliance on fossil fuels is becoming less viable. Rewiring frames the fork in the road as:

- **Back the status quo** — continue with a broken system New Zealanders are losing faith (and money) in, protect the big incumbents, and preserve arrangements that aren't fit for purpose; or
- **Back customers and the country** — choose a cheaper, cleaner, more secure and more productive future.

## Vision A — the bare minimum (2030)

In the "do the minimum" future, electrification stays the preserve of the well-off and the motivated. As a representative household puts it: we know a few people with solar, batteries and EVs, but they generally have the money or access to a loan; it's a lot to spend upfront and there's not much to spare given other rising costs; and it's complicated, confusing and time-consuming — you have to be really keen, "a bit of an energy nerd," to go down that path. The savings and resilience exist, but most people can't reach them.

## Vision B — doing it properly (2030)

In the better future, "the decision makers took Rewiring Aotearoa's vision and ran with it" — accessible finance, better information and modernised rules let ordinary households and businesses electrify, so the savings, lower emissions, and greater energy security and resilience are broadly shared.

## The underlying claim

Rewiring's vision is for New Zealand to become the **world's most electric economy** — and it insists this "is not some utopian dream." It's achievable with technology that exists right now and it's affordable, but it needs **leadership from the top down and energy from the bottom-up**.
