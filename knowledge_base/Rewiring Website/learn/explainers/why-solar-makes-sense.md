# Why solar makes sense

Rewiring's summary of the case: solar on our rooftops, farms and businesses can displace the emissions from burning fossil fuels in our homes and especially our cars, provide the extra electricity we need to run our electric machines, help bring the cost of electricity down for everyone on the network, and improve energy security by keeping water in our hydro lakes for when we need it most.

## The four arguments

1. **It's the cheapest energy** — rooftop solar is the cheapest form of delivered electricity available to NZ homes, at less than half the average grid price, because it's generated where it's consumed and avoids the cost of poles, wires and margins. Financed solar produces power at roughly $0.06/kWh (at 0%) to $0.13/kWh, versus a grid average around $0.34–0.35/kWh. Even a hypothetical free central power plant would be dearer to customers once delivery costs are included.
2. **It displaces the biggest emissions** — the extra electricity lets households run efficient electric machines, and especially charge EVs, cutting the largest chunk of a household's emissions (petrol and diesel).
3. **It lowers costs for everyone** — more local generation reduces transmission costs across the network; widespread uptake could double or triple renewable generation (9 kW systems on ~80% of homes ≈ +40% electricity; mid-sized systems on ~50,000 farms ≈ +60%).
4. **It improves energy security** — solar keeps water in the hydro lakes for winter and dry years (when it doesn't rain, it tends to shine — sunshine hours run ~5% higher in a dry year), reducing the need for coal and gas.

## Addressing the doubts

- **"We already have a renewable grid."** True for electricity (~80%+ renewable), but only about 30% of *total* energy is renewable once transport, heating and industry are counted — so we need much more renewable electricity, and rooftop solar is the lowest-cost way to supply it.
- **"Is it sunny enough?"** New Zealand's solar resource is similar to Victoria (which is at ~30% household solar versus NZ's ~3%) and better than Germany. Only about 5% of roofs are genuinely unsuitable, and modern panels work on cloudy days and even south-facing or shady roofs.
- **"Shouldn't we just build big solar and wind farms?"** We'll need more grid-scale generation too, but focusing only on farms uses new technology "in an old-fashioned way" — centralising generation and still paying rising distribution costs. Rooftop solar cuts out the middleman; the 14 biggest Auckland rooftops alone would rival NZ's largest solar farm.

The through-line: rooftop solar is a rare win-win-win — cheapest for the household, good for the grid and community, and good for the climate.
