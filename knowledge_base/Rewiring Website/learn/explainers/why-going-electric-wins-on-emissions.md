# Why going electric wins on emissions

This explainer answers one of the most common questions Rewiring gets: *will switching to an electric machine actually reduce my carbon footprint if making the new machine or its materials produces emissions?* Rewiring agrees it's a fair question and that the **full life cycle** matters — electric alternatives (including EVs) often do produce more emissions during **manufacturing** — but over the lifetime of the machine they create much less carbon pollution because **they don't burn any fuel**.

## Embodied vs operational emissions

- **Embodied emissions** are those associated with producing the machine and its materials (e.g. an EV battery). This is where electric machines can start "behind."
- **Operational emissions** are those from running the machine. A petrol car keeps emitting every kilometre; an EV on New Zealand's highly renewable grid emits very little — so it quickly "pays back" its higher manufacturing emissions and then pulls far ahead over its life.

Rewiring's conclusion: buying a **new electric car is better for the environment than keeping an existing petrol car**, and because many older cars are less fuel-efficient, continuing to drive them is worse still. The European Federation for Transport and Environment estimates that around **two tonnes of CO₂e** can be avoided in EV production through circular supply chains or recycling — which brings the emissions payback time forward by about a year. (Rewiring also notes more than 90% of an EV battery's components can be recycled, so even counting production, the environmental impact of EVs is small compared with fuel-powered cars.)

## Putting the lifetime difference in perspective

Rewiring illustrates the lifetime emissions gap using domestic flights (Auckland–Queenstown return) as a yardstick. Over the life of the machine, choosing the fossil option instead of the efficient electric one is roughly equivalent to:

- **107 return flights** — buying a new petrol car instead of an EV;
- **35 return flights** — using LPG to heat your home instead of a heat pump;
- **20 return flights** — installing a gas hot-water system instead of a hot water heat pump;
- **6 return flights** — a gas cooktop instead of induction.

## The same logic applies to cost

Just as with carbon, electric machines (and solar panels) cost more to buy upfront but — because they're so much more efficient — cost much less to run, producing significant long-term savings. And, as Rewiring is careful to add, "it's not all about carbon" — the switch also brings health, resilience and cost-of-living benefits.
