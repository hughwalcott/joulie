# Explainer — Scale and speed: why the climate needs electric vehicles

Rewiring's position: we need great urban design that encourages **electric public and active transport** (for safer neighbourhoods and healthier lifestyles), but we *also* need **electric cars** for rapid decarbonisation and to address all the vehicle travel we realistically aren't going to shift or avoid. These two things are complementary, not competing — the data shows that even in the best cities for mode-shift, cars still do most of the distance travelled.

## Why electrification is the biggest lever

Comparing total energy use by light vehicles across four scenarios — (i) status quo, (ii) an ambitious mode-shift scenario, (iii) fully electrifying the light-vehicle fleet, and (iv) both combined — the explainer shows that **electrifying vehicles delivers the biggest single step-change** in energy consumption (and therefore emissions). Mode-shift helps, and combining both is best, but on its own mode-shift can't match the drop that comes from swapping combustion engines (which waste ~80% of their fuel energy) for far more efficient electric drivetrains.

## Why speed matters

Climate change is a cumulative problem: the faster the emissions curve bends down, the less warming we ultimately cause. Yet every year New Zealanders buy **over 120,000 new petrol and diesel cars**, each locking in at least a decade of emissions. Rewiring argues that by rapidly increasing EV uptake we could **cut car emissions roughly in half within about a decade**, and by 2050 (ideally sooner) make transport emissions a problem of the past. The Norway example is instructive — from virtually no EVs in 2010 to over 90% of new-car sales — showing how quickly a fleet can turn over when the incentives line up.

The takeaway: keep pushing walking, cycling and public transport, but recognise that electric cars are essential to decarbonise transport at the speed and scale the climate demands.
