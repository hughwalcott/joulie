# Explainer — Why electric vehicles matter

Rewiring's framing: plenty of people need cars, plenty still want them, and other vehicles are "literal engines of prosperity." We don't have to give them up to reach our climate goals — **we just need them to be electric**.

## Why vehicles are the priority

Vehicles are the single biggest lever for a household, on both bills and emissions. Counting cars as part of household energy, they make up around **60% of the total** and are the largest source of household emissions. Petrol and diesel cars are about **98% of the fleet**, and burning that fuel wastes roughly three-quarters of its energy as heat, noise and vibration — whereas an EV is around **3–4× more efficient** at turning energy into motion.

## The economics

EVs cost more upfront but far less to run and service. Using a petrol-price equivalent, you can "fill" an EV for around **$0.30/L on rooftop solar** and **$0.60/L from the grid** (before RUCs) — versus petrol at well over $2.60/L — and even with road user charges added, EVs are substantially cheaper to drive. Filling a 50 L petrol tank costs about $130; the same distance in an EV is about $30 from the grid or ~$10 from solar. Global forecasts point to EVs reaching price parity with petrol cars in the next few years, and a growing second-hand market makes electric driving cheaper still.

## Common worries, addressed

- **Range/charging:** most new EVs exceed 500 km range; the average NZ car drives ~200 km/week, and about 85–90% of charging happens at home, so expensive fast-chargers are rarely needed.
- **Rural driving:** EVs make *more* sense the more you drive — and rural drivers cover far more distance.
- **Batteries and emissions:** even counting manufacturing, EVs come out far ahead over their lifetime because they burn no fuel; more than 90% of battery components can be recycled.
- **Batteries on wheels:** EVs double as large batteries (V2L/V2H/V2G) — a resource for the home and grid, not a problem.

## The bottom line

Rewiring's position is that EVs are the main vehicle type to encourage and support for decarbonisation — hybrids still burn petrol and are, at best, a stepping stone. Keep supporting walking, cycling and public transport too, but for the driving we're going to do anyway, making it electric is the biggest single win available to households.
