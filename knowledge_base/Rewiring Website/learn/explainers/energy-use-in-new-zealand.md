# Explainer — Energy use in New Zealand

The premise: New Zealand's highly renewable **electricity** grid is often said to put us ahead on decarbonisation — and it helps — but electricity is only part of the picture. Gas (for heating homes, water and cooking), petrol and diesel (for vehicles), and coal and gas (for industrial processes) make up the vast majority of the country's *total* energy consumption. So we can't just decarbonise the grid; we need to switch fossil-fuel machines to electric alternatives run on renewable electricity. That will increase the amount of electricity we use, but — because electric machines are so efficient — it will **decrease total energy use and emissions**.

## The numbers (2023)

- New Zealand consumed about **156 petajoules (PJ) of electricity**, of which **88% was renewable** (one PJ would power roughly 40,000 average homes for a year).
- But **total energy consumption was about 547 PJ**, making electricity just **26%** of the total.
- So of all the energy consumed, only about **23% is renewable electricity**. Adding ~7% from renewable fuels used directly (like wood) brings the share of total consumer energy from renewables to about **30%**.
- **Oil supplied over half (52%)** of consumer energy — all imported as petrol, diesel and other fuels — and about a fifth came from fossil gas. (Around 23% of gas goes to generating electricity, roughly half of which is lost in generation; the majority of oil and gas is burned directly in homes, vehicles and industry.)

## Why vehicles dominate

Petrol and diesel cars make up about **98%** of the vehicle fleet, and light vehicles account for around **70%** of petrol and diesel use. Counting cars as part of household energy, they're about **60% of a household's total energy** and its largest source of emissions. Crucially, when we burn petrol or diesel, around **three-quarters of the energy is lost** as waste heat, noise and vibration — providing no useful service. Electricity doesn't have this problem: when we use it, virtually all the available energy is made useful. This is the heart of Rewiring's "use more electricity, use less energy overall" argument, and why transport is the single biggest lever.
