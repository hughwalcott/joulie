# Electrification FAQ — Gas and hydrogen

## Do we need gas as a transition fuel?

Some industries may need gas for a while as they transition, and NZ uses gas for some electricity generation, but Rewiring's position is that gas is not clean and not renewable. It points to a peer-reviewed study finding gas can be worse than coal for the climate because of methane leakage (methane being ~80× more potent than CO₂ for warming over a short period). Rewiring argues gas shouldn't be used in homes — it's more expensive, running out, and burning it is bad for health — and that new houses shouldn't be built with gas connections, which lock families into decades of expensive, unhealthy fuel.

## What about biogas or "renewable" gas?

Creating biogas from waste streams is an interesting example of a circular economy, but Rewiring argues so-called renewable gas is even more expensive than fossil gas and there simply isn't enough of it to make a meaningful difference.

## What about hydrogen?

Think of hydrogen as a (poor) battery rather than an energy source. Making hydrogen from electricity and converting it back returns only about a third of the energy, so it's inefficient and more expensive for customers than using electricity directly or storing it in a battery. There may be a role in heavy transport, steel and cement making where the advantages outweigh the inefficiencies, but Rewiring argues hydrogen should not play a role in light-vehicle transport — NZ already has a nationwide electricity grid and growing home solar, and no hydrogen refuelling network.
