# Electrification FAQ — Batteries

## How will home batteries make a difference to the energy system?

Individually small, batteries add up. Just **120,000 homes** (about 5% of NZ households) with a medium-sized battery could reduce peak load by as much as NZ's largest hydro station, Manapouri — not in total energy, but in the power they could deliver for an hour or two when the system needs it most. Every home with a battery effectively removes itself from peak, and potentially its neighbours too.

## How much do batteries cost?

A **5 kWh** battery is about **$6,000–7,500**; a **15 kWh** battery about **$14,000–18,000**.

## How long do batteries last?

Most lithium-ion batteries are warrantied for around ten years, and some now for 15. Actual life depends on conditions, storage and use. Smart energy management that controls charging/discharging can improve efficiency and prolong life.

## Are home batteries safe?

Yes. NZ has strict standards covering battery location and installation; if followed, the risk of fire is extremely low.

## Is it worth getting a battery now?

It depends on your motivation. It's a firm "yes" if you want to reduce emissions, improve resilience and create an all-electric home. If your main concern is return on investment, batteries aren't yet the clear economic call that solar is — but the economics are improving fast and prices keep dropping. A battery can make financial sense on a time-of-use plan, or on a plan that rewards exporting at peak (e.g. Octopus, Flick). Rewiring advocates for **Symmetrical Export Tariffs** to make batteries more "bankable."

## Can batteries be recycled?

Yes — around **95%** of a lithium battery can potentially be recycled. Rewiring's "Closing the Loop" explainer argues the transition is a chance to build a circular economy; by 2050, more than half the demand for materials like cobalt, graphite and lithium could be met by recycled secondary supply (possibly earlier).

## What about going off-grid?

Going fully off-grid needs a very large solar-and-battery system and can face town-planning hurdles. Rewiring is **not** advocating going off-grid — there are big benefits to staying grid-connected — but solar plus a battery gives more resilience, control and independence while remaining connected.

## Old EV batteries

When no longer fit for driving, EV batteries still work well for stationary storage (a crucial part of the renewables transition); NZ has used "second-life" batteries at fast-charging stations. If they can't be reused, EV lithium-ion batteries are highly recyclable — recycling can already recover >90% of lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese, with best-in-class rates >99.6% for cobalt and nickel and >95% for lithium.
