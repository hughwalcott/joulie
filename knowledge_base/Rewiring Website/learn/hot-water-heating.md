# Electrification FAQ — Hot water heating

Water heating is around 30% of an average home's electricity use (more with more people), so it's one of the highest-value things to get right.

## How do I heat water cheaply?

Install a timer or wifi control so you can choose when to heat. With solar panels, the best time to heat water is during the day, when solar production peaks and a heat pump runs most efficiently.

## How long does a hot water heat pump last?

About 10–12 years.

## Do hot water heat pumps work in cold climates?

Yes. Heat pumps work more efficiently in warmer climates but still work effectively in the cold — they heat roughly half the homes in Norway, Finland and Sweden, which get much colder than NZ. Rewiring's advice: don't buy one of the cheaper versions.

## I have an electric resistive water heater — should I switch to a heat pump?

If you can power the resistive heater from a large solar array during the day, it may make economic sense to keep it. It still uses 3–4× more energy than a heat pump (which is why Rewiring recommends heat pumps), but if budget is tight, adding a timer and drawing on solar is worthwhile. Over 15 years, electric-resistance with solar and grid works out around the same cost as a hot water heat pump on the grid, including upfront costs — so a resistive cylinder plus solar can act as a "thermal battery."
