# Electrification FAQ — Geoengineering and carbon capture

## What about carbon capture?

Rewiring argues it's unrealistic to expect enough carbon sequestration to hit climate targets in time, which underlines the need to electrify and decarbonise faster. When a hydrocarbon is burned it becomes about three times heavier as a molecule (adding oxygen) and roughly 5,000 times larger in volume as a gas — the world turns ~10 billion tonnes of fossil fuel a year into ~30 billion tonnes of CO₂. Capturing, compressing, transporting and burying that CO₂ takes a great deal of energy and expensive equipment. Rewiring's conclusion: sequestration can only make fossil fuels more expensive and less competitive with cheaper renewables, and we can't do enough of it to sustain even a fraction of the existing fossil-fuel industry — electrification is cheaper anyway and roughly halves the energy needed.

## Can geoengineering save us?

Rewiring notes we're "already geoengineering" by burning fossil fuels. It argues geoengineering (e.g. space mirrors, reflective particles, artificial clouds) is not a decarbonisation strategy but an attempt to manage the sun's incoming energy while giving up on cutting CO₂ — with many unintended environmental, social and political consequences, and a dependency on continuing it forever ("like using liposuction as the solution to obesity when you're still eating cheeseburgers"). Studying it can improve understanding of Earth systems, but it isn't a realistic permanent solution and risks drawing resources away from technologies already known to work.
