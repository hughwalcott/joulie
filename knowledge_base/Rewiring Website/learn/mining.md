# Electrification FAQ — Mining, waste and materials

## What about all the mining needed for batteries and solar?

Any mining should be done as ethically and responsibly as possible. Rewiring's argument is one of scale: fossil fuels are "single-use mining," and running a machine on fossil fuel consumes far more material over time than running the same machine on electricity — "like a backpack vs a plastic bag." Past issues (e.g. cobalt mining) have improved as the industry matured, with more transparent supply chains and alternatives like lithium iron phosphate (LFP) batteries; Australia is a leader in ethically produced critical minerals. Crucially, more than **90%** of an EV battery's components can be recycled, and rates keep improving. Even including battery production, Rewiring cites the environmental impact of EVs as tiny compared with fuel-powered cars.

## What about all the solar panels and old batteries?

Material recovery rates for solar panels are already well over 90% (local firm Phoeniix Metalman recovers ~98%). Even with a massive global rollout, plausible solar waste to 2050 is roughly equal to the coal ash produced globally each month. For batteries, ~95% of a lithium battery can potentially be recycled, and by 2050 more than half the demand for materials like cobalt, graphite and lithium could be met by recycled secondary supply. Rewiring's "Closing the Loop" explainer argues the transition is a chance to build a circular, rather than linear, economy — supported by strong recycling regulation and product-stewardship rules. The broader point from "Electricity means Efficiency": electrifying everything massively reduces the total material and energy the global energy system needs.
