Evaluation · 5 September 2026

A bigger model fixes most of it — but not the newest one

All 107 questions in Joulie Test Question Suite 1 were run through the live RAG + LLM pipeline three times: on the configured 3B, and on two 14B models that fit the same Mac mini. Nothing else changed — same corpus, same prompt, same retrieval. qwen2.5:14b is the recommendation at 58%. The newer qwen3:14b scores lower, runs 2.5× slower, and declines to answer more than a quarter of the bank.

On qwen2.5:14b outright failures halve, from 52 to 25 — 37 answers improved, 4 regressed, 66 held. The cost is latency: 1.8 to 6.9 seconds median, worst case 10.2, still inside conversational range for a kiosk. qwen3:14b is the same size and a newer generation, and is worse on every axis that matters here.

Where the gain lands

The bank splits naturally: topics 1–5 are policy, cost and programme facts; topics 6–12 are how the technology works. Joulie's corpus — EECA, the Electricity Authority, MBIE, the Commerce Commission and Rewiring Aotearoa — covers the second group well and the first only in part, and every model inherits that shape.

qwen2.5:14b leads on nine of twelve topics and on both material types. The technology half is close to solved at 86% — induction and smart meters perfect, solar 94%. The policy half stays weak at 44%, which is where the corpus gaps live rather than the model.

The behaviour change matters more than the score

The headline number understates what actually changed. The 3B's characteristic failure was inventing a confident answer and crediting it to a regulator. The 14B's characteristic failure is declining to answer, or answering vaguely and pointing at the right source. For a trusted community advisor those are not remotely the same failure.

01

It now says when it doesn't know

qwen2.5:14b explicitly states the material isn't in the corpus six times — "The GIDI Fund isn't mentioned in the provided references", "Electrify NZ isn't a specific programme or organisation mentioned in my reference materials" — and redirects to NZTA, MBIE or Tenancy Services sixteen times more. The 3B managed three in total, and neither of its two hedges was a refusal; both were caveats buried inside an otherwise invented answer. qwen3:14b goes much further and declines on 29 questions, which is where its lower score comes from.

explicit refusals — 3B: 2 · qwen2.5:14b: 6 · qwen3:14b: 29  |  defers to a named source — 1 / 16 / 18
02

The kiosk formatting rules are now obeyed

The system prompt forbids bullets and asterisks, caps answers at four sentences, and forbids speaking URLs — all of which matter because the output is spoken aloud. The 3B broke those rules in roughly a quarter of answers. qwen2.5:14b breaks them once. qwen3:14b relapses almost to 3B levels, emitting markdown bullets or bold in 16 answers and reading out a URL.

bullets — 3B: 18 · qwen2.5:14b: 1 · qwen3:14b: 16  |  over four sentences 7 / 1 / 3  |  spoken URLs 1 / 0 / 1
03

Attribution is far less promiscuous

The 3B invoked the Electricity Authority in 26 answers, frequently as the source of something it had made up, and twice glossed EECA as "(Electricity Authority)". qwen2.5:14b names the Authority in 8, correctly credits MBIE for the renewable-generation figure, and labels Rewiring as advocacy in 28. qwen3:14b leans on Rewiring in 47 of 107 answers — nearly half — which is a concern given the project's rule that an advocacy charity must never be presented as neutral fact. All three under-cite years, and both 14Bs are worse at it than the 3B, so the prompt's currency requirement is being followed less often, not more.

names Electricity Authority — 26 / 8 / 25  |  attributes Rewiring — 25 / 28 / 47  |  cites a year — 31 / 19 / 17
04

Consistency under rephrasing nearly doubles

Nine of ten reworded questions get a consistent answer from qwen2.5:14b, against five of ten from the 3B. The 3B contradicted itself on gas connections, invented a "Gas Supply Certificate" regime, and failed to name a single generator when asked a second time; qwen2.5 holds all of those. qwen3:14b is close behind at eight, but contradicts itself within the main run — it says EVs pay road user charges, then two questions later says they are exempt.

Why the newer model loses

qwen3:14b is a generation newer than qwen2.5:14b and the same size on disk. It is nonetheless the wrong choice here, for three reasons that only show up when you measure against this bank on this hardware.

01

It declines too readily to be useful

Refusing beats fabricating, but 29 declines out of 107 is a quarter of the bank returned as "I don't have that information" — including questions the corpus does answer. It declines on solar panel types, on battery chemistry and on the 2030 climate target; qwen2.5:14b answers the first two correctly, and the 3B answered the third correctly. A visitor at an expo who gets a shrug to one question in four will stop asking.

02

It is too slow for a conversational kiosk

17.0 seconds median and 42.5 seconds worst case, against 6.9 and 10.2 for qwen2.5:14b. That is before speech synthesis. A visitor standing at a handset will assume it has broken.

03

It reverts to markdown the kiosk cannot speak

16 answers with bullets or bold, against one from qwen2.5:14b, plus a spoken URL. This is the 3B's problem returning, and it matters more here than in a text interface because the output goes to text-to-speech.

The 30B model does not fit this machine. qwen3:30b-a3b was downloaded and attempted, and Metal failed it with kIOGPUCommandBufferCallbackErrorOutOfMemory. At 18.6GB it exceeds the default GPU wired limit on a 24GB Mac mini once the retrieval stack is also resident — and even with that limit raised it could not be deployed, because Joulie's production kiosk must simultaneously hold XTTS v2 (1.7GB on disk, more in memory), faster-whisper, ChromaDB, the embedding model and a Chrome kiosk window. Realistically that leaves 13–15GB for the language model. 9GB is the size that fits with room to speak.

What a bigger model cannot fix

Two limits are structural. Neither was removed by a bigger model; both were confirmed by all three runs.

Seven questions are unanswerable from this corpus. Their expected figures — 109 TJ/day, 100 PJ, 53.5%, 150,000 km, 51–55%, 56%, $230m, 18–22% — appear nowhere in the knowledge base. No model at any size can produce them. Both 14Bs mostly handle these correctly now by declining; the 3B invented numbers instead.

The topical gaps are larger than the numeric ones. NZTA, Beehive and the Ministry of Transport, the Climate Change Commission, MfE and Tenancy Services are absent from the corpus entirely. That is why topics 3 and 5 remain the weakest at every model size, and why they improve least: the model stops fabricating, but there is still nothing for it to retrieve.

There is also a corpus conflict worth resolving. Asked how long a hot water heat pump lasts, all three models answer "10–12 years" — which is genuinely in the corpus, from Rewiring Aotearoa. EECA's 15-year working life, which the bank treats as the right answer, is not in the corpus at all. The 3B misattributed the figure to EECA; both 14Bs correctly credit Rewiring. The model behaved properly and still scored badly, because the corpus only holds one side.

What to do about it

Revised in light of the comparison. The first item has changed: it is now a configuration change, not a research question.

  1. Switch the default model to qwen2.5:14b

    A one-line change to config.py buys 20 points, halves outright failures, near-doubles consistency, and brings the spoken-output formatting into line — with no prompt, corpus or retrieval changes. It fits the 24GB Mac mini at 9.0GB with room for the speech stack. Budget for the latency: 1.8s to 6.9s median, which changes the feel of the kiosk and may want a "thinking" cue in the UI. Do not take qwen3:14b on the assumption that newer is better — it scores 6 points lower, runs 2.5× slower, and breaks the spoken-output rules 16 times.

  2. Fill the four corpus gaps

    NZTA (RUC rates, charger counts, EVRoam), Beehive and Ministry of Transport releases, the Climate Change Commission and MfE emissions plans, and Tenancy Services' healthy homes standards. This is now the top accuracy lever: the 14B has stopped inventing on these topics, so the only way its answers improve is if the material exists to retrieve. Topics 3 and 5 are 37% and 30%.

  3. Still add an explicit scope statement and refusal path

    Both 14Bs decline more readily than the 3B, but they are doing it on instinct rather than instruction, and the two land in different places — qwen2.5 under-declines on 25 wrong answers, qwen3 over-declines on 29 answerable ones. The distance threshold remains inert in all three runs, rejecting none of the 428 retrieved chunks. Naming the corpus's scope in the system prompt is what makes the behaviour reliable rather than a property of whichever model is loaded.

  4. Restore the year-citation habit

    The prompt requires a year whenever a statistic is quoted. Both 14Bs do this less than the 3B — 19 and 17 answers against 31 — a genuine regression on the bank's currency criterion that the model upgrade makes worse, not better. Worth an explicit instruction, since energy figures date quickly and the kiosk speaks them as fact.

  5. Constrain attribution to the retrieved set

    Much improved but not solved: qwen2.5:14b still credits the gas connection charge to the Electricity Authority when it comes from EECA, and qwen3 leans on Rewiring in nearly half its answers. A post-check comparing named publishers against retrieved chunk metadata would catch the remainder.

  6. Fix the contradiction inside the test bank

    Topic 3 Q4 gives the light PHEV road user charge as "$38 per 10,000 kilometres"; Q15 gives "$38/1,000 km" for the same vehicle class. Resolve it before the bank is used as ground truth again.

Method. Each question went to a fresh Agent with no conversation history, against the live Retriever over the existing 1,006-chunk store — the same path the kiosk uses. Only the model identifier differed across the three runs; qwen3 emitted no reasoning blocks, so no thinking-mode switch was applied. Grading is manual against the bank's expected answers: pass where the substance is present, partial where the direction is right but a key figure or element is wrong or missing, fail where the answer contradicts the expected answer or its substance is fabricated. All three runs were graded against the same rubric, with the 3B verdict visible while grading the others to keep the standard fixed.

Caveats. The bank is dated 25 August 2026 and flags 18 answers for re-verification; it is treated here as ground truth as written. One run per model captures no sampling variance. A 20-point gap is well clear of run-to-run noise; the 6-point gap between the two 14Bs is narrower, though it points the same way as the latency, formatting and decline-rate measurements, which are not close. A correctly-declined answer is still graded fail on accuracy — it does not match the expected answer — so both 14B scores understate how much their harm profile improved.
